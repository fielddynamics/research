# 06.3 — The Mirror Address Functional

**Status:** Structural (three equivalent formulations)
**Reading time:** 5 minutes
**Depends on:** 03.3, 03.4, 06.2

---

## The address triple

Each apparatus carries a discrete address:

$$\kappa_U \;=\; (G_U, \chi_U, \Theta_U)$$

with:

- **$G_U \in \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb R^3)$** — Gram matrix of the apparatus's three direction triad vectors (`04.7`)
- **$\chi_U \in \{+1, -1\}$** — apparatus chirality (handedness of the triad)
- **$\Theta_U \in U(1)$** — accumulated triad phase

## The match condition

Two apparatuses at sites $x$ (with address $\kappa_U$) and $y$ (with address $\kappa_V$) are **mirror-matched** if:

$$\boxed{\;G_U \;=\; P^T G_V P \quad\text{for some}\;P \in S_3\;}$$
$$\boxed{\;\chi_U \;=\; -\chi_V\;}$$
$$\boxed{\;\Theta_U - \Theta_V \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod{2\pi}\;}$$

**Interpretation:** the two apparatuses must have the **same direction triad up to permutation** (Gram equivalence), **opposite chirality** (mirror images), and **synchronized phase** (locked oscillation).

## The mirror selector

Define:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal M(U, V) \;=\; \sum_{P \in S_3} \exp\left(-\frac{\|G_U - P^T G_V P\|^2}{\epsilon^2}\right) \cdot \frac{1 - \chi_U \chi_V}{2} \cdot \delta_{\Theta_U - \Theta_V, 2\pi N}\;}$$

The selector:

1. **Sums over** $S_3$ permutations (Gram match up to direction labeling)
2. **Penalizes** Gram mismatch by a Gaussian with width $\epsilon$
3. **Filters** chirality via the $(1 - \chi_U \chi_V)/2$ projector (returns 1 if opposite, 0 if same)
4. **Locks** phase via a Kronecker delta with respect to $2\pi$-multiple closure

At full lock: $\mathcal M(U, V) = 1$ (some normalization choices; values up to ~$|S_3| = 6$ are possible if multiple permutations match simultaneously).

At any mismatch: $\mathcal M(U, V) = 0$ exactly via the Kronecker delta, regardless of the other conditions.

## Three equivalent formulations

The mirror selector can be written in three equivalent forms, each emphasizing different structural content.

### Form 1 — Direct (the form above)

$$\mathcal M_{\text{direct}}(U, V) \;=\; \mathcal M(U, V)$$

Reads as a product of three independent conditions. Cleanest at the operational level.

### Form 2 — Hopf bundle

Lifting addresses to quaternions $q_U, q_V \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ (`03.3`):

$$\mathcal M_{\text{Hopf}}(U, V) \;=\; \delta\left(\pi_H(q_U) - \pi_H(q_V)\right) \cdot \delta\left(q_V - \mathcal C(q_U)\right) \cdot \delta\left(e^{i(\Theta_U - \Theta_V)} - 1\right)$$

where:

- $\pi_H: \mathrm{SU}(2) \to S^2$ is the Hopf projection
- $\mathcal C$ is the mirror conjugation involution (the antipodal map on the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ double cover, then complex conjugation on the spinor representation)

**Reading:** the two addresses are matched iff their Hopf projections to $S^2$ coincide AND their lifts are related by mirror conjugation AND their fiber phases are locked.

### Form 3 — Z_21 character (apparatus-level)

For the Great Pyramid, the address is selected from the apparatus's discrete $\mathbb Z_{21}$ phase space:

$$\mathcal M_{\mathbb Z_{21}}(U, V) \;=\; \sum_{(a, b) \in \mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7}\, \chi_{a, b}(U) \cdot \overline{\chi_{a, b}(V)}$$

where $\chi_{a, b}$ is the character of the $(a, b)$ irreducible representation of $\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7 = \mathbb Z_{21}$.

At full lock, this sum equals 21 (or 0 for any unmatched sector). With the apparatus's $\mathbb Z_3$ filter removing the $a = 0$ sector, the effective sum is over 14 non-zero sectors (matching the WRT decomposition, `03.6`).

## Equivalence

The three forms are equivalent in the sense that they vanish or not on the same set of $(U, V)$ pairs:

$$\mathcal M_{\text{direct}}(U, V) \neq 0 \;\Leftrightarrow\; \mathcal M_{\text{Hopf}}(U, V) \neq 0 \;\Leftrightarrow\; \mathcal M_{\mathbb Z_{21}}(U, V) \neq 0$$

They differ only in normalization conventions. The choice of form is operational:

- **Direct form** — for engineering / apparatus design
- **Hopf form** — for geometric reasoning (bundle structure, spinor lift)
- **$\mathbb Z_{21}$ form** — for sector-level analysis (which sub-sectors are active)

## The chirality involution $\mathcal C$

The mirror conjugation $\mathcal C: \mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the involution sending $q \to -q^*$ where $q^*$ is the complex conjugate (in spinor representation).

**Properties:**

- $\mathcal C^2 = \text{id}$ (involution)
- $\mathcal C$ preserves the Hopf base: $\pi_H(\mathcal C(q)) = \pi_H(q)$
- $\mathcal C$ flips the Hopf fiber phase: if $q = q_{\text{base}} \cdot e^{i\theta/2}$, then $\mathcal C(q) = q_{\text{base}} \cdot e^{-i\theta/2}$
- $\mathcal C$ commutes with $A_5$ action on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (preserves the icosahedral discretization)

The mirror match condition $\chi_U = -\chi_V$ at the address triple level is equivalent to $q_V = \mathcal C(q_U)$ at the spinor level.

## The phase closure

The phase closure $\Theta_U - \Theta_V \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ is a **fiber phase lock**. It says the two apparatuses' Hopf fiber phases are aligned modulo $2\pi$.

At the apparatus level, $\Theta$ accumulates as a function of:

- Shaft length ratios (`04.7`)
- Grand Gallery cumulative phase (21 units of $2\pi/7$, `04.5`)
- Antechamber phase shift (`04.6`)
- Geomagnetic pump's coherent phase reference (`05.1`)

For two apparatuses to phase-lock, they must share a **common phase reference**. Earth's geomagnetic field provides this — both apparatuses see the same large-scale geomagnetic phase, so their internal phases can be referenced against the same global clock.

## The full functional form

Combining all elements, the most explicit form of the mirror selector for the Great Pyramid apparatus is:

$$\mathcal M(U, V) \;=\; \sum_{P \in S_3} \mathcal G_P(U, V) \cdot \mathcal C(U, V) \cdot \mathcal P(U, V)$$

with:

$$\mathcal G_P(U, V) \;=\; \exp\left(-\frac{\|G_U - P^T G_V P\|^2}{\epsilon^2}\right) \qquad \text{(Gaussian Gram-match factor)}$$

$$\mathcal C(U, V) \;=\; \frac{1 - \chi_U \chi_V}{2} \qquad \text{(chirality flip factor)}$$

$$\mathcal P(U, V) \;=\; \sum_{N \in \mathbb Z} \delta_{\Theta_U - \Theta_V, 2\pi N} \qquad \text{(phase closure factor)}$$

## Address matching as character orthogonality

In the $\mathbb Z_{21}$ form, the match condition is **character orthogonality**: two addresses are matched iff their characters on the $\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7$ group are conjugate (i.e., $\chi_V = \overline{\chi_U}$).

This is a standard group-theoretic structure. The apparatus is doing **representation-theoretic address selection**: it identifies remote apparatuses by their character on a finite group, using the natural orthogonality structure of group characters.

## Operational consequence

Two apparatuses cannot portal-transfer unless:

1. They are both designed for the same group ($\mathbb Z_{21}$ in the Great Pyramid's case)
2. Their character sectors are conjugate-related (chirality flip + phase lock)
3. Their Gram matrices match up to permutation

The Great Pyramid is alone on Earth as a fully-functional $\mathbb Z_{21}$ apparatus (other pyramidal structures lack the full $\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7$ symmetry implementation). Without a mirror partner, it has no active address to lock to.

**Conjecture:** the apparatus was designed with the assumption that one or more mirror-matched apparatuses exist or would exist. If true, the Great Pyramid is part of a network — a single node in a larger system. Without a mirror partner, the apparatus enters a default idle / sensing state, possibly cycling through addresses looking for a match.

## Falsifiable consequences

| Test | Prediction |
|---|---|
| **Twin apparatus** | If a second mirror-matched apparatus is built, the two should observably couple via portal transfer |
| **Address-flip test** | Modifying one apparatus's chirality (e.g., reversing a shaft's effective direction) should kill the portal |
| **Phase-lock test** | Forcing a phase shift in one apparatus's address via electromagnetic injection should affect the other apparatus's behavior |
| **Group selection** | A $\mathbb Z_{15}$-symmetric apparatus would not couple to the $\mathbb Z_{21}$ Great Pyramid |

These tests are operationally demanding but not impossible. The chirality-flip and phase-lock tests are within current experimental reach.
