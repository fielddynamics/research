## The K=5 directed complete graph

The K=5 ideal interaction graph is the directed complete graph on five vertices:

$$K_5^{\rightarrow}$$

It has:

$$5 \cdot (5 - 1) \;=\; 20$$

directed edges (each undirected edge counted both ways).

This graph is the 1-skeleton of the **pentachoron** (4-simplex), the regular polytope with 5 vertices in $\mathbb R^4$.

## The integer identity behind the gravity-Coulomb gap

The framework's exponent at K=4:

$$\Phi_3(K) - 1 \;=\; K^2 + K \;=\; K(K+1)$$

At K=4:

$$\Phi_3(4) - 1 \;=\; 21 - 1 \;=\; 20$$

Also:

$$20 \;=\; 2\binom{5}{2}$$

This is the ordered-edge count of the 5-vertex simplex (the pentachoron) — exactly $K_5^{\rightarrow}$.

## The edge product

Let every directed edge of $K_5^{\rightarrow}$ carry a Coulomb phase amplitude:

$$A_{ij} \;=\; \alpha\, e^{i\theta_{ij}}, \qquad i \neq j$$

The full directed-edge product:

$$\mathcal A_{20} \;=\; \prod_{i \neq j}^{5} A_{ij} \;=\; \alpha^{20} \exp\left(i \sum_{i \neq j} \theta_{ij}\right)$$

## Phase closure

The edge network closes when:

$$\boxed{\;\sum_{i \neq j} \theta_{ij} \;=\; 0 \pmod{2\pi}\;}$$

At closure:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal A_{20} \;=\; \alpha^{20}\;}$$

Attach the primitive Coulomb edge:

$$\alpha \cdot \mathcal A_{20} \;=\; \alpha^{21}$$

This is the **raw attenuation core** of the portal coupling.

## The "1 + 20" factorization of $\alpha^{21}$

The framework's $\alpha^{21}$ has two independent algebraic decompositions:

**Decomposition 1: Three-sweep spectral kernel.**

$$\alpha^{21} \;=\; \alpha^{K+D-1} \cdot \alpha^{K+D-1} \cdot \alpha^{K+D-1} \;=\; \alpha^7 \cdot \alpha^7 \cdot \alpha^7$$

with three sweeps of seven levels each. See `01.3`.

**Decomposition 2: Pentachoron edge product.**

$$\alpha^{21} \;=\; \alpha \cdot \alpha^{20} \;=\; \underbrace{\alpha^1}_{\text{primitive Coulomb edge}} \cdot \underbrace{\alpha^{20}}_{\text{pentachoron edge product}}$$

Both readings give the same integer because both come from $\Phi_3(K) = K^2 + K + 1 = 1 + K(K+1)$ at K=4.

The apparatus's geometry encodes the algebraic factorization directly.

## Connection to the icosahedral structure

The boundary of the pentachoron is a tessellation of $S^3$ by 5 tetrahedral cells. The orientation-preserving symmetry group of these cells is:

$$A_5$$

(alternating group on 5 elements, order 60).

$A_5$ is isomorphic to the rotational symmetry group of the regular icosahedron. So the K=5 pentachoron carries an **icosahedral discretization** of its boundary $S^3$ under the symmetry group action.

This is the origin of the **Poincaré homology sphere** $\Sigma(2, 3, 5) = S^3 / 2A_5$ structure in the apparatus's address space (see `03.4`).

## Edge phase distribution

In the ideal pentachoron with equilateral edges, all 20 directed edges would carry identical amplitudes. The phase closure $\sum_{i \neq j} \theta_{ij} = 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ is trivially satisfied with all $\theta_{ij} = 0$.

In a *distorted* pentachoron (e.g., the 5 relieving chambers with their non-equilateral spacing), the edge phases $\theta_{ij}$ are distributed according to inter-chamber geometry. The phase closure becomes a non-trivial **geometric constraint** on the chamber spacings.

Specifically: if the 5 chambers are at heights $h_1, \dots, h_5$ above the King's Chamber roof, the directed-edge phase $\theta_{ij}$ encodes a phase difference proportional to $|h_i - h_j|$ (with sign). The phase closure condition demands:

$$\sum_{i \neq j} k(h_i - h_j) \;=\; 0$$

for some wavenumber $k$. By antisymmetry this is automatically zero. So the closure condition is structurally automatic for *any* chamber height distribution — provided each pair $(i,j)$ contributes a phase $\theta_{ij} = k(h_i - h_j)$ that antisymmetrizes under $i \leftrightarrow j$.

This is consistent with the relieving chambers' physical role: they realize the pentachoron edge product *automatically* by their inter-spacing, with closure guaranteed by antisymmetry.

## The 21st edge

The phase-closed pentachoron edge product gives $\alpha^{20}$. The 21st edge — the "primitive Coulomb edge" — is the **chamber-to-coffer volumetric step**. This is not a 20th element of the same graph; it is the **input edge** that feeds the pentachoron network.

The architecture is:

$$\text{Input}\;\xrightarrow{\alpha^1}\;\text{King's Chamber}\;\xrightarrow{K_5^{\rightarrow}\text{ traversal}}\;\text{output}$$

The coffer provides the input load (the primitive $\alpha$). The relieving chambers provide the edge product ($\alpha^{20}$). Their composition gives $\alpha^{21}$, which is the framework's Coulomb attenuation.
