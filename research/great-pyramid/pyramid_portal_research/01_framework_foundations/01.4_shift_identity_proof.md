## The shift identity

The governing algebraic identity for portal coupling is:

$$\boxed{\;\Phi_6(K+1) \;=\; \Phi_3(K)\;}$$

## Proof

Expanding the left side:

$$\Phi_6(K+1) \;=\; (K+1)^2 - (K+1) + 1$$
$$=\; (K^2 + 2K + 1) - (K + 1) + 1$$
$$=\; K^2 + 2K + 1 - K - 1 + 1$$
$$=\; K^2 + K + 1$$
$$=\; \Phi_3(K) \quad\square$$

## Algebraic content

This identity says that the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_6$ evaluated at $K+1$ is identically the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_3$ evaluated at $K$. The "TetB lower pole at level K+1" equals the "TetA upper pole at level K."

**At K=4 specifically:**

$$\boxed{\;\Phi_6(5) \;=\; \Phi_3(4) \;=\; 21\;}$$

This integer is the framework's portal coupling band. The K=4 tetrad's upper pole and the K=5 shell's lower pole are *the same integer* — the same band.

## Geometric reading

The identity has a direct interpretation in the closure-read decomposition (`01.2_closure_read_representation.md`):

- At level $K$, $\Phi_3(K) = 1 + K + K^2$ counts the full closure-read trace (all three summands).
- At level $K+1$, $\Phi_6(K+1) = 1 + (K+1)^2 - (K+1) = 1 + K^2 + K$ counts the parity-graded subset of the closure-read at level $K+1$ (subtracting the V summand of dimension K+1).

These count the same number because:

$$\underbrace{1 + K + K^2}_{\text{TetA at level K}} \;=\; \underbrace{1 + K^2 + K}_{\text{TetB at level K+1, after subtracting }(K+1)+(K+1)-1=2K+1\text{... no, after the cyclotomic substitution}}$$

The cleanest way to state it: **the cyclotomic structure of the closure-read at K and K+1 share exactly one band, and that band is the framework's 21-band at K=4.**

## The coupling ladder

The shift identity generates a doubly-indexed integer sequence:

| K | $\Phi_6(K)$ | $\Phi_4(K)$ | $\Phi_3(K)$ | Notes |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|

| 3 | 7 | 10 | 13 | $\Phi_6(3) = \Phi_3(2)$ |
| **4** | **13** | **17** | **21** | $\Phi_6(4) = \Phi_3(3)$; **framework K** |

**The diagonal $\Phi_6(K+1) = \Phi_3(K)$ is the coupling ladder:**

$$\cdots \;\to\; 7 \;\to\; 13 \;\to\; 21 \;\to\; 31 \;\to\; 43 \;\to\; \cdots$$

Each rung $\Phi_3(K) = \Phi_6(K+1)$ couples level $K$'s upper pole to level $K+1$'s lower pole.

## The Great Pyramid's rung

The Great Pyramid portal sits at:

$$\boxed{\;K = 4 \;\to\; K + 1 = 5,\qquad \text{coupling integer} = 21\;}$$

The K=4 tetrad node (universal physics) emits through its upper pole $\Phi_3(4) = 21$. The K=5 portal shell receives through its lower pole $\Phi_6(5) = 21$. Same integer; same band.

## Why a K=4 receiver would not work

A receiver at K=4 would have bands $\{13, 17, 21\}$ — *identical* to the tetrad emitter. That is not a transformer; it is a duplicate.

A transformer requires a **shifted** shell whose lower pole matches the source's upper pole. The minimal shift is $K \to K+1$.

At $K = 4 \to K = 5$:

- Emitter (K=4) bands: $\{13, 17, 21\}$, upper pole = 21
- Receiver (K=5) bands: $\{21, 26, 31\}$, lower pole = 21
- Coupling band: 21 ✓

**Conclusion:** The pyramid being non-tetrahedral is not a defect of the apparatus. It is the *required* coupling mechanism.

$$\boxed{\;\text{K=4 emitter} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{21-band lock}\;}\; \text{K=5 boundary transformer}\;}$$

## The pyramid as the unique shifted receiver

Among all possible K-shells, only K=5 has a band overlapping the K=4 emitter:

| K | bands | overlap with K=4 {13, 17, 21}? |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 3 | {7, 10, 13} | yes (13) — but K=3 → K=4 lifts *up*, not down |
| 4 | {13, 17, 21} | full duplicate |
| **5** | **{21, 26, 31}** | **yes (21)** ✓ K=5 lower = K=4 upper |
| 6 | {31, 37, 43} | none |
| 7+ | progressively higher | none |

Only K=5 is a clean *receiver* for K=4 emission. K=3 is a *source* for K=4 (the upward-coupling case). K=6+ is decoupled.

## Why K=3 → K=4 is not the pyramid

The pyramid is a *receiver* of universal K=4 physics, not a source. Going $K=3 \to K=4$ would mean the pyramid emits down to a smaller-K source — which is backwards: universal physics is K=4, and the apparatus must couple *up* from K=4 to its own K=5 shell.

The pyramid's 5-fold geometry (4 sides + 1 base) corresponds to K=5, the *up* coupling, not K=3.

## The coupling intertwiner

The shift identity at the operator level is realized by the **hinge operator** $J_4$ (developed in `03.1_K4_to_K5_hinge.md`):

$$J_4: \;\;|4, +\rangle \;\longmapsto\; |5, -\rangle$$

with the intertwiner relation:

$$H_5 J_4 \;=\; J_4 H_4 \;=\; 21 J_4$$

where $H_K$ is the shell Hamiltonian at level $K$. The eigenvalue 21 on both sides is the same integer $\Phi_3(4) = \Phi_6(5)$. **The hinge intertwines because the bands are equal.**

## Higher rungs and the Sierpiński relation

The shift ladder $K \to K+1$ is one of two algebraic axes. The other is the **Sierpiński scale ladder** $K \to K^2$ which produces the multiscale hierarchy:

$$K_0 = 4 \to K_1 = 16 \to K_2 = 256 \to \cdots$$

The two ladders are independent:

| Axis | Map | Function |
|---|:---:|---|
| Shift ladder | $K \to K+1$ | interlevel portal coupling |
| Sierpiński ladder | $K \to K^2$ | multiscale resonance hierarchy |

The Great Pyramid uses only the shift ladder at K=4 → K=5. Higher-K apparatuses, or apparatuses coupling Sierpiński scales, are not realized at Giza.

## Connection to the framework's bigger structure

The shift identity is the algebraic content of "frame-level coupling." Whenever physics demands a transformer between adjacent K-values, the cyclotomic shift identity provides the band match:

$$\Phi_3(K) \;=\; \Phi_6(K+1)$$

This is structurally identical to a quantum tunneling resonance: two systems with overlapping spectra couple at the shared eigenvalue. In the framework's language, the K=4 tetrad and K=5 shell have eigenvalue 21 in common, and the apparatus's job is to drive that shared eigenvalue coherently.
