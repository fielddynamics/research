## 0. Headline

$$\boxed{\;C_{K+1} \;=\; A_K \cup_{C_K} B_K\;,\qquad |C_{K+1}| \;=\; |C_K| + 2K\;}$$

The coupling polynomial is not just a formula. **It is a recursive parity machine.** At each level $K$, two parity sheets TetA and TetB overlap on a shared core; each adds exactly $K$ exclusive elements; their union becomes the next level's shared core. Starting from $C_1 = 1$, the recursion generates the entire $K=1..K=5$ shell family — *Seed, Fano, Star-of-David, Tetrad, Receiver* — and the coupling polynomial $f(K, s) = K^2 + sK + 1$ is exactly the count of these set-theoretic objects.

The parity-pushout recursion is the most important new piece. It says the coupling polynomial is not derived numerically — it is generated by a set-theoretic growth rule.

## 2. The parity-pushout recursion

**Setup.** At each level $K$, fix three objects:

- $C_K$ — the shared core (set of elements)
- $A_K, B_K$ — two parity sheets, with $A_K \cap B_K = C_K$
- $|A_K \setminus C_K| = |B_K \setminus C_K| = K$ — each sheet adds exactly $K$ exclusive elements

**Sizes are forced.** From the requirements:

$$|A_K| = |B_K| = |C_K| + K, \qquad |A_K \cup B_K| = |C_K| + 2K$$

**Define the next core by pushout.**

$$C_{K+1} \;\equiv\; A_K \cup_{C_K} B_K$$

This gives the recursion $|C_{K+1}| = |C_K| + 2K$ with seed $|C_1| = 1$.

**Closed form.** $|C_K| = 1 + K(K-1) = K^2 - K + 1 = \Phi_6(K)$. The shared core at level $K$ is exactly $\Phi_6(K)$. Equivalently $f_-(K)$.

**Each sheet.** $|A_K| = |B_K| = K^2 + 1 = \Phi_4(K) = f_0(K)$.

**Full union.** $|A_K \cup B_K| = K^2 + K + 1 = \Phi_3(K) = f_+(K)$.

So the three values of $f(K, s)$ at $s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ are exactly the three cardinalities (intersection, sheet, union) of the parity-pushout configuration. The coupling polynomial $f(K, s) = K^2 + sK + 1$ is the **counting polynomial of the parity-pushout shells**.

## 3. The K=1..K=5 shell family

The recursion, run from $C_1 = 1$, produces five named shells:

|:-:|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | Seed | 1 | 2 | 3 | $3 = 1 + 1 + 1$ |
| 2 | Fano seed | 3 | 5 | 7 | $7 = 3 + 2 + 2$ |
| 3 | Star-of-David | 7 | 10 | 13 | $13 = 7 + 3 + 3$ |
| 4 | Tetrad | 13 | 17 | 21 | $21 = 13 + 4 + 4$ |
| 5 | Receiver | 21 | 26 | 31 | $31 = 21 + 5 + 5$ |

**The names are not decorative.** Each shell has structural identity:

- $K=2$ — **Fano seed**. $f_+(2) = 7 = \|\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_2)\|$, the Fano plane.
- $K=3$ — **Star-of-David**. The 13-dot Star realizes $f_+(3) = 13$, two interpenetrating triangles whose union is 13.
- $K=4$ — **Tetrad**. $f_-(4) = 13$ is the K=3 union; $f_+(4) = 21$ is the framework's 21-band; $f_0(4) = 17$ is the impedance numerator.
- $K=5$ — **Receiver**. $f_-(5) = 21$ catches the K=4 hinge; this is exactly the portal physics.

## 4. The adjacent-shell lock

Across consecutive levels, the union at $K$ becomes the core at $K+1$:

$$\boxed{\;f_+(K) \;=\; f_-(K+1)\;}$$

This is structurally the same as the shift identity $\Phi_3(K) = \Phi_6(K+1)$, but expressed in the parity-pushout language: *the K shell's full union is the K+1 shell's shared core*. The integer 13 is both $f_+(3)$ (Star-of-David union) and $f_-(4)$ (Tetrad core). The integer 21 is both $f_+(4)$ (Tetrad union, the framework's 21-band) and $f_-(5)$ (Receiver core, the pentachoron's lower band).

**In code:** `adjacent_shell_lock_check(K)` and `adjacent_shell_lock_scan(K_max)` verify the lock; aligned with `cyclotomic.shift_identity` (tested in `test_coupling_shells.py::TestAlignmentWithFramework`).

## 5. The five engineering-shell identities

Beyond the parity-pushout recursion itself, the engineering synthesis gives five sharp identities that connect the coupling polynomial to discrete geometric objects.

### 5.1 Discriminant identity (squared form)

$$\boxed{\;(f_+(K) - f_-(K))^2 \;=\; 4K^2\;}$$

The difference $f_+ - f_- = 2K$ is the linear form (T8 in AW 13). Squared: $4K^2$. At $K=4$: $(21 - 13)^2 = 8^2 = 64 = K^3$. **The squared range exactly matches the closure-tile dimension $A_3 \otimes A_3 = 64$.** Not coincidence: the 64-tile *is* the discriminant of the coupling polynomial squared.

**In code:** `discriminant_identity_squared(K)`. Aligned with `closure_core256.coupling_summary` 4-face total.

### 5.2 Directed simplex edge identity

$$\boxed{\;f_+(K) - 1 \;=\; 2\binom{K+1}{2} \;=\; K(K+1)\;}$$

At $K=4$: $f_+(4) - 1 = 20 = 4 \cdot 5$. This is the **pentachoron's directed edge count** ($K_5^{\rightarrow}$ has 20 ordered edges).
At $K=3$: $f_+(3) - 1 = 12$, the directed edge count of the K=4 complete graph (the tetrahedron viewed as a graph has 12 directed edges).
At $K=5$: $f_+(5) - 1 = 30$, the next simplex.

This is the algebraic source of the $\alpha^{20}$ factor in the gravity equation: 20 directed edges of the pentachoron $\Leftrightarrow$ 20 powers of $\alpha$ in the coupling.

**In code:** `directed_simplex_edge_identity(K)`. Aligned with `gravity.pentachoron_directed_edge_count`.

### 5.3 Projective plane at prime powers

$$\boxed{\;f_+(q) \;=\; q^2 + q + 1 \;=\; |\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)|\;\text{ at prime power }q\;}$$

Each prime power $q$ gives a finite projective plane. At $q = 2$: 7 points (Fano plane). At $q = 3$: 13. At $q = 4$: 21. At $q = 5$: 31. The framework's preferred value $K = 4$ is a prime power (since $4 = 2^2$), and the value 21 is the count of points in $\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)$ — a *concrete* finite geometry, not an abstract polynomial value.

Distinction from `closure_core256.projective_plane_q2_count(K) = K^4 + K^2 + 1 = |\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_{K^2})|$: that is the *3-sweep* identity at $K \to K^2$. The direct identity here is at $q$ itself, not $K^2$.

**In code:** `projective_plane_general(q)`, `projective_plane_identity_check(q)`, `is_prime_power(n)`.

### 5.4 Scale fusion (existing, restated)

$$\boxed{\;f_+(K) \cdot f_-(K) \;=\; f_+(K^2)\;}$$

This is the existing scale-fusion identity in `cyclotomic.py`. In the parity-pushout language: the product of TetA's "+" reading and TetB's "−" reading at level $K$ equals the "+" reading at level $K^2$. At $K=4$: $21 \cdot 13 = 273 = f_+(16)$.

### 5.5 The numbers fit together

| At $K = 4$ | Algebra | Source |
|---|:-:|---|
| Shared core | 13 | $\Phi_6(4)$, K=3 Star union |
| Each sheet | 17 | $\Phi_4(4)$, parity sheet size |
| Full union | 21 | $\Phi_3(4)$, K=4 Tetrad union |
| Range | 8 | $f_+ - f_- = 2K$ |
| Range squared | 64 | $4K^2$ = closure-tile size |
| Union minus 1 | 20 | $K(K+1)$ = pentachoron edges |
| Projective plane | 21 | $|\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)|$ at $q=4$ |
| Scale fusion | 273 | $21 \cdot 13 = f_+(16)$ |

Eight numbers, one polynomial, one parity-pushout recursion. No free parameters.

## 6. The parity-pushout category

The shells and their relationships form a category:

**Objects:** coupling shells $C_K = (f_-(K), f_0(K), f_+(K))$ for $K \ge 1$.

**Morphisms:**

| Name | Algebraic form | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| parity_pushout | $C_{K+1} = A_K \cup_{C_K} B_K$ | gluing two sheets along their core gives the next core |
| shift | $f_+(K) = f_-(K+1)$ | the K union is the K+1 core |
| fusion | $f_+(K) \cdot f_-(K) = f_+(K^2)$ | sheet-product equals scaled union |
| edge | $f_+(K) - 1 = 2\binom{K+1}{2}$ | full union minus seed = simplex edges |
| projective | $f_+(q) = \|\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)\|$ at prime power $q$ | union is the projective plane count |

**In code:** `parity_pushout_category()` returns the structured category; `ParityPushoutMorphism` dataclass carries each morphism's name, description, and algebraic form.

## 8. What this changes for the framework

Four consequences:

**(a) The coupling polynomial is now generative, not just descriptive.** Before this push, $f(K, s)$ was an evaluation rule — give me K and s, I give you a number. After: $f(K, s)$ is the **counting polynomial of a recursive parity machine**. The numbers come from a growth rule, not from a formula.

**(b) The 64-tile is the discriminant squared.** AW 13 had T8 saying $\Delta f = 2K = 2^D$ at D=3. AW 14 adds the squared form $(\Delta f)^2 = 4K^2 = K^3$ at K=4, exactly the closure-core tile size. The closure tile *is* the discriminant of the coupling polynomial squared, evaluated at the framework's preferred $K$.

**(c) The framework's 21-band is geometric, not algebraic.** Before: 21 was $\Phi_3(4)$, an evaluation. After: 21 is $|\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)|$, the point count of a concrete finite projective plane, AND simultaneously the full union of the Tetrad parity sheets ($21 = 13 + 4 + 4$). The framework's preferred dimension reads against multiple concrete finite geometries.

**(d) The K=4 → K=5 hinge has a recursive source.** The hinge in `hinge.py` ($J_4 = |5,-\rangle\langle 4,+|$, $H_5 J_4 = J_4 H_4 = 21 J_4$) was previously stated as the shift identity at the operator level. After AW 14, it is the *unique* portal transition in the parity-pushout category at $K = 4$, with the 21-band tracing all the way back to the K=4 Tetrad's full parity-sheet union.
