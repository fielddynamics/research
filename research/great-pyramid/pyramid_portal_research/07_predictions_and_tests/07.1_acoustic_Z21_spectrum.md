## The prediction

The King's Chamber's natural acoustic mode spectrum should organize into a **21-mode comb** with structure matching the apparatus's $\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7$ symmetry.

Specifically:

- 21 distinct frequency peaks below ~1 kHz
- Grouped into 7 "septets" (or 14 conjugate-pair peaks with 7 magnitudes)
- With Z_3 selection rule: 7 peaks suppressed relative to 14 active
- Spacing pattern consistent with the WRT τ_21 sector magnitudes

## Why 21 modes

Three derivation paths converge on 21:

**1. Cavity mode quantization.** The King's Chamber's dimensions $L : W : H = 20 : 10 : 5\sqrt 5$ cubits give a mode density at low frequencies that, when truncated below the first $|\vec k|^2 = 25$ threshold, has 21 modes (matching $\Phi_3(4)$).

**2. Z_21 = Z_3 × Z_7 structure.** The apparatus's discrete symmetry has 21 sectors (3 cyclotomic × 7 Grand Gallery), each supporting one mode.

**3. SU(2)_19 Chern-Simons.** At level $k = 19$, there are $k + 2 = 21$ shifted-level integrable channels.

All three predictions land on 21. If the apparatus is what the framework says it is, the chamber's mode spectrum should reflect this.

## The 7-corbel structure

Within the 21 modes, the spectrum should organize into **7 frequency clusters**, one per Grand Gallery corbel. Each cluster consists of 3 modes (the 3 cyclotomic sectors), with the $a = 0$ sector suppressed by the antechamber filter.

14 active peaks (in 7 conjugate-pair septets) + 7 suppressed peaks = 21 total mode positions.

## Frequency scale

The apparatus's fundamental acoustic frequency is set by the chamber's largest dimension (20 cubits = 10.47 m) and the speed of sound in the chamber's medium (air, ~343 m/s):

$$f_0 \;\sim\; \frac{c_{\text{air}}}{2L} \;\sim\; \frac{343}{20.94} \;\sim\; 16.4 \text{ Hz}$$

But the chamber is granite-walled, and the modes that mode-couple to granite at depth are *not* purely acoustic — they involve elastic modes of the wall material. For granite ($c_p \sim 6000$ m/s in longitudinal mode):

$$f_{\text{granite}} \;\sim\; \frac{c_p}{2L_{\text{wall}}} \;\sim\; 60-1000 \text{ Hz}$$

depending on the relevant length scale. The actual chamber-wall coupled modes will fall in this Hz - kHz range.

**The 21 modes should fall within ~10 Hz to ~1 kHz**, with specific frequencies determined by the chamber + relieving stack + coupled granite walls. Computing the exact spectrum requires detailed mode analysis of the coupled cavity-wall system.

## Test protocol

1. **Acoustic source**: install a calibrated broadband acoustic source (sweep or impulse) in the King's Chamber.

2. **Microphone array**: place ~10 calibrated microphones at known positions to capture the mode structure (avoid antinodes of the dominant mode).

3. **Sweep frequencies**: from 5 Hz to 2 kHz, with resolution ~0.1 Hz, dwell time ~10 s per frequency.

4. **Record amplitudes**: at each frequency, record the chamber's response. Identify resonance peaks.

5. **Analysis**: count the resonance peaks. Should be ~21 distinct peaks if the framework is right. Identify groupings into 7 clusters.

6. **Z_3 selection rule test**: in each cluster, identify the relative amplitudes. Predicted ratio of suppressed:active should be small (the antechamber filter suppresses by a factor of ~10 or more in amplitude).

7. **WRT magnitude comparison**: rank the 7 cluster magnitudes. Compare to the computed conjugate-pair magnitudes from `03.6`. Dominant pair (4.78) should correspond to the strongest cluster.

## What success looks like

- Exactly 21 peaks in the 5 Hz - 1 kHz range (within experimental resolution)
- Peaks grouped into 7 clusters (with sub-structure)
- Z_3 selection rule visible (suppressed peaks at predicted positions)
- Strongest cluster matches WRT-dominant-pair prediction

## What failure looks like

- Significantly more or fewer than 21 peaks in the predicted range
- No grouping into 7 clusters
- Z_3 selection rule absent
- WRT-magnitude ranking does not match acoustic-cluster ranking

A negative result on this test would falsify the framework's apparatus-level identification at the acoustic-mode level. The algebra and gravity match would survive, but the cavity-QED reading of the King's Chamber would be in trouble.

## Historical / partial data

Some acoustic measurements of the King's Chamber exist:

- Sound levels at specific frequencies (anecdotal reports of mode peaks)
- The chamber's resonance at ~110 Hz is widely noted
- Tom Danley's measurements (2000s) identified multiple resonance peaks

None of the historical measurements were designed to test a 21-mode comb prediction. A targeted test at the resolution and bandwidth required has not been published.
