# 07.4 — μ-metal Magnetic Shielding Test

**Status:** Conjecture (decisive test of pump identification)
**Reading time:** 3 minutes
**Depends on:** 05.1

---

## The prediction

If Earth's geomagnetic field is the apparatus's pump, then **magnetic shielding inside the King's Chamber should observably degrade the apparatus's coherent operation**:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Reduce } B_0 \text{ at KC by } 100\times \;\Longrightarrow\; \Omega_{\text{pump}} \text{ reduces by } 100\times \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{measurable mode-amplitude reduction}\;}$$

## Test design

**Equipment:** mu-metal (or equivalent high-permeability ferromagnetic) shielding sheets enclosing a measurement volume inside the King's Chamber. Mu-metal can reduce DC magnetic field by 100-1000× depending on configuration.

**Configuration:** construct a temporary mu-metal-shielded enclosure (~1 m³) inside the chamber. Place sensitive magnetometers and acoustic detectors inside.

**Baseline measurement:** with no shielding, record:
- Magnetic field at measurement volume (~45 μT expected, possibly higher due to apex concentration)
- Acoustic mode spectrum (the predicted 21-mode comb from `07.1`)
- Any other apparatus-level observables

**Shielded measurement:** with mu-metal enclosure active, record the same quantities. The B-field should drop by ~100×; the acoustic spectrum and apparatus observables should change accordingly.

## Predicted changes

If the geomagnetic pump identification is correct:

1. **Acoustic mode amplitudes** should decrease (less coherent pump → less collective response)
2. **Mode quality factors** might shift (cavity coherence changes)
3. **Z_3 selection rule** should still be visible (it's structural, not pump-dependent), but might be weaker
4. **Inter-mode coherence** should decrease

If the geomagnetic pump identification is wrong:

1. Shielding makes little difference to apparatus observables
2. Acoustic mode spectrum is essentially unchanged
3. Apparatus operates from some other (non-magnetic) pump

## Quantitative prediction

If the apparatus is in the strong-coupling regime at $g\sqrt N / \gamma \approx 4 \times 10^7$ (`05.3`), then reducing the pump by 100× would shift the system to $\sim 4 \times 10^5$ — still strong coupling.

Reducing by 10⁵× (best mu-metal performance) would shift to $\sim 4 \times 10^2$ — marginal strong coupling.

To **fully disrupt** strong coupling, would need shielding by ~10⁷×, well beyond mu-metal's capability. So **the apparatus probably remains in strong coupling even under best practical shielding**. What would change:

- Pump Rabi $\Omega_{\text{pump}}$ scales with $B_0$, reducing by 100-1000×
- Collective Rabi $\Omega_R = 2g\sqrt N$ is independent of pump (set by cavity + N)
- The *steady-state inversion fraction* depends on pump vs decay, so reduces by 100-1000×
- Mode amplitudes (proportional to inversion) reduce by 100-1000×

**Predicted observable:** acoustic mode amplitudes inside the shielded volume should be **~100-1000× lower** than outside the shielded volume (other things equal).

## Subtleties

The chamber's mode spectrum is set by the chamber's *geometry* and the *cavity Q*, neither of which changes under shielding. So the *positions* of mode peaks should not shift. Only the *amplitudes* should shift, reflecting the reduced inversion fraction.

If the mode positions shift under shielding, this would imply the geomagnetic field actively modifies the cavity (not just pumps it). That would be inconsistent with the framework's reading and a surprise.

If the mode amplitudes shift but positions don't, that's exactly the framework's prediction.

## What success looks like

- Acoustic mode amplitudes decrease by ~100-1000× inside shielded volume
- Mode positions unchanged
- Z_3 selection rule still visible but weaker
- Recoverable: remove shielding, amplitudes return to baseline

## What failure looks like

- Acoustic mode amplitudes unchanged inside shielded volume (within ~10%)
- Or amplitudes change in some other way not predicted (e.g., position shifts)

## Time-dependence

Magnetic shielding takes effect within seconds (the mu-metal's response time). If the chamber's mode amplitudes also adjust within seconds, the framework's identification is confirmed.

If they adjust slowly (hours), or do not adjust, the identification is challenged.

## Cost and difficulty

- **Equipment**: mu-metal sheets, magnetometers, acoustic gear (~$30k - $100k)
- **Time**: 1 week on-site
- **Access**: requires permission
- **Risk**: minimal (no physical contact with apparatus surfaces)

This is a **medium-cost, high-information** test. It directly tests the framework's most engineering-specific claim (geomagnetic pump). A negative result would be a strong falsification.
