## 1. The temporal face coupling

The stellated octahedron has eight faces. Four belong to TetA
and four to TetB. The scheme proof (supplementary to Letter 1)
establishes that each tetrahedron carries a 3+1 decomposition:
one face normal has uniform sign and three have mixed signs.

The face normals, expressed as sign patterns on the three
spatial axes, are:

**TetA:**
- Temporal: (+, +, +) — all three components positive
- Spatial: (+, −, −), (−, +, −), (−, −, +) — one positive, two negative

**TetB:**
- Temporal: (−, −, −) — all three components negative
- Spatial: (−, +, +), (+, −, +), (+, +, −) — one negative, two positive

The temporal faces are the only faces with uniform sign. Every
spatial face has a mix of positive and negative components.

The dot product of the two normalised temporal face normals is
exactly −1. They are perfectly antiparallel. No spatial face
pair achieves this: under parity, every pair is antiparallel,
but the temporal faces are distinguished by their uniform-sign
structure — they encode all three spatial dimensions with the
same orientation.

The two temporal normals span a single direction in ℝ³. The
coupling between the two time directions occurs along one axis,
not spread across three. Meanwhile, the six spatial face normals
(three from each tetrahedron) span the full three-dimensional
space.

This means the interaction between the two roots at the
octahedral core is fundamentally non-spatial. The two tetrahedra
couple through their temporal faces — along the direction in
which all three spatial coordinates agree. The core is where two
antiparallel time directions meet.

## 2. The parity operator and the two-state basis

Spatial parity P: eⁱ → −eⁱ (i = 1, 2, 3) negates all three
spatial legs of the frame field. Under this operation, every
TetA face normal maps to the corresponding TetB face normal.
The transformation is exact: P applied to TetA gives TetB, and
P applied to TetB gives TetA. Crucially, P² = I — applying
parity twice returns to the original state. This is an
involution.

The Z₂ symmetry generated by P defines a natural two-state
system. Label the two states |TetA⟩ and |TetB⟩. On this
two-dimensional Hilbert space ℋ = span{|TetA⟩, |TetB⟩}, the
parity operator acts as the Pauli matrix σₓ:

σₓ |TetA⟩ = |TetB⟩,  σₓ |TetB⟩ = |TetA⟩

The "which root" observable — the operator that measures whether
the system is in the TetA state or the TetB state — acts as
σ_z:

σ_z |TetA⟩ = +1 |TetA⟩,  σ_z |TetB⟩ = −1 |TetB⟩

Both operators square to the identity: σₓ² = σ_z² = I. This is
the standard Pauli algebra for a spin-½ system.

The two states are not an analogy. The tetrad equation is
quadratic. Its solution space has two connected components
(Letter 1, scheme proof). The parity that exchanges them is a
discrete Lorentz transformation. The Z₂ symmetry group is the
same group that generates spin-½ representations. The algebraic
structure is identical because the symmetry structure is
identical: a degree-2 equation with a discrete exchange
operation.

## 3. The projection operators

The standard quantum-mechanical projectors onto the two
eigenstates of σ_z are:

P₊ = ½(I + σ_z) — projects onto |TetA⟩
P₋ = ½(I − σ_z) — projects onto |TetB⟩

These satisfy all four axioms of a complete set of orthogonal
projectors:

1. **Idempotency:** P₊² = P₊ and P₋² = P₋. Projecting twice
   gives the same result as projecting once.

2. **Completeness:** P₊ + P₋ = I. Every state is resolved into
   its TetA and TetB components with nothing left over.

3. **Orthogonality:** P₊ P₋ = 0. The two projections are
   mutually exclusive.

4. **Hermiticity:** Both projectors are self-adjoint. The
   measurement is a physical observable.

Now define the state of the gravitational field at dimensionless
acceleration x = g/a₀:

|ψ(x)⟩ = √μ(x) |TetA⟩ + √(1−μ(x)) |TetB⟩

where μ(x) = x/(x+1) is the constitutive law derived from
exchange symmetry in Letter 2.

The expectation value of the TetA projector on this state is:

⟨ψ(x)|P₊|ψ(x)⟩ = μ(x)

This is exact. It is verified computationally at x = 1 (μ = 0.5)
and x = 10 (μ = 10/11 ≈ 0.909). The constitutive law is not
merely an interpolating function. It is the Born-rule probability
of measuring the system in the TetA state.

The idempotency holds at the operator level (P₊² = P₊ on the
Hilbert space) even though the expectation value μ is continuous.
This resolves the apparent tension between the continuous
partition μ(x) = x/(x+1) and the discrete quantum projector
P₊ = (I+σ_z)/2. The projector is discrete. The state varies
continuously with x. The expectation value interpolates smoothly
between the eigenvalues.

## 4. The maximally entangled core

This is the maximally entangled state for a two-state system —

S = −μ ln μ − (1−μ) ln(1−μ) = −½ ln ½ − ½ ln ½ = ln 2 ≈ 0.693

μ(1−μ) = (1 − s²) / 4

This is the variance of a σ_z measurement on a state with
expectation value s, divided by 4. In the spin-½ formalism:

⟨σ_z²⟩ − ⟨σ_z⟩² = 1 − s²

The quantum fluctuation of the root observable is 1 − s².
