## 1. Purpose

The working notes already set a Japanese motif beside the merkavah and the I Ching. [`closure_vortex_synthesis-1.md`](../i-ching/closure_vortex_synthesis-1.md) and [`-2.md`](../i-ching/closure_vortex_synthesis-2.md) both say of the "Triforce / Three Dragon Scales (Japan)" that it "encodes the face structure. Each triangular face subdivided into 3 corners + 1 central."

The motif is the *mitsuuroko* (三つ鱗, "three scales"): three triangles set within a larger one, the crest of the Hōjō, the regent clan of the Kamakura shogunate (1203–1333).

This programme asks:

> **Which Japanese motifs reproduce the geometry of the GFD face structure exactly, under criteria fixed in advance, and how common is that geometry in unrelated traditions?**

As with the Merkavah programme, the study is comparative and structural. It makes no claim of transmission between cultures and no claim about what the motif's makers intended.

## 2. What GFD brings to the comparison

All of the following is stated in the documentation before any motif is examined:

- **The face subdivision.** When TetB's vertex passes through a face of TetA along a body diagonal, it meets the face at its centroid. The face splits into four equal triangles: **three outer corner channels and one central inverted coupling channel**. The three corners are shared with the sibling faces and carry spatial coupling. The central triangle is unique to its face and couples through the Field Origin to the opposite chamber. This is the $3 + 1$ of the spacetime decomposition ([`08-face-structure-and-spin.md`](../../../docs/01-foundations/08-face-structure-and-spin.md)).
- **The channel count.** Four faces × four sub-channels = $K^2 = 16$.
- **The recursion.** Three levels of subdivision give $4^3 = K^D = 64$ sub-channels ([`the_photonic_substrate_enterprise_full_thinking.md`](../../photonic/the_photonic_substrate_enterprise_full_thinking.md)).
- **Full subdivision versus the Sierpinski triangle.** The two must be distinguished, because the distinction is the programme's sharpest test.
  - The **Sierpinski triangle** removes the central triangle at every level and keeps $3^n$ cells: 3, 9, 27.
  - **GFD's subdivision** keeps the centre, the coupling channel, and has $4^n$ cells: 4, 16, 64.

  At the first level the two look identical: three filled triangles around an empty, inverted centre. They diverge at every level after.
- **The hexagram.** Seen along a body diagonal, the stellated octahedron projects to a hexagram, two opposed triangles with both polar vertices on the centre (verified in the [Merkavah plan](../merkavah/PLAN.md) §3).
- **Threefold rotation.** Each triangular face has the rotation group $\mathbb Z_3$. The photonic notes tie this to the photon's spin 1 ([`gfd_light_substrate_synthesis.md`](../../photonic/gfd_light_substrate_synthesis.md)).

## 3. The sources, in three layers

### Layer A — the motifs and their dated uses

| Motif | Form | Dated use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Mitsuuroko*** (three scales) | three triangles within a larger triangle, centre empty | crest of the Hōjō, regents of the Kamakura shogunate (Tokimasa first regent, 1203; fall of Kamakura, 1333); a red war banner with three gold scales | the defining motif |
| ***Uroko-mon*** (scale pattern) | an all-over field of alternating triangles | Noh costume: in *Dōjōji*, when the woman is revealed as a serpent, her outer robe is folded down to show an inner robe of gold triangles, the serpent's scales | protective and serpent associations; dated examples to be collected |
| ***Kagome*** (basket eye) | the woven-bamboo lattice: hexagons each ringed by six triangles, forming hexagrams; the trihexagonal tiling | traditional basketry; used as a protective motif | gave its name to the *kagome lattice* of condensed-matter physics (Husimi) |
| ***Asanoha*** (hemp leaf) | a six-pointed star built from triangles radiating from a centre | common textile pattern, Heian period onward (to be confirmed) | its geometry is to be compared with body-diagonal projections in Phase 1, not assumed |
| ***Mitsudomoe*** (three commas) | three swirls with threefold rotation | Shinto shrines, crests | a $\mathbb Z_3$ case; interpretive |

### Layer B — the narrative and religious context

- **The Enoshima legend of the three scales.** Hōjō Tokimasa makes a 21-day retreat at Enoshima, praying for the fortune of his descendants. A goddess appears, foretells his line's rule, and vanishes into the sea in serpent form, leaving three scales, which become the Hōjō crest. The tale is usually traced to the *Taiheiki* (14th century); this is to be confirmed against the text in Phase 0. The goddess of Enoshima is Benzaiten.
- **The *Enoshima Engi*** (1047, by the Tendai monk Kōkei). The island's founding legend: Benzaiten raises Enoshima from the bay in 552 and descends upon it; the five-headed dragon Gozuryū, which had terrorised the coast, falls in love with her; she refuses until he reforms, and he turns into Dragon's-Mouth Hill facing her island.
- **Benzaiten** herself. She is a syncretic deity, the Indian river goddess Sarasvatī received through Buddhism and associated in Japan with water, serpents and dragons. Her serpent form is the source of the scales.

### Layer C — reception history (context, not evidence)

- **The Triforce.** Nintendo's emblem in *The Legend of Zelda* (1986 onward) is widely traced to the *mitsuuroko*. It is cited only to separate it from Layers A and B.
- **Theories of Japanese–Israelite common descent** (*Nichiyu dōsoron*). These claim, among other things, that hexagrams at Ise and the *kagome* motif show ancient contact. They are pseudo-historical, and the programme keeps them out, as the Merkavah programme keeps out the modern "Mer-Ka-Ba".

## 5. Method and controls

1. **Fix the GFD side first.** Only the structures in §2 may be used.
2. **Base-rate control: the geometry is common.** The first-level subdivision is one of the most natural figures in geometry, and it appears in unrelated traditions. The floor of Anagni Cathedral in Italy, laid around 1104, is often cited as an early Sierpinski pattern, and Cosmatesque floors of the 12th–13th centuries, contemporary with the Hōjō, use the same nested triangles. A match at the first level therefore shows a shared *geometry*, not a shared *source*. The programme must say so.
3. **The discriminating test is depth.** Since the first level cannot separate Sierpinski from GFD, the evidential weight lies at the second level and beyond. Does any Japanese motif subdivide the central triangle too, giving $4^n$, or does it leave the centre empty at every level, giving $3^n$? The answer must be recorded honestly either way.
4. **Primary sources and dated objects.** Every motif claim is tied to dated objects (crests, banners, costumes, textiles) in museum collections, and every narrative claim to its text (*Taiheiki*, *Enoshima Engi*) in a named translation or edition.
5. **Publish everything examined**, with class and outcome, including failures and null results.
6. **Review.** The synthesis is offered for comment to scholars of Japanese art history and religion before it is presented as a result.
