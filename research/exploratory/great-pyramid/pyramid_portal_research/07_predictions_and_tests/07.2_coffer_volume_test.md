# 07.2 — Coffer Volume Test

**Status:** Consistent with Petrie's data (0.1% with floor-to-ceiling chamber height; 2.3% with wall height); refinement test recommended

## The prediction

$$\boxed{\;V_{\text{coffer (external)}} \;=\; \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}\;}$$

with $V_{\text{KC}}$ the floor-to-ceiling chamber volume. On Petrie's measured chamber (19,550,960 in³) this gives $V_{\text{coffer}}^{\text{predicted}} = 142{,}670$ in³ = 2.338 m³ = 16.25 c³. On the idealised chamber $1000\sqrt5$ c³ it gives 16.32 c³.

**The measurement agrees with the prediction to 0.1%, well within its ±0.4% uncertainty, provided the floor-to-ceiling chamber height is used.** Earlier versions of this file reported 0.79%; that figure came from a mistyped coffer height (41.13 for 41.31 in) and the idealised chamber volume.

Two caveats apply before this is counted as confirmation:

- **Convention dependence.** The granite floor stands ~5 in above the base of the walls, and the wall height gives 140.2. The prediction must specify the floor-to-ceiling height in advance, as it now does.
- **Look-elsewhere effect.** The comparison was chosen after inspecting the data, from many possible ratios. A refined measurement is needed to see whether 137.0 survives at higher precision.

## Refined measurement protocol

**Goal:** measure the coffer's external volume to ~0.1% precision.

**Method:**

1. **3D laser scanning** of the coffer at sub-mm resolution. Modern handheld scanners achieve 0.1 mm accuracy over scale of meters.

2. **Identify original surfaces.** Catalog any chipping, erosion, or damage. Reconstruct the original surface profile.

3. **Volume computation** from the surface mesh. Standard solid-geometry numerical integration to 0.01% precision.

4. **Uncertainty analysis.** Quantify uncertainty from (a) scanning resolution, (b) original-surface reconstruction, (c) damage corrections. Total expected uncertainty: ~0.1%.

5. **Compare** to predicted $V_{\text{coffer}} = \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}$ using a fresh measurement of $V_{\text{KC}}$ (also via laser scanning, with similar precision).

## Sensitivity

At 0.1% measurement precision:

- If $V_{\text{coffer}}/V_{\text{KC}}$ lands within 0.1% of $\alpha$: framework's α-step is **strongly verified**
- If it lands at 0.5% offset: borderline (within measurement uncertainty of the framework)
- If it lands at 5% offset: framework's α-step identification is **falsified**

The current Petrie data is at 0.1%, inside its own ~0.4% uncertainty. A refined measurement will either hold the ratio at 137.04 or move it away.

## What is being measured

The α-step is **dimensionless**: $V_{\text{coffer}}/V_{\text{KC}}$ is a pure number. It is **not** sensitive to the choice of measurement units (royal cubits, meters, etc.).

This is a key feature: the test is anchored to the fine structure constant $\alpha$, an independent physical constant known to better than 0.1 ppb. The framework predicts a specific dimensionless ratio in the apparatus.

## What success means

If the ratio lands within 0.1% of $\alpha$:

- The framework's identification of the coffer as the primitive Coulomb edge is **quantitatively verified**
- The apparatus's algebraic design is precise to the limits of stone-age construction
- The "1 + 20" factorization $\alpha^{21} = \alpha^1 \cdot \alpha^{20}$ has direct apparatus realization

This would be one of the framework's strongest individual empirical anchors — comparable to the gravity G prediction at 7.5 ppm.

## What failure means

If the ratio deviates by 1% or more:

- The coffer α-step identification is wrong
- Either (a) the coffer plays a different role, or (b) the apparatus has more relaxed dimensional tolerances than implied
- The framework's broader claims still survive (the gravity equation is independent), but the apparatus-specific identifications would need rework

Failure on this test would not falsify the framework's gravity derivation. It would falsify the **specific apparatus identification** of the coffer as the primitive α-step.
