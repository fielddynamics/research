# 02.4 — Permutation Discrimination

**Status:** Verified (empirical)
**Reading time:** 3 minutes
**Depends on:** 02.1, 02.3

---

## The question

The framework derives three integers $\{13, 17, 21\}$ from the closure-read trace at K=4. The gravity equation places them as:

$$\alpha_G \;=\; \frac{17}{13} \cdot \alpha^{21} \cdot \frac{g_e}{2}$$

with role assignment: 21 = exponent, 17 = numerator, 13 = denominator.

**Question:** is this assignment uniquely picked out by the data, or could any of the six permutations match CODATA G equally well?

## The test

For each permutation $(a, b, n)$ of $\{13, 17, 21\}$ with $a$ in the numerator, $b$ in the denominator, and $n$ as the exponent, solve:

$$G \cdot m_e^2 / \hbar c \;=\; \frac{a}{b} \cdot \alpha^n \cdot \frac{g_e}{2}$$

for $n$, using CODATA values of $G, m_e, \hbar, c, \alpha, g_e$. The framework predicts the resulting $n$ should be an integer (specifically, the framework predicts 21).

If the measured $n$ is far from any integer for five of the six permutations and is exactly an integer for one, that one permutation is uniquely picked out.

## The result

| Numerator | Denominator | Exponent (measured) $n$ | Distance to nearest integer $|n - \text{round}(n)|$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **17** | **13** | **20.999998** | **2.5 × 10⁻⁶** |
| 21 | 17 | 20.988422 | 1.2 × 10⁻² |
| 21 | 13 | 21.042944 | 4.3 × 10⁻² |
| 17 | 21 | 20.902528 | 9.8 × 10⁻² |
| 13 | 17 | 20.890953 | 1.1 × 10⁻¹ |
| 13 | 21 | 20.848006 | 1.5 × 10⁻¹ |

## Reading the result

**The (17/13, 21) assignment is the unique permutation giving an integer exponent.**

The next-best permutation (21/17) gives $n \approx 20.988$, off the nearest integer (21) by 1.2 × 10⁻². The framework's prediction is off by 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ — **four orders of magnitude better**.

## Statistical reading

If the framework's role assignment were random, the expected distribution of $|n - \text{round}(n)|$ would be roughly uniform on $[0, 0.5]$. The probability of hitting a value below $10^{-5}$ by chance for a single permutation is $\sim 2 \times 10^{-5}$.

For the framework's structural assignment to hit this bound out of 6 permutations is a $\sim 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ event under the null hypothesis. The result is therefore not consistent with chance assignment.

## Why the framework predicts (17/13, 21)

From the closure-read decomposition and three-sweep kernel (see `01.3`):

- $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{+1}) = 21$ → exponent of $\alpha$ (three-sweep total attenuation)
- $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{0}) = 17$ → field-origin trace (numerator)
- $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{-1}) = 13$ → parity-graded trace (denominator)

The ratio $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_0)/\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{-1}) = 17/13$ is the *impedance step* between the parity-invariant and parity-graded reads of the same closure-read representation.

The framework derives this assignment **before** comparing to G data. The permutation test is a consistency check, not a fit.

## Robustness to uncertainty

The 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ deviation from integer 21 propagates from:

- CODATA G uncertainty (~22 ppm)
- $m_e$ uncertainty (~0.3 ppm)
- $\alpha$ uncertainty (~0.15 ppb)
- $g_e/2$ uncertainty (~0.0003 ppb)

The dominant uncertainty is in $G$ itself. The framework's integer prediction is **inside the experimental uncertainty band of measured G**, meaning future improvements in $G$ measurement should sharpen or shift the integer-21 lock.

If a future $G$ measurement places the exponent at, say, $20.9999 \pm 0.0001$, the framework would be falsified at that level. The current best measurement places it at $20.9999975 \pm 0.0000022$ — consistent with integer 21.
