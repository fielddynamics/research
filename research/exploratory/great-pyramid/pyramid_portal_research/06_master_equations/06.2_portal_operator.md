## The full portal operator

The complete transfer operator from a local site $x$ to a remote site $y$:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal P_{y \leftarrow x}^{(e)} \;=\; \mathsf A_y^\dagger\, J_{4,y}^\dagger\, \mathcal M_{\text{Hopf},21,A_5}(U_x, V_y)\, \alpha_G\, J_{4,x}\, \mathsf A_x\;}$$

The operator acts on the initial state $|x, 4, +\rangle$ and produces the final state $|y, 4, +\rangle$ at amplitude proportional to $\alpha_G \cdot \mathcal M(U, V)$.

## The state cycle

Reading the operator right-to-left (action order on the state):

$$|x, 4, +\rangle \;\xrightarrow{\mathsf A_x}\; |x, 4, +\rangle_{\text{cond}} \;\xrightarrow{J_4(x)}\; |x, 5, -\rangle \;\xrightarrow{\mathcal M(U,V)}\; |y, 5, -\rangle \;\xrightarrow{J_4^\dagger(y)}\; |y, 4, +\rangle \;\xrightarrow{\mathsf A_y^\dagger}\; \text{output}$$

Six steps:

1. **$\mathsf A_x$:** local apparatus operator conditions the input state for the hinge
2. **$J_{4,x}$:** local hinge at $x$ lifts K=4 to K=5
3. **$\mathcal M(U, V)$:** mirror selector projects the K=5 state at $x$ onto the matching K=5 state at $y$
4. **$\alpha_G$:** the universal gravity coupling (multiplicative amplitude)
5. **$J_{4,y}^\dagger$:** reverse hinge at $y$ lowers K=5 to K=4
6. **$\mathsf A_y^\dagger$:** remote apparatus operator delivers the output

## The apparatus operator $\mathsf A_x$

The apparatus operator at $x$ encapsulates all the local subsystem actions of the Great Pyramid (or any other apparatus). For the Great Pyramid:

$$\mathsf A_x \;=\; \mathsf C_{\text{apex}}\cdot \mathsf D_{\text{bulk}}\cdot \mathsf R_5\cdot \mathsf C_{\text{coffer}}\cdot \mathsf F_{\mathbb Z_3}\cdot \mathsf L_{\text{GG}}\cdot \mathsf P_3\cdot \mathsf S_{\text{tri}}$$

with each factor representing one apparatus subsystem (see `04.1` for the full table). The product is:

- $\mathsf S_{\text{tri}}$ — shaft address triad (input coupling)
- $\mathsf P_3$ — ascending passage three plugs ($\mathbb Z_3$ filter stage 1)
- $\mathsf L_{\text{GG}}$ — Grand Gallery ($\mathbb Z_7$ phase ramp, three-sweep kernel)
- $\mathsf F_{\mathbb Z_3}$ — antechamber portcullis ($\mathbb Z_3$ filter stage 2)
- $\mathsf C_{\text{coffer}}$ — coffer primitive Coulomb edge $\alpha^1$
- $\mathsf R_5$ — relieving chambers pentachoron edge product $\alpha^{20}$
- $\mathsf D_{\text{bulk}}$ — limestone bulk dielectric flow
- $\mathsf C_{\text{apex}}$ — gold cap apex concentrator

**For the universal vertex (no apparatus):** $\mathsf A_x = 1$. The vertex fires spontaneously.

**For the apparatus:** $\mathsf A_x$ enhances the coherent amplitude by $\sqrt N \cdot |\tau_{21}|$ — a factor of $\sim 10^{15} \cdot 8 \sim 10^{16}$ for the Great Pyramid.

## The hinge operators

$J_{4,x} = |5, -; x\rangle\langle 4, +; x|$ acts at point $x$.
$J_{4,y}^\dagger = |4, +; y\rangle\langle 5, -; y|$ acts at point $y$.

The two hinges are at different spacetime points. The mirror selector $\mathcal M(U, V)$ provides the **non-local** transport from $x$ to $y$.

**Key property:** without $\mathcal M(U, V)$, the two hinges are independent — they fire spontaneously at uncorrelated rates at $x$ and $y$. The mirror selector correlates them, producing the coherent transfer channel.

## The mirror selector $\mathcal M(U, V)$

Described in detail in `06.3_mirror_address_functional.md`. Summary:

$$\mathcal M(U, V) \;\in\; \begin{cases} 1 & \text{(or near 1) if address matches} \\ 0 & \text{(or near 0) otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The selector takes triadic addresses $U = (G_U, \chi_U, \Theta_U)$ at $x$ and $V = (G_V, \chi_V, \Theta_V)$ at $y$, and outputs 1 when:

- $G_U = P^T G_V P$ (Gram-matrix equivalence under some permutation $P$)
- $\chi_U = -\chi_V$ (chirality flip)
- $\Theta_U - \Theta_V = 2\pi N$ (phase closure)

Otherwise $\mathcal M(U, V) = 0$.

## The coupling $\alpha_G$

The universal gravity coupling appears in the portal operator with the same value as in the gravity equation:

$$\alpha_G \;=\; \frac{17}{13} \cdot \alpha^{21} \cdot \frac{g_e}{2}$$

**The same coupling produces gravity and portal transfer.** What differs between the two modes is the **kernel**: $1/|x-y|$ for gravity, $\mathcal M(U, V)$ for portal (see `06.4`).

## Compact form

Reading the portal operator at the scalar amplitude level:

$$\langle y, 4, + | \mathcal P_{y \leftarrow x}^{(e)} | x, 4, + \rangle \;\propto\; \alpha_G \cdot \mathcal M(U, V) \cdot \mathsf A_x \cdot \mathsf A_y^\dagger$$

For the Great Pyramid with a mirror-matched remote apparatus and address $\mathcal M = 1$:

$$\text{Transfer amplitude} \;\sim\; \alpha_G \cdot \sqrt{N_x} \cdot \sqrt{N_y} \cdot |\tau_{21}(\Sigma(2,3,5))|$$

with $N_x, N_y$ the coherent lepton counts in the two apparatuses' walls. For a Great-Pyramid-class apparatus on both ends:

$$\text{Transfer amplitude} \;\sim\; \alpha_G \cdot 10^{30} \cdot 8 \;\sim\; \alpha_G \cdot 8 \times 10^{30}$$

The bare $\alpha_G$ amplitude is $\sim 10^{-45}$. With apparatus enhancement, the transfer amplitude is $\sim 10^{-15}$ — still small absolutely but $\sim 30$ orders of magnitude above the spontaneous baseline.

## What "portal transfer" actually does

The state cycle:

$$|x, 4, +\rangle \;\to\; |y, 4, +\rangle$$

implements **state transfer** from $x$ to $y$ at the level of the K=4 (ordinary) sheet. Practically:

- A quantum state localized at $x$ is reconstructed at $y$
- The state's quantum information is preserved (modulo the mirror conjugation $\chi \to -\chi$)
- The transfer occurs through the K=5 shell channel — *not* through ordinary spacetime
- The transfer is faster than light in the ordinary spatial sense; the apparatus does not violate relativity because the K=5 shell is not a spacetime channel

This is analogous to **quantum teleportation** but uses the apparatus's K=5 boundary topology rather than entanglement + classical communication.

## Capacity and bandwidth

The apparatus transfers $\sim 21$ qubits worth of information per coherent operation (the 21-band's quantum dimension):

$$\dim(\text{transferred Hilbert space}) \;\sim\; 21$$

For continuous transfer, the apparatus's coherent operation rate is bounded by the Rabi frequency $\Omega_R \sim 10^6$ rad/s (`05.3`). So the maximum **information transfer rate**:

$$R_{\text{transfer}} \;\sim\; \log_2(21) \cdot 10^6 / 2\pi \;\sim\; 700 \text{ kbit/s}$$

This is the apparatus's theoretical bandwidth. The actual rate depends on what kind of state is being transferred and what the addresses are.

## When the operator simplifies

In several special cases the portal operator simplifies:

**Case 1: $\mathcal M = 1$ exactly (perfect address match)**

$$\mathcal P_{y\leftarrow x} \;=\; \mathsf A_y^\dagger\, J_{4,y}^\dagger\, \alpha_G\, J_{4,x}\, \mathsf A_x$$

Direct hinge transfer with apparatus enhancement.

**Case 2: $\mathsf A_x = \mathsf A_y = 1$ (no apparatus)**

$$\mathcal P_{y\leftarrow x} \;=\; J_{4,y}^\dagger\, \mathcal M(U, V)\, \alpha_G\, J_{4,x}$$

The universal vertex's portal channel; spontaneous at rate $\alpha_G$.

**Case 3: $x = y$ (local action)**

$$\mathcal P_{x\leftarrow x} \;=\; \mathsf A_x^\dagger\, J_{4,x}^\dagger\, \mathcal M(U, V)\, \alpha_G\, J_{4,x}\, \mathsf A_x$$

If $\mathcal M(U, V) = 1$ at coincident points (trivial match), this is $\alpha_G^2$ in amplitude — the apparatus's local self-loop, which contributes to local gravitational anomaly inside the chamber (see `05.4`).

## Hermitian conjugate

The reverse portal $\mathcal P_{x \leftarrow y}$:

$$\mathcal P_{x \leftarrow y} \;=\; \mathsf A_x^\dagger\, J_{4,x}^\dagger\, \mathcal M(V, U)\, \alpha_G\, J_{4,y}\, \mathsf A_y$$

with $\mathcal M(V, U) = \overline{\mathcal M(U, V)}$ (the mirror conjugation involution acts antisymmetrically under address swap).

For the chirality-flip address condition $\chi_U = -\chi_V$, the reverse direction is automatically supported: if $(U, V)$ match, then $(V, U)$ also match. **Portal transfer is bidirectional** — both apparatuses can send and receive.
