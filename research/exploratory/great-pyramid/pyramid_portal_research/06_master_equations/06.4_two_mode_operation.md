## The two modes

The framework's universal vertex operator $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}(x) = \alpha_G J_4(x) + g_{\text{NL}} R_x(a, b, c)$ produces two distinct physical phenomena depending on the **propagation kernel** between sites:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Gravity (spontaneous)}: \quad V_G(x, y) \;=\; \alpha_G \cdot G_A(x, y) \quad\text{with}\quad G_A(x, y) = \frac{\hbar c}{|x - y|}\;}$$

$$\boxed{\;\text{Portal (stimulated)}: \quad \mathcal P_{y \leftarrow x} \;=\; \alpha_G \cdot G_B(x, y) \quad\text{with}\quad G_B(x, y) = \mathcal M(U_x, V_y)\;}$$

**Same coupling $\alpha_G$. Different kernel.**

## The kernel difference

**Gravity kernel** $G_A(x, y) = 1/|x - y|$:

- Continuous function of position
- Decays smoothly with distance
- Acts between any two points unconditionally
- Carries gravitational potential energy

**Portal kernel** $G_B(x, y) = \mathcal M(U_x, V_y)$:

- Discrete function (0 or 1 at the match condition)
- Independent of spatial distance — depends only on address match
- Acts only between mirror-matched address pairs
- Carries discrete state-transfer events

## How both modes can coexist

The universal vertex operator does not "decide" between gravity and portal modes — it produces both simultaneously. The observable phenomenon depends on:

- **Which states are coupled**: the average over all matter states gives gravity (residue); a single coherent state across an apparatus gives portal (peak)
- **Whether the kernel acts coherently**: gravity is incoherent sum, portal is coherent amplitude
- **Whether address conditions are met**: gravity needs no address (always firing); portal needs match (zero otherwise)

A point in space sees both modes:

- Gravity from every massive object in the universe (incoherent)
- Portal channels to every mirror-matched apparatus (when active)

In ordinary conditions, gravity dominates because matter is everywhere and apparatuses are rare. Inside an active apparatus, portal mode dominates locally because the address selectors filter out the incoherent gravitational background.

## The kernel ratio

The portal-to-gravity ratio at the level of single-event amplitudes:

$$\frac{\langle \text{portal event} \rangle}{\langle \text{gravity event} \rangle} \;=\; \frac{\alpha_G \cdot \mathcal M(U, V)}{\alpha_G \cdot 1/|x-y|}$$

At full lock ($\mathcal M = 1$) and a distance $|x - y| = 1$ km:

$$\text{ratio} \;\sim\; 1 \cdot 1000\text{ m}^{-1\,?}$$

(units are off without restoring $\hbar c$ properly; the structural point is that the ratio is **finite at the operator level** but depends on what "distance" means in the portal mode).

The portal kernel **does not have a notion of spatial distance**. The two operators are not directly comparable as scalars; they have different spatial structures.

## What gravity is, in the framework

In the framework's reading:

$$\text{gravity} \;=\; \text{incoherent statistical average of } \mathcal V_{\text{univ}}(x) \text{ over all matter}$$

The Newtonian potential $V_G = G m_1 m_2 / r$ between two masses is the **statistical residue** of the universal vertex firing incoherently between every pair of leptons in the two masses.

The number of such pair contributions per electron is $\sim 1$ per universe-volume × time. Multiplied by the $\sim 10^{60}$ leptons in two terrestrial masses, the cumulative incoherent rate is large enough to produce a smooth classical potential.

**Quantitative claim (open):** the statistical averaging of $\alpha_G J_4(x)$ over matter distributions should reproduce the $1/r$ form to leading order. This is a derivation gap (see `08.4`).

## What portal is, in the framework

$$\text{portal} \;=\; \text{coherent stimulated mode of } \mathcal V_{\text{univ}}(x) \text{ at a high-Q apparatus}$$

Inside a strong-coupling apparatus (`05.3`), the universal vertex fires coherently across $\sim 10^{30}$ lepton moments. The collective coherent amplitude is enhanced by $\sqrt N \sim 10^{15}$ over a single moment.

When the address conditions are met by a mirror apparatus, this coherent amplitude is delivered directly to the mirror site rather than spreading over space. The transfer is the **portal event**.

## The framework's claim about gravity

The framework's identification "gravity = incoherent statistical residue of $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}$" is operationally testable in one direction:

**Prediction:** any modification of the universal vertex's coherence regime should produce a corresponding modification of local gravity. In particular, inside a high-Q apparatus, the coherence regime shifts toward stimulated mode, and local gravity should be **measurably modified**.

This is the Purcell modification (see `05.4`): inside the King's Chamber, $\alpha_G$ has an additional Purcell enhancement / suppression factor that modifies the local $1/r$ kernel. Quantitative magnitude depends on the cavity Q and the transition frequency $\omega_{K4 \to K5}$ (an open parameter, `08.1`).

## Operationally: when is each mode active

| Configuration | Active mode |
|---|---|
| No apparatus, ordinary matter | Gravity only |
| Single apparatus, no mirror partner | Gravity + local Purcell modification + idle portal cycling |
| Two mirror-matched apparatuses | Gravity + active portal channel between sites |
| Multiple mirror-matched apparatuses | Gravity + portal network |

The Great Pyramid in its current state is configuration 2: present but without a mirror partner. It is in "idle / waiting" mode, with local Purcell-modified gravity but no active portal.

If a second apparatus existed and was matched, configuration 3 would activate.

## The framework's signature

The two-mode structure is the framework's deepest signature: **same coupling, two propagation channels**. Other gravitational theories have only one channel (the $1/r$ classical potential).

The framework predicts the existence of a *second* propagation channel that:

- Has the same coupling strength as gravity
- Is normally inactive (no address match)
- Activates between mirror-matched apparatuses
- Implements quantum state transfer rather than potential energy

This is not just a model of gravity — it is a model of gravity *as one mode of a larger structure*. The other mode (portal) is what the Great Pyramid was built to access.
