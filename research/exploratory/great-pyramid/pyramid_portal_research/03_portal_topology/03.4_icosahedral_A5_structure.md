## The five sub-tetrahedra of the pentachoron

The K=5 pentachoron has $\binom{5}{4} = 5$ tetrahedral cells (its 3-faces), each obtained by omitting one of the 5 vertices. Label them $T_1, T_2, T_3, T_4, T_5$ where $T_v$ excludes vertex $v$.

The orientation-preserving permutations of the 5 vertices form the alternating group:

$$A_5 \;=\; \{\text{even permutations of } 5 \text{ vertices}\},\qquad |A_5| = 60$$

$A_5$ acts transitively on the 5 sub-tetrahedra by permuting which vertex is omitted.

## $A_5$ as the icosahedral rotation group

**Standard isomorphism:**

$$A_5 \;\cong\; I \;=\; \text{rotation group of the regular icosahedron}$$

Both groups have order 60. The icosahedron has 12 vertices, 30 edges, 20 faces; its rotational symmetries pair up with $A_5$ via the action on its 5 inscribed tetrahedra (the icosahedron contains exactly 5 regular tetrahedra related by rotations).

**Consequence:** The 5 sub-tetrahedra of the K=5 pentachoron carry an *icosahedral* symmetry. The pentachoron's combinatorial structure is the same as the 5-tetrahedron decomposition of the icosahedron, which is the same as $A_5$ acting on a 5-element set.

## The binary icosahedral group

Lifting $A_5$ from $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ to $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ via the double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ gives the **binary icosahedral group**:

$$2I \;=\; 2A_5 \;\subset\; \mathrm{SU}(2),\qquad |2A_5| = 120$$

This is a discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ of order 120. It is one of the **ADE-type** finite subgroups (specifically, type $E_8$).

## The Poincaré homology 3-sphere

The quotient of $\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3$ by the binary icosahedral group is the **Poincaré homology sphere**:

$$\boxed{\;\Sigma(2, 3, 5) \;=\; S^3 / 2A_5\;}$$

**Properties of $\Sigma(2, 3, 5)$:**

- 3-manifold
- Same rational homology as $S^3$: $H_*(\Sigma; \mathbb Q) = H_*(S^3; \mathbb Q)$
- **Different integral homology:** the fundamental group is finite but non-trivial:
$$\pi_1(\Sigma(2, 3, 5)) \;=\; 2A_5,\qquad |2A_5| = 120$$
- Historically: Poincaré's 1904 counterexample showing that integer homology does not determine 3-manifold topology (motivating the Poincaré conjecture in homotopy form)
- Has 120-element fundamental group, which acts freely on $S^3$ to produce $\Sigma$
- Carries a flat $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-bundle structure with monodromy $2A_5$
- Is the only known integer homology 3-sphere with finite fundamental group

## Why this is the apparatus's address space

The K=5 shell boundary is $S^3$ (from `03.3`). The discrete address resolution of the apparatus is set by the 5 sub-tetrahedral choices, which transform under $A_5$. Lifting to the Hopf bundle, this becomes the 120-element $2A_5$ acting on $\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3$.

**The apparatus's discrete address space is $\Sigma(2, 3, 5) = S^3 / 2A_5$.**

The continuous Hopf-bundle address (`03.3`) and the discrete icosahedral address (this file) combine into a single statement:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Address space} \;=\; \Sigma(2, 3, 5)\;\times\;\mathbb Z_{21}\;}$$

with $\Sigma(2, 3, 5)$ for the geometric address and $\mathbb Z_{21}$ for the 21-band phase address.

Total discrete address count:

$$|2A_5| \times |\mathbb Z_{21}| \;=\; 120 \times 21 \;=\; 2520$$

This is $\text{lcm}(1, 2, \dots, 10)$. The smallest integer divisible by all single-digit positive integers. Why this number — significance is structural and may or may not be deep. Flagged.

## Seifert fibered structure

$\Sigma(2, 3, 5)$ is the **Seifert fibered space** $\Sigma(2, 3, 5)$ with three exceptional fibers of multiplicities 2, 3, 5. The "2, 3, 5" refers to the orders of three exceptional fibers in a fibration over $S^2$.

The exceptional multiplicities have a direct apparatus reading:

| Multiplicity | $\Sigma$ exceptional fiber | Apparatus structure |
|:---:|---|---|
| 2 | $\mathbb Z_2$ | chirality / parity flip (mirror $\chi$) |
| 3 | $\mathbb Z_3$ | three plugs / three portcullis / three cyclotomic polynomials |
| 5 | $\mathbb Z_5$ | five sub-tetrahedra / five relieving chambers / five vertices of $K_5$ |

**Reading:** the three exceptional fibers of $\Sigma(2, 3, 5)$ correspond to the three discrete symmetries the apparatus realizes physically.

The product $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$ matches the number of edges of the icosahedron (and equivalently, the number of edges of its dual dodecahedron).

## $A_5$ representations

$A_5$ has 5 irreducible complex representations:

| Rep | Dimension | Character |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| trivial | 1 | identity |
| 3-dim "standard" | 3 | $\bar 3$ |
| second 3-dim | 3 | $3'$ (Galois conjugate) |
| 4-dim | 4 | $\bar 4$ |
| 5-dim | 5 | $\bar 5$ |

Sum of squared dimensions: $1 + 9 + 9 + 16 + 25 = 60 = |A_5|$. ✓

The 3-dimensional representations are the **two icosahedral rotation representations**: they differ by a Galois conjugation involving $\sqrt 5$. The 4-dimensional is the "standard" representation of $A_5$ as permutations on 5 elements (minus the trivial fixed direction). The 5-dimensional is the symmetric square of one 3-dim minus a trivial.

## $2A_5$ representations and McKay correspondence

The binary icosahedral group $2A_5$ has 9 irreducible representations. The McKay correspondence relates these to the affine Dynkin diagram $\tilde E_8$:

$$2A_5 \;\longleftrightarrow\; \tilde E_8$$

This is the "$E_8$ singularity" in algebraic geometry. The 9 reps correspond to the 9 nodes of the affine $\tilde E_8$ diagram.

**Why this matters:** the $E_8$ classification is one of the most exceptional in mathematics. Its appearance via the binary icosahedral group is not a coincidence — it reflects the deep structure of the icosahedron.

The framework's K=5 boundary apparatus is *automatically* linked to $E_8$ structure through the pentachoron's icosahedral symmetry. This is a structural observation; the operational consequences for the apparatus are conjectural.

## Connection to the apparatus

The 5 relieving chambers physically realize the 5 sub-tetrahedra of the K=5 pentachoron. Their inter-chamber Coulomb couplings transform under $A_5$ at the orbit level, and under $2A_5$ when lifted to the spinor / Hopf-bundle level. Specifically:

- Permuting the 5 chambers corresponds to choosing different elements of the coset $S_5 / S_4 = $ 5-element set, with $A_5$ acting by orientation-preserving even permutations.
- Each chamber has an orientation (the chamber's geometric handedness through its slope angle and material loading), giving the $\mathbb Z_2$ deck transformation.
- The full discrete address is then an element of $2A_5$ acting on the apparatus's spinor lift.

When the address is selected by the shaft system to match a remote apparatus, the matching condition $\mathcal M(U, V) = 1$ requires that the two apparatuses' $2A_5$ elements be related by the mirror conjugation involution (chirality flip).
