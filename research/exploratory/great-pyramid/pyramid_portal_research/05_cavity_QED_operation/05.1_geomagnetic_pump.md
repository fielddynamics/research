## The pump physics

For an apparatus with effective magnetic moment $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ coupling to the geomagnetic field $B_0$ through the apex concentration factor $\mathcal G$, the pump Rabi frequency is:

$$\boxed{\;\Omega_{\text{pump}} \;=\; \frac{\mu_{\text{eff}} \cdot B_0 \cdot \mathcal G_{\text{apex}}}{\hbar}\;}$$

with:

- $\mu_{\text{eff}} = \mu_B \cdot (g_e/2)$ — lepton-anchored, the K=4 ↔ K=5 transition magnetic moment
- $B_0 \approx 45\,\mu$T — Giza geomagnetic field
- $\mathcal G_{\text{apex}} \approx 2.6$ — bulk geometric concentration factor (computed below)

## Geometric apex concentration

The Great Pyramid has slope angle 51.84° from horizontal. The angle from vertical (the apex axis) is $90° - 51.84° = 38.16°$.

A conducting cap on a conical apex concentrates an external static B-field by a factor depending on the apex half-angle:

$$\mathcal G_{\text{apex}} \;\sim\; \frac{1}{\sin^2(\theta_{\text{half}})}$$

At $\theta_{\text{half}} = 38.16°$:

$$\sin(38.16°) \;\approx\; 0.618$$

$$\mathcal G_{\text{apex}} \;\approx\; \frac{1}{0.618^2} \;\approx\; 2.62$$

This is the **bulk concentration** — the factor by which the apex region's flux density exceeds ambient. It propagates down to the King's Chamber roughly intact (static fields have no exponential attenuation through diamagnetic limestone).

For comparison: a sharp gold capstone with tip-to-base ratio of ~30 would concentrate the local field at the tip by an additional factor of ~30 (lightning-rod effect). But this is *at the tip*, not at the King's Chamber — and only the bulk concentration propagates down to the chamber.

## Pump Rabi frequency

Numerical evaluation:

$$\mu_{\text{eff}} \;=\; \mu_B \cdot (g_e/2) \;=\; 9.274 \times 10^{-24} \cdot 1.00116 \;=\; 9.285 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{J/T}$$

$$\Omega_{\text{pump}} \;=\; \frac{9.285 \times 10^{-24} \cdot 45 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 2.62}{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}$$

$$\Omega_{\text{pump}} \;\approx\; 1.04 \times 10^{7}\,\text{rad/s}$$

$$\Omega_{\text{pump}} / (2\pi) \;\approx\; 1.65 \,\text{MHz}$$

**The pump drives the apparatus at ~1 MHz Rabi frequency.** This is the rate at which population inversion is sustained between the K=4 and K=5 levels.

## Why cardinal alignment

The Great Pyramid is aligned to true north with ~3 arc-minute precision. This precision is at the limit of unaided ancient observation and represents a substantial engineering cost.

**Framework reading:** the geomagnetic field at Giza points toward magnetic north (declination varies slowly over millennia). The apparatus's pump axis must align with the pump direction to first order.

The pump axis is *not* exactly cardinal:

- The geomagnetic dipole axis tilts ~10° from Earth's rotation axis
- Local declination at Giza in 2500 BCE is poorly constrained; archaeomagnetic data for Egypt in the third millennium BCE are sparse, and declination can change by several degrees per century
- True cardinal alignment is therefore *slightly* offset from optimal pump alignment

But:

- Cardinal alignment is **stable** across geological timescales (Earth's rotation axis precesses slowly)
- Magnetic alignment **drifts** on shorter timescales (centuries to millennia)

The apparatus's choice of true cardinal alignment (not magnetic alignment) is consistent with a **long-baseline** design — accepting a small first-order misalignment from the pump in exchange for centuries-stable cardinal alignment.

The 3 arc-minute precision overdelivers for static-pump alignment (where 1° misalignment gives a $\cos^2$ loss of only ~0.015%) but exactly matches the requirement for **coherent phase tracking** of the geomagnetic field's daily AC component over years of operation.

## Cardinal alignment and pump coherence

For the apparatus to maintain coherence across long times (years to centuries), the alignment precision must keep the pump phase coherent over the coherence time $T_2$.

Coherence loss from misalignment: $\delta T_2 \sim T_2 \cdot \sin^2(\delta\theta)$ where $\delta\theta$ is the alignment error.

For $\delta\theta = 3$ arc-minutes $\approx 0.00087$ rad: $\sin^2(\delta\theta) \approx 7.6 \times 10^{-7}$. Coherence loss per second: negligible. The apparatus's alignment is precise enough to maintain coherence over geological timescales.

## Pump-axis lengths

The apparatus's pump axis is the apex-to-base vertical line. From the apex (146.7 m) down to the King's Chamber (~43 m above the base), the pump travels ~103 m through limestone bulk.

At static field, no exponential attenuation. The flux density at the KC is essentially the bulk-concentrated apex value:

$$B_{\text{KC}} \;\approx\; \mathcal G_{\text{apex}} \cdot B_0 \;\approx\; 2.62 \cdot 45\,\mu\text{T} \;\approx\; 118\,\mu\text{T}$$

For AC components, the limestone's dielectric loss provides modest attenuation, but at low frequencies (sub-kHz, the relevant range for geomagnetic AC), the attenuation is small. The pump arrives at the KC essentially undiminished.
