## The claim

The apparatus operates **deep in the strong-coupling regime** of cavity QED — by approximately seven orders of magnitude over threshold:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{g\sqrt{N}}{\gamma_{\text{cav}}} \;\approx\; 4 \times 10^7\;}$$

with collective coupling $g\sqrt{N} \approx 2.6 \times 10^6$ rad/s vs. cavity decay rate $\gamma_{\text{cav}} \approx 7 \times 10^{-2}$ rad/s. The apparatus is **over-pumped** by this margin, ensuring robust lasing-like operation under all plausible loss budgets.

For comparison, laboratory atomic cavity-QED systems typically achieve $g\sqrt{N}/\gamma \sim 10$-$100$. The apparatus exceeds typical strong-coupling regimes by $\sim 5$-$6$ orders of magnitude, driven by the enormous $N$-count provided by the granite cavity walls.

## The strong-coupling criterion

In cavity QED, **strong coupling** is the regime where the coherent atom-cavity coupling rate exceeds all decay rates:

$$g\sqrt{N} \;>\; \max(\gamma_{\text{cav}}, \gamma_{\text{atom}})$$

with:

- $g$: single-atom-cavity coupling rate (vacuum Rabi splitting per atom)
- $N$: number of coherent atoms in the cavity mode
- $\gamma_{\text{cav}}$: cavity field decay rate ($\omega/Q$)
- $\gamma_{\text{atom}}$: atomic spontaneous emission rate

In strong coupling, the system exhibits **coherent oscillation** between atom and cavity states, sustained over many oscillation periods. The apparatus's operation requires strong coupling to maintain the coherent vertex-cavity dynamics that produce the portal channel.

## The $N$-count

The number of coherent lepton magnetic moments in the cavity walls:

$$N \;=\; n_{\text{atom}} \cdot V_{\text{walls}}$$

with $n_{\text{atom}}$ = atomic density in granite ≈ $2.66 \times 10^{28}$ atoms/m³. For $V_{\text{walls}} \approx 60$ m³:

$$N \;=\; 2.66 \times 10^{28} \cdot 60 \;\approx\; 1.6 \times 10^{30}$$

This is the **maximum possible** $N$-count, assuming all electrons in the granite walls couple coherently. In practice, only those electrons whose magnetic moments are aligned with the geomagnetic field axis contribute coherently. The realistic $N$ is likely a fraction of this maximum, but as long as the fraction exceeds $\sim 10^{-14}$, the apparatus remains in strong coupling (by the calculation below).

## Single-atom coupling $g$

The single-atom-cavity coupling for a magnetic dipole interaction is:

$$g \;=\; \frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}\, B_{\text{vac}}}{\hbar}$$

with $B_{\text{vac}}$ the vacuum magnetic field amplitude of the cavity mode:

$$B_{\text{vac}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_0 \mu_0}{2 V_{KC}}}$$

For the KC mode at $\omega_0 = 2\pi \cdot 110$ Hz:

$$B_{\text{vac}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 110 \cdot 4\pi \times 10^{-7}}{2 \cdot 80.25}} \;\approx\; 2.4 \times 10^{-20}\ \text{T}$$

The single-atom coupling:

$$g \;=\; \frac{9.29 \times 10^{-24} \cdot 2.4 \times 10^{-20}}{1.055 \times 10^{-34}} \;\approx\; 2.1 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{rad/s}$$

Tiny on a per-atom basis. The strong coupling comes entirely from the **collective enhancement** $g\sqrt{N}$.

## Collective coupling $g\sqrt{N}$

$$g\sqrt{N} \;=\; 2.1 \times 10^{-9} \cdot \sqrt{1.6 \times 10^{30}} \;=\; 2.1 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 1.26 \times 10^{15} \;\approx\; 2.65 \times 10^6\ \text{rad/s}$$

The collective coupling rate is **2.65 MHz**, dramatically enhanced over the single-atom rate by the factor $\sqrt{N} \approx 10^{15}$.

## Cavity decay rate $\gamma_{\text{cav}}$

The cavity field decay rate is:

$$\gamma_{\text{cav}} \;=\; \frac{\omega_0}{Q} \;=\; \frac{2\pi \cdot 110}{10^4} \;\approx\; 7 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{rad/s}$$

Note this is much slower than the cavity mode frequency itself ($691$ rad/s) — the high-Q cavity sustains modes for $\sim Q$ oscillation periods before they decay.

## Strong coupling ratio

$$\frac{g\sqrt{N}}{\gamma_{\text{cav}}} \;=\; \frac{2.65 \times 10^6}{7 \times 10^{-2}} \;\approx\; 3.8 \times 10^7$$

**Strong coupling factor: ~$4 \times 10^7$.** The apparatus exceeds strong-coupling threshold by 7-8 orders of magnitude.

## Why such a huge margin

The apparatus's strong-coupling margin is enormous because of the **scale** of the structure:

- $N \sim 10^{30}$ — set by the cavity walls' mass (millions of tons of granite)
- $Q \sim 10^4$ — set by granite's mechanical quality factor (~$10^4$ at audio)
- $V_{KC} \sim 80$ m³ — set by chamber size

A laboratory atomic-cavity experiment has $N \sim 10^4$-$10^6$ atoms in a $\sim$mm³ cavity. The pyramid has $N$ larger by $\sim 10^{24}$, and the cavity larger by $\sim 10^9$, with $Q$ comparable. The $\sqrt{N}$ enhancement scales as $10^{12}$ over typical labs.

The apparatus is a **megaton-scale cavity-QED experiment**, fundamentally different from laboratory atomic systems by virtue of pure scale.

## Comparison to laser threshold

The apparatus's analogue of laser-threshold is:

$$\Omega_{\text{pump}} \;>\; \gamma_{\text{cav}}$$

i.e., the pump rate exceeds the cavity decay rate. For the apparatus:

$$\Omega_{\text{pump}} / \gamma_{\text{cav}} \;=\; 10^7 / (7 \times 10^{-2}) \;\approx\; 1.4 \times 10^8$$

The pump is **$10^8$ above lasing threshold**. The apparatus is so far above threshold that any nonlinear saturation effects would dominate the steady-state behavior — the apparatus is in **strongly saturated lasing-like operation**.

## Population inversion

The framework's vertex coupling $\alpha_G \sim 10^{-45}$ gives spontaneous decay rates of:

$$\Gamma_{\text{spont}} \sim \alpha_G \cdot \omega \;\sim\; 10^{-45} \cdot \omega$$

For any reasonable transition frequency, $\Gamma_{\text{spont}}$ is negligible compared to $\Omega_{\text{pump}}$. Population inversion in the upper $K = 5$ state is trivially maintained — pumping completely dominates spontaneous decay.

This is the apparatus's operational regime: a heavily-pumped, strongly-coupled cavity with population fully inverted by the geomagnetic pump.
