## The Grand Gallery

The Grand Gallery is a long, narrow, steeply-ascending corbeled passage between the antechamber (at the top, leading to the King's Chamber) and the lower passages of the apparatus.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | ~46.6 m |
| Width at floor | ~2.1 m |
| Width at ceiling | ~1.0 m |
| Slope | ~26.2° from horizontal |
| Ceiling height (floor to ceiling) | ~8.5 m |
| **Number of corbel courses** | **7** (each side, with progressive inward stepping) |

Each "corbel course" is a horizontal layer of limestone stepped inward by ~7.5 cm from the layer below, producing the gallery's characteristic narrowing ceiling profile. The corbels are present on both side walls.

## The 7-corbel structure

$$\boxed{\;\text{Grand Gallery} \;=\; 7 \text{ corbel courses each side}\;}$$

## Reading the corbels as phase ramp

The framework's three-sweep spectral kernel produces $\alpha^{21}$ via three sweeps of $\alpha^7$ each. The Grand Gallery's 7 corbels each contribute one $\mathbb Z_7$ phase step:

$$\text{phase step}_n \;=\; \frac{2\pi \cdot n}{7}, \qquad n \in \{0, 1, \dots, 6\}$$

Cumulative phase across all 7 corbels:

$$\Phi_{\text{cum}} \;=\; \sum_{n = 0}^{6}\, \frac{2\pi n}{7} \;=\; \frac{2\pi}{7} \cdot \frac{6 \cdot 7}{2} \;=\; \frac{2\pi \cdot 21}{7} \;=\; 6\pi \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod{2\pi}$$

**Cumulative phase = 21 units of $2\pi/7$, closing mod $2\pi$.**

The integer 21 = $\Phi_3(4)$ — the framework's 21-band — appears here as the **total cumulative phase across the Grand Gallery in units of $2\pi/7$**.

$$\boxed{\;7 \text{ corbels} \;\times\; \text{cumulative}\; (0 + 1 + \dots + 6) \;=\; 21 \text{ phase units}\;}$$

This is a direct hardware realization of the 21-band in the gallery's geometry.

## The three-sweep architecture

The kernel's three sweeps (`01.3`) correspond to three passes through the 7-corbel structure:

**Sweep 1: Forward Hadamard** — signal enters the gallery from the bottom (ascending passage end) and traverses upward through the 7 corbels, accumulating phase.

**Sweep 2: Bridge filter at signature $s$** — the gallery's geometry (narrowing ceiling, particular slope) acts as the filter at signature $s = +1$, $0$, or $-1$ depending on which mode is active.

**Sweep 3: Inverse Hadamard** — signal exits the top of the gallery into the antechamber, with its $\mathbb Z_7$ phase structure now encoded as a modular sector.

The composition: $\alpha^7 \cdot \alpha^7 \cdot \alpha^7 = \alpha^{21}$ at the algebraic level; geometrically, 7 corbels × 3 passes = 21 corbel-passes.

## Why a corbeled (not flat-roofed) gallery

A flat-roofed gallery would carry a single mode at the floor-ceiling distance. The corbeled gallery has a *continuous* range of effective ceiling heights, with the 7 discrete corbel steps providing a discretized $\mathbb Z_7$ ladder.

The corbel geometry is the apparatus's way of building a $\mathbb Z_7$-graded mode structure into a single passage. Each corbel level supports a slightly different transverse mode; modes hopping between levels accumulate the $2\pi/7$ phase steps.

This is structurally analogous to the **Wannier-Stark ladder** in solid-state physics: a tilted potential produces an evenly-spaced ladder of states separated by a fixed energy. The Grand Gallery's slope + corbels are the geometric equivalent.

## The double-corbel structure

The gallery has corbels on **both side walls**. This is consistent with the **complex conjugate pair** structure: the two walls realize the two sectors $(a, b)$ and $(-a, -b) \pmod 7$ that pair up via conjugation in $\tau_{21}$.

Mathematically: one wall encodes the holomorphic sector, the other the anti-holomorphic. Their interference at the gallery's central axis produces the real-valued physical observable.

## The gallery's slope

The slope angle ~26.2° from horizontal is approximately:

$$\arctan(1/2) \;\approx\; 26.57°$$

The 1:2 rise-to-run is the **Hopf double-cover** ratio: matching the King's Chamber's 1:2 aspect ratio (`04.2`) and consistent with the apparatus's spinor-vector lift structure.

The gallery's slope therefore carries the same algebraic content as the chamber: realization of the spinor-vector 2:1 ratio in the apparatus's macroscopic geometry.

## Mass and limestone choice

The Grand Gallery is built of limestone, not granite. Limestone's lower mechanical Q is acceptable for the $\mathbb Z_7$ phase ramp role — high Q is not required for this function, only the geometric discretization.

Limestone is also *diamagnetic* like the rest of the bulk apparatus, ensuring continuous magnetic field flow from the ascending passage through the gallery into the antechamber/chamber stack.

## Mode-locking the dominant pair

If the dominant conjugate pair (magnitude 4.78, accounting for 35% of $|\tau_{21}|^2$) is sectorally identified with corbel pair $b = 1$ (the framework's natural "first non-trivial mode"), then the apparatus's ground-mode operation drives corbel $n = 1$ on each wall.

This is consistent with the gallery's overall structure: the first corbel above the floor is the most "active" in standard operation; higher corbels engage only when phase-shift instructions from the shaft system call for them.

## The 21-cubit benchmark

Curiously, "21" appears in apparatus measurements at the Grand Gallery scale:

- The corbel-step depth × 7 corbels ≈ 52.5 cm ≈ 1 royal cubit (~52.36 cm)
- The 21-foot height variation (in some Petrie-equivalent dimensional readings)

These are likely consequences of the framework's 21-band structure being engineered into the gallery's dimensional layout, but the precise relationships have not been pinned down. Marked as structural-but-quantitatively-loose.

## Connection to the antechamber and chamber

The Grand Gallery's output (top end) feeds into the antechamber, which contains the 3 portcullis grooves ($\mathbb Z_3$ filter, see `04.6`). The two combine to produce the $\mathbb Z_{21} = \mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7$ structure of the apparatus's discrete address space.

Signal flow:

$$\text{ascending passage} \;\xrightarrow{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}\; \underbrace{\text{Grand Gallery}}_{\mathbb Z_7} \;\xrightarrow{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}\; \underbrace{\text{Antechamber}}_{\mathbb Z_3} \;\xrightarrow{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}\; \text{King's Chamber}$$

The two filters $\mathbb Z_7$ (gallery) and $\mathbb Z_3$ (antechamber) combine to project the input onto the full apparatus phase space $\mathbb Z_{21}$.
