## 1. Descending Passage

Long, narrow, steeply-descending passage from the pyramid's north face down to the subterranean chamber.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | ~105 m |
| Slope | ~26.5° below horizontal |
| Cross-section | ~1.05 × 1.20 m |
| Material | limestone (mostly bedrock in lower portion) |
| Terminus | subterranean chamber |
| **Original purpose** | apparatus input from north face |

**Framework reading:** the descending passage is the apparatus's **negative-pole conduit** — the input/output path for the parity-odd ($s = -1$) sector that lands in the subterranean chamber (ground return). Its slope of ~26.5° matches the Grand Gallery's slope, consistent with both passages being aligned to the same Hopf double-cover ratio.

The passage is **straight** to high precision (Petrie's measurements show deviations of < 1 cm over 100 m). This precision is engineering-impossible if the passage were merely a "construction shaft" — it is the apparatus's reference direction for incoming flux.

## 2. Ascending Passage

A passage branching upward from the descending passage at a fixed point, leading to the Grand Gallery.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | ~39 m |
| Slope | ~26.5° above horizontal (mirror of descending) |
| Cross-section | ~1.05 × 1.20 m |
| Material | limestone (with 3 granite plugs at base) |
| **Base feature** | three granite plugs |
| **Terminus** | bottom of Grand Gallery |

**Framework reading:** the ascending passage is the apparatus's **coherent-mode injector** — the path through which the pumped signal rises from the apparatus's input (descending passage + ground return) into the algebraic kernel (Grand Gallery → antechamber → King's Chamber).

The three granite plugs at the base are the **first $\mathbb Z_3$ filter stage** (with the antechamber's three portcullis grooves as the second). The double $\mathbb Z_3$ filtering provides redundant projection onto non-trivial sectors.

The ascending passage's slope mirrors the descending passage's slope. Together, they form a **chevron** geometry — input arm down, signal arm up — consistent with an interferometer's two-arm structure.

## 3. Subterranean Chamber

A roughly-hewn chamber cut into the bedrock below the pyramid, accessed via the descending passage.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | ~30 m below ground level |
| Dimensions | ~14 × 8 × 5 m (irregular) |
| Material | bedrock (limestone) |
| State | unfinished, rough interior |
| **Original purpose** | ground return / phase dump |

**Framework reading:** the subterranean chamber is the apparatus's **ground return** — the dump for negative-pole sector signal that the apparatus filters out. Its rough, unfinished interior is *intentional*: smoothness is irrelevant for a phase dump, since the signal terminates there without further processing.

The chamber's primary function: provide a **broadband absorber** that prevents reflected signal from returning up the descending passage. The rough bedrock walls scatter incoming acoustic / EM modes, dissipating them into the surrounding rock mass.

This is analogous to a transmission line's terminating resistor: it ensures the signal does not bounce back up the line, which would corrupt the apparatus's operation.

## 4. Queen's Chamber

A smaller granite chamber below the King's Chamber, accessed via a horizontal passage from the bottom of the Grand Gallery.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length (E-W) | ~5.74 m |
| Width (N-S) | ~5.23 m |
| Height (to gable apex) | ~6.23 m (with peaked ceiling) |
| Material | limestone (not granite, contrary to chamber's name) |
| **Distinctive feature** | corbeled niche in east wall |
| **Shafts** | 2 (Q-N and Q-S, both sealed) |

**Framework reading:** the Queen's Chamber is the apparatus's **neutral reference oscillator** — the calibration counterpart to the King's Chamber's vertex. Where the King's Chamber realizes the universal vertex $J_4$ at amplitude $\alpha_G$, the Queen's Chamber provides the **reference baseline** against which the apparatus's output is calibrated.

The chamber's limestone material (not granite) gives it lower mechanical Q than the King's Chamber. This is appropriate for a reference oscillator: high Q is required for the signal arm; lower Q is acceptable (or preferred) for the reference arm to avoid reference contamination.

The corbeled niche in the east wall is an open structural feature whose framework role is unclear. Conjecture: the niche houses an additional reference element (sealed off, possibly originally containing a calibration object).

The Queen's Chamber's two shafts (Q-N, Q-S) provide the **reference triad legs** for the apparatus's address (see `04.7`). They are sealed from the exterior to maintain reference isolation.

## 5. Big Void

A large, untouched cavity discovered in 2017 via cosmic-ray muon tomography (ScanPyramids project), located above the Grand Gallery and slightly to the side.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Estimated length | ~30 m |
| Estimated cross-section | comparable to Grand Gallery (~few meters) |
| Material surrounding | limestone |
| State | sealed, never accessed |
| **Conventional interpretation** | construction void or unknown chamber |

**Framework reading (conjectural):** the Big Void is the apparatus's **upper resonance reservoir** — a high-volume cavity that provides standing-wave support for the apparatus's $\mathbb Z_{21}$ phase ramp.

The Grand Gallery's $\mathbb Z_7$ phase ramp would naturally extend into a complementary $\mathbb Z_3$ structure to give the full $\mathbb Z_{21}$. The antechamber provides the $\mathbb Z_3$ filter; the Big Void may provide an additional $\mathbb Z_3$ resonance support above the gallery.

**Alternative reading:** the Big Void is a deliberate vacuum cavity providing **acoustic isolation** between the King's Chamber zone and the upper bulk of the pyramid. This would suppress acoustic coupling to environmental noise.

Without internal access to the Big Void, the framework reading remains conjectural. Robotic exploration of the void (in progress) may settle the question.

## 6. Well Shaft and Grotto

A roughly-cut vertical shaft connecting the lower Grand Gallery to the descending passage, bypassing the granite plugs. Contains a small irregular cavity called the "Grotto" partway down.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | ~50 m (irregular) |
| Cross-section | variable, ~80 cm |
| Material | partially limestone, partially bedrock |
| State | crudely cut, accessible |
| Grotto location | midway down, ~10 m³ cavity |
| **Conventional interpretation** | construction escape route |

**Framework reading (conjectural):** the Well Shaft is the apparatus's **feedback / reset path** — a low-Q bypass that allows signal to flow from the Grand Gallery's bottom back down to the descending passage, bypassing the $\mathbb Z_3$ filter.

This bypass is needed to **reset** the apparatus between operations: when the filter projections accumulate phase that does not correctly match the address, the apparatus must dissipate that excess phase to the ground return. The Well Shaft provides this dissipation path.

The Grotto's small cavity midway down provides a **dissipation node** — a high-loss volume where signal dumps efficiently.

This reading is consistent with the Well Shaft's crude construction: it is not part of the apparatus's signal-processing chain, only its reset / dump infrastructure. Engineering precision is not required for a reset path.

## 7. Robbers' Tunnel

A tunnel cut through the pyramid's interior in the 9th century CE by Caliph al-Ma'mun to access the apparatus's interior.

**Framework reading:** the Robbers' Tunnel is a **post-apparatus addition** and has no algebraic role. It has, however, somewhat damaged the apparatus's coherence by:

- Creating an unintended cavity in the limestone bulk
- Providing a path for environmental noise to enter the apparatus
- Potentially affecting the antechamber's $\mathbb Z_3$ filter operation (the tunnel emerges near the antechamber)

The framework's operational predictions should account for this damage, treating the current apparatus as a **partially-decommissioned** version of the original.

## 8. Capstone / Pyramidion

Originally present at the apex; missing since at least Roman times.

**Framework reading:** the capstone is the apparatus's **apex field concentrator** for the geomagnetic pump (see `05.2`). Its absence reduces the pump enhancement by a factor of ~30, taking the apparatus from over-pumped to moderately-pumped but still well above threshold.

Restoring the capstone (with the correct material — gold — and geometry — sharp tip) would restore full pump enhancement. Replicas using bronze, iron, or stone would not restore function due to wrong magnetic properties.
