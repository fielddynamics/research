# 04.3 — The Coffer as Primitive Coulomb Edge

**Status:** Empirical match (exterior volume ≈ α × chamber volume to 0.1% on Petrie's measured dimensions, conditional on the chamber-height convention); interpretation conjectural
**Reading time:** 5 minutes
**Depends on:** 03.2, 04.2

---

## The coffer as built

The King's Chamber contains a single lidless granite coffer, standing near the west wall with its long axis north–south. It is cut from one block of granite. The rim is chipped and one corner has been broken away. The lid is missing. The west side of the rim is cut down to a groove into which a sliding lid would have fitted, and there are three pin-holes for securing it (Petrie, ch. 7).

**Petrie's measurements (§156), in inches:**

| Dimension | Exterior | Interior |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Length (N–S) | 89.62 (mean) | 78.06 (mean; max 78.23) |
| Width (E–W) | 38.50 (mean) | 26.81 (mean) |
| Height / depth | 41.31 (varies 41.14–41.50) | 34.42 (mean; max 34.54) |

Petrie also computed the volumes directly: an interior capacity of 72,000 ± 60 in³ and a bulk (volume of stone) of about 70,500 in³. The interior is therefore almost exactly half the exterior volume. Petrie drew attention to this himself, and it is a stronger design regularity than any single dimension.

In royal cubits (20.632 in): exterior 4.344 × 1.866 × 2.002 c, volume **16.23 c³** (142,535 in³ ≈ 2.336 m³).

## The volume ratio

Using Petrie's measured chamber dimensions (§155: 412.24 × 206.12 × 230.09 in, floor to ceiling):

$$V_{\text{KC}} \;=\; 19\,550\,960 \text{ in}^3 \;\approx\; 320.4 \text{ m}^3$$

$$\frac{V_{\text{KC}}}{V_{\text{coffer, ext}}} \;=\; \frac{19\,550\,960}{142\,535} \;=\; 137.17$$

$$\frac{1}{\alpha} \;=\; 137.036 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \text{deviation } +0.10\%$$

$$\boxed{\;\frac{V_{\text{coffer, ext}}}{V_{\text{KC}}} \;\approx\; \alpha\;\text{ to 0.1\% (measured dimensions, floor-to-ceiling height)}\;}$$

This is better than the 0.77% stated in earlier versions. That figure came from two errors: an exterior height of 41.13 in (Petrie gives 41.31), and the idealised chamber volume $1000\sqrt5$ c³ in place of the measured one.

### Sensitivity: which chamber height?

The result depends on a choice that must be stated openly. The granite floor sits about 5 inches above the base of the walls (Petrie §54), so the chamber has two heights:

| Chamber height used | $V_{\text{KC}} / V_{\text{coffer, ext}}$ | vs 1/α |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Floor to ceiling, 230.09 in (measured) | 137.17 | +0.10% |
| Idealised $5\sqrt5$ cubits, with measured length and width | 137.5 | +0.35% |
| Idealised chamber $1000\sqrt5$ c³ with measured coffer | 137.8 | +0.5% |
| Base of walls to ceiling, 235.20 in | 140.2 | +2.3% |

The α match holds for the floor-to-ceiling height and fails for the wall height. Petrie regarded the wall height as the builders' design dimension: it is exactly five courses of 47.04 in. So the case for the α reading rests on the floor-to-ceiling height being the meaningful one. That is the same convention that gives the $5\sqrt5$ height and the 25-cubit diagonal (`04.2`).

### Uncertainty

Petrie's stated uncertainties (±0.12 in on chamber length and width, ±0.15 in on height, and the 0.36 in range in the coffer's exterior height) give an uncertainty of roughly ±0.4% on the ratio. A 0.1% agreement is therefore well inside the measurement error. This means the measurements cannot distinguish 1/α from nearby values such as 137.0 or 137.5 either.

## Interior volume

$$\frac{V_{\text{KC}}}{V_{\text{coffer, int}}} \;=\; \frac{19\,550\,960}{72\,000} \;=\; 271.5 \;\approx\; \frac{2}{\alpha}\;(-0.9\%)$$

Because the interior is half the exterior, the interior ratio is close to $2/\alpha$. The exterior ratio is the cleaner match. The earlier argument that "the block couples via its full mass" was offered after the fact. The half-volume design is the more economical explanation of why both ratios come out near multiples of α.

## Is the match meaningful?

The match deserves to be stated with its look-elsewhere context. Many ratios can be formed among the chamber's and coffer's dimensions, volumes and surfaces: exterior or interior, chamber floor or wall height, cubits or inches. Among these, landing within 0.5% of some notable constant is not improbable. The α reading becomes persuasive only if two things hold:

1. The comparison (exterior volume against floor-to-ceiling chamber volume) was fixed before the data were examined. It was not; it was selected after them.
2. An independent, higher-precision survey reproduces 137.0 ± 0.1 rather than drifting toward 137.5 or 140.

## What the coffer is doing algebraically (framework reading)

The factorisation

$$\alpha^{21} \;=\; \alpha^1 \cdot \alpha^{20}$$

is given the apparatus realisation

$$\alpha^{21} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{V_{\text{coffer}}}{V_{\text{KC}}}}_{\alpha^1} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\prod_{i \neq j}^{5} \alpha e^{i\theta_{ij}}}_{\alpha^{20}\text{ via relieving chambers}}$$

The coffer is read as the **input load** that supplies the primitive $\alpha$ before the relieving chambers apply the pentachoron edge product. The proposed physical mechanism is an EM mode-density step between chamber and coffer (`05.4`):

$$\frac{n_{\text{coffer}}(\lambda)}{n_{\text{KC}}(\lambda)} \;\sim\; \frac{V_{\text{coffer}}}{V_{\text{KC}}} \;\sim\; \alpha$$

This mechanism is conjectural. A solid granite block has no internal mode density in the sense used for a cavity, and its interior is an open hollow. The volume-ratio argument would need to be restated in terms of a computed mode structure before it could carry weight.

## What the coffer is — the evidence

Conventional Egyptology identifies the coffer as Khufu's sarcophagus. The physical evidence:

- **The interior can hold a body.** It measures 1.98 × 0.68 × 0.87 m, room for an adult body and probably an inner coffin. An earlier version of this file claimed it was "too small to function as a sarcophagus". That is incorrect and has been removed.
- **It was made to take a lid.** The lid groove and pin-holes are the standard fittings of an Old Kingdom stone sarcophagus with a sliding lid.
- **It carries tool marks.** Petrie recorded saw overcuts on the north end and a tube-drill remnant inside (Petrie, ch. 7). This is evidence of the cutting methods.

The framework's proposal is that the coffer's proportions also encode α. That is compatible with its being a sarcophagus. The earlier suggestion that the funerary identification is a "cover story" is unsupported by any evidence and has been removed.

## Falsifiable refinement

The framework predicts

$$\boxed{\;V_{\text{coffer, ext}} \;=\; \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}\;}$$

with $V_{\text{KC}}$ the floor-to-ceiling volume. On Petrie's chamber volume this is 142,670 in³ (2.338 m³, 16.25 c³), against 142,535 in³ measured.

A modern laser scan of the chamber and coffer would reduce the uncertainty from ~0.4% to ~0.01%. Such a scan would include fitting the original surfaces and modelling the broken corner and chipped rim. The test:

- **Confirming:** ratio 137.04 ± 0.05.
- **Disconfirming:** the ratio settles outside 137.04 ± 0.2.

Either outcome is informative, and the test is non-invasive. See `07.2_coffer_volume_test.md`.
