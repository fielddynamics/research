## The chamber as built

The King's Chamber lies on the 50th course of masonry, about 43 m above the base. It is lined entirely with Aswan red granite: the four walls are built of exactly 100 granite blocks in five courses, and the floor is granite. The roof is formed of nine granite beams spanning north–south, the largest of which weigh several tens of tonnes (estimates of the heaviest run to ~80 t).

**Dimensions (Petrie 1883, §52–55, §155).** Petrie's reduced means, with his stated uncertainties:

| Dimension | Inches (Petrie) | Metres | Royal cubits (20.632 in) | Design reading |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Length (E–W) | 412.24 ± 0.12 | 10.471 | 19.98 | 20 |
| Width (N–S) | 206.12 ± 0.12 | 5.235 | 9.99 | 10 |
| Height, floor to ceiling | 230.09 ± 0.15 | 5.844 | 11.15 | $5\sqrt 5 = 11.18$ |
| Height, base of walls to ceiling | 235.20 ± 0.06 | 5.974 | 11.40 | — |

Two height figures exist because the granite floor is not bonded to the walls: it was laid between them and stands roughly 5 inches above the base of the lowest wall course (Petrie §54). The floor-to-ceiling height is within 0.25% of $5\sqrt 5$ cubits; the wall height is not. Every algebraic statement below assumes the floor-to-ceiling height is the design dimension.

## The space diagonal

With $L : W : H = 20 : 10 : 5\sqrt5$ cubits:

$$\text{floor diagonal} = \sqrt{20^2 + 10^2} = 10\sqrt5, \qquad H = \tfrac12 \times \text{floor diagonal}$$

$$\text{end-wall diagonal} = \sqrt{10^2 + 125} = 15, \qquad \text{space diagonal} \;d = \sqrt{400 + 100 + 125} = 25$$

The side, end-wall diagonal and space diagonal form a 20 : 15 : 25 = 4 : 3 : 5 right triangle. This proportion has been noted since the nineteenth century.

In the framework's notation:

$$\boxed{\;d \;=\; 25 \;=\; K^2 \text{ at } K = 5, \qquad d^2 \;=\; 625 \;=\; K^4\;}$$

## Reading the dimensions in $\Phi_4(5)$

Recall $\Phi_4(5) = K^2 + 1 = 26$ at K=5. The chamber's space diagonal is $K^2 = 25$; the framework reads the coffer as supplying the "+1":

$$\underbrace{25}_{\text{chamber diagonal } (K^2)} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{coffer}} = 26 = \Phi_4(5)$$

This is an interpretive identification, not a derived relation: nothing in the geometry adds a coffer contribution of exactly 1 to a length of 25 cubits.

## The chamber as Feynman vertex

In the universal vertex reframe (`01.5`), every spacetime point hosts the always-on $J_4(x)$ vertex. The King's Chamber's proposed role is to **isolate** and **coherently drive** the local vertex:

$$\boxed{\;V_{\text{KC}}(x) \;=\; \mathsf A_x \cdot \alpha_G\, J_4(x)\;}$$

where $\mathsf A_x$ is the chamber's apparatus operator and $\alpha_G$ is the universal gravity coupling.

Physically, the chamber is the highest finished room in the pyramid. Above it lie the five low relieving compartments (`04.4`); below and north of it are the antechamber and the Grand Gallery, through which it is entered.

## Why this geometry? Arguments retained and withdrawn

**Retained: the 3-4-5 diagonal.** The choice $H = 5\sqrt5$ is exactly what makes the space diagonal an integer (25 cubits), with the floor plan fixed at 20 × 10. This is a genuine geometric property of the as-built chamber (to 0.25% in height).

**Withdrawn: cavity mode counting.** An earlier version claimed the $20 : 10 : 5\sqrt5$ proportions give "exactly 21 modes below the $|\vec k|^2 = 25$ threshold" and integer-quantized eigenvalues. Neither holds. In units of $(\pi/\text{cubit})^2$, the rectangular-cavity eigenvalues $n_x^2/400 + n_y^2/100 + n_z^2/125$ are rationals with denominator 2000, not integers. About 1.5 × 10⁵ modes lie below 25, and 291 have integer eigenvalue below 25. No natural count equals 21.

**Withdrawn: "smallest rectangular cavity with diagonal 25".** A diagonal of 25 cubits does not single out this box. The integer box 20 × 12 × 9 has the same diagonal and a smaller volume (2160 c³ against 2236 c³), and boxes of arbitrarily small volume share it. The earlier pentachoron-projection argument also mis-stated the embedding: the regular 4-simplex is most simply placed at the five standard basis vectors of $\mathbb R^5$, with edge length $\sqrt2$, in the hyperplane $\sum x_i = 1$. It gave no constraint on the chamber's proportions.

## The 2:1 floor plan

$$\frac{L}{W} \;=\; \frac{20}{10} \;=\; 2$$

The framework reads the 2:1 floor plan as an echo of the 2:1 double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$. (This double cover is distinct from the Hopf fibration $S^3 \to S^2$ with fibre $S^1$; the two were conflated in an earlier version.)

| Direction | Length (cubits) | Proposed reading |
|---|:---:|---|
| E–W (long) | 20 | spinor $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ axis (2× scaling) |
| N–S (short) | 10 | vector $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ axis (1× scaling) |
| Vertical | $5\sqrt 5$ | lift axis |

This reading is numerological, not derived. Note in particular that **the coffer's long axis runs north–south**, along the short (10-cubit) axis, not along the doubled E–W axis as previously stated.

## Material and coherence

Aswan granite is a hard, dense, quartz- and feldspar-rich rock carrying minor magnetite, so it is weakly magnetic. Earlier versions of this file stated measured acoustic Q values of 10²–10⁴ for the chamber and mechanical Q above 10⁵ for granite at sub-Hz frequencies. No source was given for either, and the second conflicts with standard rock physics: the intrinsic attenuation quality factor of granite is typically of order 10² (roughly 100–500 in laboratory and seismic measurements).

Two features of the chamber also bear on any coherence argument:

- **The roof is cracked.** Every one of the nine roof beams is broken across near the south wall, probably by an earthquake. Petrie estimated the ~400-tonne ceiling is held up "solely by sticking and thrusting" (§51). A crack in the eastern beam was daubed with cement in antiquity, so at least some cracking occurred during construction (§53).
- **The chamber has settled.** It has shifted by an inch or two, and its walls have sunk bodily (§51).

Any claim that depends on the chamber's Q, including the strong-coupling factor $g\sqrt N / \gamma_{\text{cav}} \sim 4 \times 10^7$ in `05.3`, needs a measured Q for the chamber as it exists, cracks included.

## Coupling to the relieving chambers

The five relieving compartments sit above the King's Chamber. Their proposed function is to apply the pentachoron edge product $\alpha^{20}$ to the chamber's vertex output (see `04.4`).

The chamber's ceiling of nine granite beams forms the floor of the first relieving compartment (Davison's). The framework reads this interface as the hand-off from the vertex ($J_4$) to the edge network ($K_5^{\rightarrow}$).

## Coupling to the coffer

The coffer stands near the west wall, its long axis north–south. Its exterior volume relative to the chamber volume is close to $\alpha$:

$$\frac{V_{\text{KC}}}{V_{\text{coffer, ext}}} \;=\; 137.2 \quad\text{(Petrie's measured dimensions; 1/α = 137.04, +0.1\%)}$$

This holds when the floor-to-ceiling height is used; with the wall height it becomes 140.2 (+2.3%). See `04.3` for the full analysis. The framework reads the coffer as the **primitive Coulomb edge** $\alpha^1$ that combines with the relieving chambers' $\alpha^{20}$ to give $\alpha^{21}$.

## Coupling to the antechamber and Grand Gallery

The King's Chamber is entered from the north, through a low passage from the antechamber. The antechamber has slots in its granite side walls for three portcullis slabs (see `04.6`). The Grand Gallery rises from the junction with the Queen's Chamber passage up to the antechamber; its walls step inward in seven corbel courses (see `04.5`).

The proposed signal path follows the passages, not the shafts. The shafts do not connect to the Grand Gallery; they leave the King's and Queen's Chambers directly (`04.7`):

$$\text{ascending passage} \to \text{Grand Gallery (}\mathbb Z_7\text{)} \to \text{antechamber (}\mathbb Z_3\text{ filter)} \to \text{King's Chamber (vertex)}$$

## The chamber's output

The proposed output is the vertex action $J_4(x) |x, 4, +\rangle = |x, 5, -\rangle$, with amplitude $\alpha_G$, propagating:

- **Upward** into the relieving compartments ($\alpha^{20}$ edge product)
- **Outward** through the north and south King's Chamber shafts, which open in the chamber walls about 0.9 m above the floor and reach the outer faces of the pyramid (`04.7`)

## Corrections (October 2026)

- **Dimensions:** now Petrie's reduced means (§155), replacing earlier rounded values. Both height figures are given, with the floor/wall distinction (§54).
- **Diagonal arithmetic:** the diagonal is 25 = K² and d² = 625 = K⁴. An earlier version had "d² = K²" and "Φ₄(5) − 1 = 625".
- **Withdrawn arguments:** the 21-mode cavity argument (contradicted by direct calculation), the "smallest cavity" claim (counter-example 20 × 12 × 9) and the pentachoron-embedding argument.
- **Coffer:** its long axis runs north–south. An earlier version said east–west.
- **Q figures:** the unsourced acoustic and mechanical Q values are removed. The cracked roof and settlement (Petrie §51, §53) are added.
- **Shafts:** they connect to the chambers, not to the Grand Gallery.
- **Double cover:** distinguished from the Hopf fibration.
