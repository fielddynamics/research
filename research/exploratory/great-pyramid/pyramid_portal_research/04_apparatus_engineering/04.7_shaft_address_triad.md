## The four shafts as built

The Great Pyramid has four narrow, roughly square shafts, two leaving the King's Chamber and two leaving the Queen's Chamber, one of each pair running north and one south. All four lie close to the north–south meridian plane, sharing the pyramid's alignment to true (geographic) north. In each case the shaft runs horizontally for about 2 m before turning upward.

| Shaft | Mouth in chamber | Rise angle | Length | Upper end |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| K-N (King's, north) | north wall, ~0.9 m above floor; ~23 × 22 cm | mean ~31–32° (30°43′–32°04′); several bends, jogging west around the Grand Gallery | ~71 m | opens on the north face |
| K-S (King's, south) | south wall, ~0.9 m above floor, facing K-N; ~22 × 22 cm | ~45° (44°26′–45°30′) | ~53 m | opens on the south face, near the 101st course |
| Q-N (Queen's, north) | north wall; **concealed behind the wall face until 1872** | ~37½–39° (Petrie mean 37°28′); deflects ~45° to pass the Grand Gallery | ~73 m (to its blocking) | blocked by a limestone slab; no exterior exit known |
| Q-S (Queen's, south) | south wall; **concealed behind the wall face until 1872** | ~38½–39½° (Petrie mean 38°28′) | ~60–65 m to the first blocking slab | blocked by a limestone slab with two copper pins; a second slab lies ~18 cm beyond it; no exterior exit known |

Angles are quoted as ranges because the shafts are not perfectly straight and surveys differ by up to a degree or so. Petrie's figures and later robotic surveys disagree at that level, and figures of 32½° (K-N) and 39½° (Q-N, Q-S) are also widely cited.

### Exploration history

- **1837:** Howard Vyse cleared the King's Chamber shafts to the outside. Air flowed through them at once, which is the origin of the name "air shafts".
- **1872:** Waynman Dixon found the Queen's Chamber shafts by probing a crack in the chamber's south wall. The shafts had been cut to within a few inches of the chamber, leaving the final skin of the wall intact, so **they never opened into the chamber**. Dixon recovered three objects from the northern shaft: a small bronze hook, a fragment of cedar-like wood and a small stone ball.
- **1993:** Rudolf Gantenbrink's robot Upuaut-2 climbed the southern Queen's shaft about 60–65 m and found a limestone slab with two copper pins (the "door").
- **2002:** The Pyramid Rover drilled through the southern slab and found a small space and a second slab behind it. A similar blocking slab was found in the northern Queen's shaft.
- **2010–11:** The Djedi robot imaged the space behind the southern slab. The copper pins pass through the slab and end in loops on its back face, and there are red-ochre markings on the floor, possibly masons' marks or numerals.

### Interpretation in Egyptology

Ventilation is no longer the favoured explanation. The Queen's shafts never opened at either end, and no other pyramid has comparable shafts. The most widely held view is religious: the shafts were symbolic routes for the king's spirit toward particular stars, the circumpolar stars to the north and Orion (Osiris) to the south (Badawy and Trimble, 1964). The stellar targets proposed at ~2500 BCE depend on the exact angles and on the construction date. The alignments are proposals, not established facts.

## Framework reading: the mirror-address triad

The framework reading is that the shafts are the apparatus's **mirror-address triad legs**. Their angles, lengths and chirality form the discrete address that the mirror selector $\mathcal M(U, V)$ uses to identify the remote apparatus. This reading does not depend on rejecting the conventional religious interpretation; the two are offered at different levels.

## The triad address

The mirror selector $\mathcal M(U, V)$ requires (`03.3`, `06.3`):

1. **Direction triad** — three vectors $\vec n_1, \vec n_2, \vec n_3$ forming a frame in $\mathbb R^3$
2. **Chirality** — orientation of the triad ($\chi \in \{+1, -1\}$)
3. **Phase** — accumulated triad phase $\Theta \in U(1)$

**Proposed apparatus realisation:**

- **K-N and K-S shafts:** two of the three direction-triad vectors. The third is the vertical apex axis.
- **Q-N and Q-S shafts:** the reference, or calibration, legs.
- **K versus Q:** the chirality.
- **Shaft lengths:** the phase.

## The shafts encode a Gram matrix

The Gram matrix of the K-shaft pair plus the apex axis:

$$G_K \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & g_{K\!N, K\!S} & g_{K\!N, \text{apex}} \\ g_{K\!S, K\!N} & 1 & g_{K\!S, \text{apex}} \\ g_{\text{apex}, K\!N} & g_{\text{apex}, K\!S} & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad g_{ij} = \cos\theta_{ij}$$

Taking the upper (outer) sections as straight, with K-N rising at ≈ 32° to the north, K-S at ≈ 45° to the south and the apex axis vertical:

- angle between K-N and K-S ≈ 180° − 32° − 45° = 103°, so $g_{K\!N,K\!S} \approx -0.22$
- $g_{K\!N,\text{apex}} = \sin 32° \approx 0.53$
- $g_{K\!S,\text{apex}} = \sin 45° \approx 0.71$

The three vectors are coplanar, because all lie in the meridian plane. So this Gram matrix is singular ($\det G_K = 0$) and does not span a 3D frame. A non-degenerate frame needs the small east–west components of the shafts, which come from their westward jogs and curvature. Those are not well surveyed. The address reading therefore depends on details of the shaft geometry that have not yet been measured.

The Q-shafts give a second Gram matrix $G_Q$, read as the **reference** that calibrates the apparatus's identification of $G_K$.

## The chirality

The K-shafts and Q-shafts share the same north and south directions but start from chambers at different heights: the King's Chamber about 43 m above the base, the Queen's Chamber about 21 m. The framework defines the apparatus's **chirality** from the relative offset between them, in height and in material (granite-lined vertex cavity against limestone reference chamber):

$$\chi \;=\; \text{sign of (K-shaft Gram orientation)} - \text{sign of (Q-shaft Gram orientation)}$$

As noted above, the in-plane Gram matrices are degenerate. Their orientation, and hence $\chi$, is defined only once the out-of-plane components are included.

## The phase

Each shaft accumulates a phase along its length, $\theta_s = k_s L_s$. The proposed net address phase is

$$\Theta_K = \theta_{K\!N} - \theta_{K\!S}, \qquad \Theta_Q = \theta_{Q\!N} - \theta_{Q\!S}, \qquad \Theta = \Theta_K - \Theta_Q$$

With the lengths above (K-N ≈ 71 m, K-S ≈ 53 m, Q-N ≈ 73 m, Q-S ≈ 60–65 m to the first slab), $\Theta$ depends on which length is used for the blocked Queen's shafts: to the slab, or to the presumed design end.

## The address triplet

$$\boxed{\;\kappa_U \;=\; (G_K, \chi, \Theta) \;\in\; \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb R^3) \times \mathbb Z_2 \times U(1)\;}$$

The remote apparatus matches if $\kappa_V = (P^T G_K P, -\chi, \Theta + 2\pi N)$ for some permutation $P \in S_3$ and integer $N$ (`06.3`).

## Reading via the Hopf fibration

The four shafts plus the apex axis are read as defining a frame in $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, which lifts to $\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3$ through the double cover. The Hopf fibration $S^3 \to S^2$ then separates a direction on $S^2$ from a fibre phase $e^{i\Theta/2}$, read as the spin holonomy $g_e/2$:

- **K-N and K-S, with the vertical:** the $S^2$ direction (once out-of-plane components are included).
- **Q-N and Q-S:** the reference $S^2$.
- **K versus Q:** the $\mathbb Z_2$ deck.
- **Shaft lengths:** the $S^1$ fibre phase.

## The sealed Queen's shafts

The Queen's shafts are sealed **at both ends**: at the chamber end by the uncut final skin of the wall, and at the upper end by the limestone slabs. As far as is known, neither reaches the exterior. The King's shafts are open at both ends.

Framework reading: the Q-shafts are internal reference legs, isolated from the external environment to give a stable calibration, while the K-shafts are the external signal legs. This mirrors an **interferometer** layout:

| Arm | Function | Coupling |
|---|---|---|
| K-shafts | signal arm | open at both ends |
| Q-shafts | reference arm | sealed at both ends |
| K versus Q comparison | output | address phase |

The double sealing fits this reading better than the earlier version's "stoppers near the outer end" alone. The reading is interpretive. Note also that the Queen's Chamber itself is generally regarded as unfinished: its floor was left rough.

## The shafts and the geomagnetic field

An earlier version claimed the shafts follow Earth's geomagnetic dipole axis and that their inclinations match the local geomagnetic dip. The record does not support either claim:

- **Alignment is to true north.** The pyramid's sides deviate on average only about 3′38″ from the geographic cardinal directions, and the shafts lie in the same meridian plane. Magnetic north at Giza is currently about 4–5° east of true north. In the third millennium BCE it differed again, since the geomagnetic field direction drifts by degrees to tens of degrees over centuries to millennia.
- **The inclinations differ from one another.** The shafts rise at ~31–32°, ~38–39° (twice) and ~45°. Today's geomagnetic inclination at Giza is about 44°, and its value in 2500 BCE is not known to the precision the claim would need. One field direction cannot match three distinct shaft angles.

A coupling to the geomagnetic field (`05.1`) would therefore have to be argued from the field's N–S horizontal component, not from a match of directions.

## The 4-shaft to 3-vector reduction

Four unit vectors in 3D carry 4 × 2 = 8 directional degrees of freedom. Removing the 3 of an overall rotation leaves 5. The address needs 3 (one frame in $\mathrm{SO}(3)$); the remaining 2 are read as chirality (the $\mathbb Z_2$ deck) and the K-versus-Q calibration ratio. In practice, because all four shafts lie nearly in one plane, most of these degrees of freedom are close to zero. The reduction holds only for the small out-of-plane components.

## The shafts as cavity perturbations

The shafts are open channels of roughly 20 cm square cross-section connecting the chamber to the exterior (K) or to sealed dead ends (Q). Acoustically, each acts as a waveguide stub that perturbs the chamber's modes. The King's Chamber's two shafts are the relevant perturbations to its mode spectrum. The Queen's shafts affect only the Queen's Chamber, and only if their sealed inner skins transmit sound. Any mode-spectrum calculation (`07.1`) should model the King's Chamber with its two shafts as built: rectangular, bent and open to the outside.

## Status

- **Measured:** four shafts in two pairs. The King's pair is open at both ends; the Queen's pair is sealed at both ends. Geometry as tabulated above.
- **Structural (framework):** the shafts encode a Gram matrix, a chirality and a phase, matching the address triplet.
- **Open:** the in-plane geometry gives a degenerate Gram matrix. The address requires out-of-plane shaft components, which need a modern 3D survey of all four shafts.
- **Not supported:** alignment with the geomagnetic axis or dip, which is withdrawn.
