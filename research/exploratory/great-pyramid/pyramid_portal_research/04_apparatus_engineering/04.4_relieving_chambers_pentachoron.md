## The five chambers

Above the King's Chamber's flat roof, the Great Pyramid contains **five low compartments**, stacked one above another. Each is floored and roofed by a layer of massive granite beams, and the side walls are of limestone. Conventional Egyptology calls them "relieving chambers" or "chambers of construction" and reads them as engineering that spreads the load of the masonry above away from the King's Chamber ceiling.

The five compartments, from bottom to top:

| Compartment | Discovered | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1, Davison's Chamber | 1765, Nathaniel Davison, by a passage from the top of the Grand Gallery | roof of granite beams |
| 2, Wellington's Chamber | 1837, Howard Vyse and J. S. Perring, by blasting upward with gunpowder | roof of granite beams |
| 3, Nelson's Chamber | 1837, Vyse and Perring | roof of granite beams |
| 4, Lady Arbuthnot's Chamber | 1837, Vyse and Perring | roof of granite beams |
| 5, Campbell's Chamber | 1837, Vyse and Perring | gabled roof of limestone beams |

The compartments are low, roughly a metre of clear height, crawl spaces rather than rooms, and the granite beams between them are thick. The apex of Campbell's gabled roof is about 70 ft (≈ 21 m) above the King's Chamber floor, so the whole stack rises about 15 m above the King's Chamber ceiling. An earlier version of this file gave compartment floor heights of 0, 1.5, 3.5, 5.5 and 7.5 m. Those figures are unsupported and far too small, and have been removed. Individual compartment dimensions should be taken from Vyse and Perring (1840–42) or a modern survey.

Three features of the compartments bear directly on how they should be interpreted:

- **They are rough-finished.** Petrie describes the beams as having "rough-dressed surfaces, very fair and true so far as they go, but without any pretence to polish", with plaster daubed into the joints (§53). They are not finished to the standard of the King's Chamber.
- **They are damaged.** In every space above the chamber, beams on the south side are "either cracked across or torn out of the wall", and the great east and west limestone walls have sunk (Petrie §51).
- **They carry builders' graffiti.** The walls bear red-ochre quarry marks and work-gang names, including the name of Khufu written more than a dozen times. One example reads "The gang, The white crown of Khnum-Khufu is powerful". These are the principal inscriptional evidence dating the pyramid to Khufu's reign.

The top compartment (Campbell's) has a gabled limestone roof, the only non-flat ceiling in the stack.

## The framework reading

The 5 chambers are the **5 vertices of the K=5 pentachoron** (the 4-simplex). The 5 = $K + 1$ at K=4 (frame dim) = $K$ at K=5 (shell vertex count).

$$\boxed{\;5 \text{ relieving chambers} \;=\; 5 \text{ vertices of } K_5^{\rightarrow}\;}$$

The $K_5^{\rightarrow}$ graph has 5 vertices and 20 directed edges. The 20 directed edges are the **inter-chamber Coulomb couplings**:

$$\binom{5}{2} \cdot 2 \;=\; 20 \;=\; \Phi_3(4) - 1$$

with each undirected pair contributing 2 directed edges.

## The algebraic decomposition

Recall the framework's coupling factorization:

$$\alpha^{21} \;=\; \alpha^1 \cdot \alpha^{20}$$

- $\alpha^1$: coffer's primitive Coulomb edge (`04.3`)
- $\alpha^{20}$: pentachoron directed-edge product (this section)

The 20-edge product:

$$\mathcal A_{20} \;=\; \prod_{i \neq j}^{5}\, \alpha\, e^{i\theta_{ij}}$$

At phase closure $\sum_{ij} \theta_{ij} = 0 \pmod{2\pi}$:

$$\mathcal A_{20} \;=\; \alpha^{20}$$

The five chambers form the vertex set; the inter-chamber couplings form the directed edges; the phase closure is automatically satisfied by the antisymmetry of the inter-chamber spacings.

## Inter-chamber couplings

For chambers at heights $h_1, \dots, h_5$ above the King's Chamber roof, the directed-edge phase between chamber $i$ and chamber $j$ is:

$$\theta_{ij} \;=\; k \cdot (h_i - h_j)$$

for some wavenumber $k$ set by the apparatus's natural mode. Antisymmetric in $(i, j)$.

Sum over all directed edges:

$$\sum_{i \neq j} \theta_{ij} \;=\; k \sum_{i \neq j} (h_i - h_j) \;=\; 0$$

by antisymmetry. **Phase closure is automatic**: it holds for any chamber spacing.

This should not be mistaken for a prediction. Any antisymmetric assignment $\theta_{ij} = -\theta_{ji}$ sums to zero over directed edges, so the closure is an identity. It holds for any five points, or any number of points. It therefore carries no information about the pyramid and cannot be tested against it. What remains substantive is the identification of the five compartments with the five vertices of $K_5^{\rightarrow}$.

## Predicted total chamber volume

If each chamber realizes its share of the directed-edge product, the total cumulative chamber volume should satisfy:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} V_i \;\sim\; \text{(framework prediction)}$$

The framework relation $\alpha^{20}$ comes from 20 edges, each carrying weight $\alpha$. Naively, each pair of chambers contributes weight $\alpha^2$ (one edge each way), and there are 10 pairs:

$$\prod_{\text{pairs}}^{10} \alpha^2 \;=\; \alpha^{20}$$

For volume distribution, if each chamber has volume proportional to its participation in the edge product, and the chambers are realizations of "stationary points" on the apparatus's algebraic manifold, then the total volume scales as:

$$\sum V_i \;\propto\; \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}} \cdot (\text{some structural integer})$$

A specific prediction (estimating the structural integer as $5$ for 5 vertices, with each chamber averaging $\alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}$):

$$\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{5}\, V_i \;\approx\; 5\alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}} \;\approx\; 5 \cdot \frac{1}{137} \cdot 2236 \;\approx\; 81.6 \text{ c}^3\;}$$

This prediction **fails**. The compartments' geometric volumes total roughly 680–840 c³, about ten times the prediction (`07.3_relieving_chamber_volumes.md`). The lowest compartment alone exceeds the predicted total. Even allowing for uncertainty in the compartment dimensions, the shortfall is an order of magnitude, so the naive $5\alpha V_{\text{KC}}$ formula is ruled out. Any revised formula must be fixed before it is compared with measured volumes; adjusting it to reach ~700 c³ after the fact would not count as a test.

## Why pentachoron specifically

A pentachoron is the 4-simplex — the regular polytope with 5 vertices in $\mathbb R^4$. It is the smallest convex 4D polytope.

The framework's shift identity $\Phi_6(K+1) = \Phi_3(K)$ at K=4 → K=5 sends the K=4 tetrahedron (4 vertices in $\mathbb R^3$, 1-simplex graph) to the K=5 pentachoron (5 vertices in $\mathbb R^4$, $K_5^{\rightarrow}$ edge graph). The 5-chamber stack physically realizes this lift.

The chambers are *not* arranged as 4D simplex vertices in real space — they sit in 3D space, stacked vertically. The algebraic relationship between them is the 4D pentachoron structure; the spatial layout is a particular 3D projection of that structure.

## The 5th chamber's gabled roof

Campbell's Chamber (the top) has a gabled (peaked) limestone roof, distinct from the flat granite roofs of chambers 1–4. The gabled top is the **closure cap** of the pentachoron — the structure that completes the 5-vertex set and seals the algebraic edge network from above.

The gable also functionally sheds force away from the chamber stack onto the surrounding limestone bulk, consistent with the "stress relief" engineering reading at the conventional level. The framework reads it as both engineering (stress) and algebra (closure cap) — these are compatible.

## The 5-sub-tetrahedron multiplicity

The pentachoron has 5 sub-tetrahedral 3-faces, each obtained by omitting one of the 5 vertices. The 5 relieving chambers correspond directly to these 5 sub-tetrahedra:

| Chamber omitted | Sub-tetrahedron $T_v$ | Apparatus reading |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | $T_1 = \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ | top 4 chambers active |
| 2 | $T_2 = \{1, 3, 4, 5\}$ | 1, 3, 4, 5 active |
| 3 | $T_3 = \{1, 2, 4, 5\}$ | 1, 2, 4, 5 active |
| 4 | $T_4 = \{1, 2, 3, 5\}$ | 1, 2, 3, 5 active |
| 5 | $T_5 = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ | bottom 4 active (5 omitted) |

The hinge operator $J_4$ has a 5-fold sub-tetrahedron multiplicity (`03.1`); the 5 relieving chambers provide the 5 distinct physical realizations.

If the apparatus is to operate in a specific sub-tetrahedron mode, the corresponding chamber must be "open" or excited while the other 4 are "closed" or quiescent. The 5-state switch is implemented by which chamber is currently mode-locked.

## Geometric arrangement vs algebraic structure

In real space, the 5 chambers are stacked vertically — a 1D arrangement. The algebraic structure they realize is the 4D pentachoron $K_5^{\rightarrow}$.

This is allowed because the apparatus's effective topology is set by **which chambers couple to which** through Coulomb / inter-chamber couplings, not by their literal spatial arrangement. The 5 chambers couple pairwise through the granite beam interfaces between them; the coupling strength depends on beam thickness and spacing, not on geometric position relative to each other.

A pentachoron in $\mathbb R^4$ has all pairs of vertices at equal distance. The 5 chambers have inter-spacings that are *not* all equal; their actual coupling strengths therefore differ. But the *structure* of "5 vertices with pairwise couplings" matches the pentachoron — and the phase closure result (`03.2`) is automatic regardless of specific spacings.

## The chambers are an "instrument," not a "tomb"

The five compartments contain no remains and no evidence of burial use. They were sealed until Davison (1765) and Vyse and Perring (1837) reached them, and the upper four are accessible only through Vyse's blasted tunnels. Unlike the King's Chamber, they are rough-finished and carry the builders' painted marks.

The framework reading: the five compartments are an **algebraic instrument** implementing the pentachoron edge product $\alpha^{20}$. The conventional load-spreading reading is not post hoc. Compartments of this kind have a clear structural rationale above a flat granite ceiling carrying a very large load. The cracked and torn beams on the south side show the load is real, and the gabled top is the classic load-shedding form. The framework reading would have to stand alongside that function, not replace it.

## Falsifiable predictions

1. **Total volume ≈ 81 c³.** Already contradicted by an order of magnitude on existing rough dimensions (`07.3`). This prediction should be treated as failed unless a formula derived in advance gives a different value.

2. **Volume distribution consistent with $K_5^{\rightarrow}$ edge counts.** The 5 chambers should not have equal volumes; they should have a pattern consistent with the pentachoron's vertex weights.

3. **Inter-chamber acoustic coupling 21-band signature.** An acoustic excitation in the King's Chamber should propagate through all 5 chambers with a $\mathbb Z_{21}$ characteristic pattern. See `07.1`.

4. **Removing one chamber breaks the apparatus.** If the framework is correct, structurally damaging one of the 5 relieving chambers (e.g., by drilling through and equalizing pressure) should observably alter the King's Chamber's mode spectrum. This is an irreversible test and should not be performed; but the prediction stands.
