# Appendix E — Petrie Measurements

**Status:** Reference / empirical (corrected October 2026 against Petrie's text)
**Reading time:** 6 minutes
**Source:** W. M. Flinders Petrie, *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh* (London: Field & Tuer, 1883); section numbers (§) refer to that work ([online text](https://www.ronaldbirdsall.com/gizeh/petrie/))

---

## Why Petrie

Sir William Matthew Flinders Petrie (1853–1942) surveyed the Giza plateau in 1880–82 with a theodolite, steel tapes and triangulation. His survey remains the standard published source for the interior dimensions of the Great Pyramid. Modern surveys (below) have refined the base and orientation but have not yet published comparable interior measurements.

Petrie quotes an individual uncertainty with each measurement: typically ±0.1 in for chamber dimensions, and more for irregular features. The ± values below are his. Where he gives a range of variation (for example, a coffer side that is not perfectly flat), the range is also given.

## Conversions

- 1 inch = 0.0254 m (exact)
- 1 royal cubit = 20.632 ± 0.004 in = 0.52405 m (Petrie's value from the King's Chamber, §52). The value 0.5236 m used elsewhere in this compendium corresponds to 20.614 in; the difference is 0.09%.
- 1 cubic cubit (c³) = 8782.6 in³ = 0.14392 m³

## King's Chamber

Petrie §52–55, reduced means in §155. The chamber is long **east–west**.

| Length (E–W), north wall | 412.4 | 10.47 | 19.99 | 20 |
| Length (E–W), south wall | 412.1 | 10.47 | 19.97 | 20 |
| **Length, reduced mean** | **412.24 ± 0.12** | **10.471** | **19.98** | 20 |
| Width (N–S), east wall | 206.3 | 5.24 | 10.00 | 10 |
| Width (N–S), west wall | 206.0 | 5.23 | 9.98 | 10 |
| **Width, reduced mean** | **206.12 ± 0.12** | **5.235** | **9.99** | 10 |
| **Height, floor to ceiling** | **230.09 ± 0.15** | **5.844** | **11.15** | $5\sqrt5 = 11.18$ |
| Height, base of walls to ceiling | 235.20 ± 0.06 | 5.974 | 11.40 | 5 courses × 47.04 in |

The granite floor is laid between the walls, not under them, and stands about 5 inches above the base of the lowest wall course (§54). The floor is not level.

**Deviation from the design reading** (floor-to-ceiling height): length −0.09%, width −0.11%, height −0.25%.

**Volume** (floor to ceiling): 412.24 × 206.12 × 230.09 = 19.551 × 10⁶ in³ = **320.4 m³**, against the idealised $1000\sqrt5$ c³ = 321.8 m³ (−0.45%).

**Construction.** The chamber has 100 granite wall blocks in five courses and a granite floor. The roof is nine granite beams spanning north–south; the ceiling weighs about 400 t. Every roof beam is cracked near the south wall, and the chamber has settled by an inch or two (§51, §53).

## Coffer

Petrie §156. The coffer stands near the west wall with its **long axis north–south**. It is chipped, has a broken corner and lacks its lid.

| Measurement | Inches | Metres |
|---|---:|---:|
| External length | 89.62 (mean) | 2.276 |
| External width | 38.50 (mean) | 0.978 |
| External height | **41.31** (varies 41.14–41.50) | 1.049 |
| Internal length | 78.06 (mean; max 78.23) | 1.983 |
| Internal width | 26.81 (mean) | 0.681 |
| Internal depth | 34.42 (mean; max 34.54) | 0.874 |

- External volume: 89.62 × 38.50 × 41.31 = **142,535 in³ = 2.336 m³ = 16.23 c³**
- Internal capacity (Petrie's direct figure, §155–156): **72,000 ± 60 in³**
- Bulk (volume of stone): **≈ 70,500 in³**. The interior is almost exactly half the exterior.

## Coffer-to-chamber ratio

$$\frac{V_{\text{KC}}}{V_{\text{coffer, ext}}} \;=\; \frac{19\,550\,960}{142\,535} \;=\; 137.17 \qquad (1/\alpha = 137.036;\ +0.10\%)$$

This uses the floor-to-ceiling chamber height. With the base-of-walls height (235.20 in) the ratio is 140.2 (+2.3%). Petrie's stated uncertainties give roughly ±0.4% on the ratio. See `04.3` for the full discussion, including the look-elsewhere caveat.

The earlier figures in this appendix (137.83, "off by 0.58%", and 0.7%) used an incorrect external height of 41.13 in.

## Grand Gallery

Petrie §148–150.

| Measurement | Inches | Metres |
|---|---:|---:|
| Horizontal length of floor | 1688.9 ± 0.2 | 42.90 |
| Length along the slope (from the horizontal length and slope) | ≈ 1884 | ≈ 47.8 |
| Width between the walls, lower part | 82.42 | 2.094 |
| Height of the vertical lower walls | 92.4–94.6 | 2.35–2.40 |
| Side ramps: width / height | 19.3–20.4 / 22.65–23.76 | — |
| Overall height (commonly quoted) | ≈ 339 | ≈ 8.6 |
| Width at the top, between the uppermost corbels | ≈ 41 | ≈ 1.04 |

Above the vertical lower walls, each side wall steps inward in **seven corbel courses**. The width falls from ~82.4 in to ~41.2 in, a factor of about 2. The step per corbel per side is therefore (82.42 − 41.21)/14 ≈ **2.9 in**, consistent with the 6–10 cm per step usually quoted. An earlier version gave 5.89 in by dividing the full width, not the reduction, by 14. Lengths of 46.7 m are also widely quoted for the gallery; they depend on where the ends are taken.

The granite wainscots on each side carry slots for **three portcullis slabs**, which would have been lowered from above. A further pair of grooves holds the two-piece "granite leaf". The slots are cut into the wainscots; they do not run to the ceiling.

## Shafts

Petrie describes the King's Chamber shafts only qualitatively. He measured the Queen's Chamber shafts (§44): north, horizontal for 76 in, then rising at a mean 37°28′; south, horizontal for 80 in, then rising at a mean 38°28′. In his day, "no outer end has yet been found for either of them."

Combining Petrie with later surveys:

| Shaft | Rise angle | Length | Upper end |
|---|---|---|---|
| King's north | ~31–32° (30°43′–32°04′), several bends | ~71 m | opens on the north face |
| King's south | ~45° (44°26′–45°30′) | ~53 m | opens on the south face, near the 101st course |
| Queen's north | ~37½–39° | ~73 m to its blocking | blocked by a limestone slab |
| Queen's south | ~38½–39½° | ~60–65 m to the first slab | blocked by a pinned limestone slab, with a second slab ~18 cm beyond |

The Queen's shafts were discovered in 1872 by Waynman Dixon. They were concealed behind the uncut face of the chamber walls, so they never opened into the chamber. The King's shafts were cleared to the outside by Vyse in 1837. An earlier version of this appendix wrongly said the King's shafts "terminate inside masonry" and attributed all four angles to Petrie. See `04.7` for sources.

## Outer pyramid

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mean side of the casing base | 9068.8 ± 0.5 in = 230.35 m | Petrie |
| Design side | 440 cubits | — |
| Original height | 5776.0 ± 7.0 in = 146.7 m (280 cubits) | Petrie §144 |
| Casing angle | **51°52′ ± 2′** | Petrie §145 |
| Apex | missing; the capstone material is unknown | — |

**Orientation.** The sides deviate from the true (geographic, not magnetic) cardinal directions by on average about **3′38″** of arc, about a sixtieth of a degree, with a slight anticlockwise rotation. The base is squared to a mean corner error of about 12″. These modern figures are from Glen Dash's survey.

An earlier version of this appendix listed per-side deviations (−2′28″, −5′30″, −1′57″, −2′30″) that do not match Petrie's or Dash's published values; they have been removed. It also said the orientation "matches the daily wander of the geomagnetic dipole". The daily geomagnetic variation is indeed a few arcminutes, but the pyramid is aligned to true north, which is set astronomically. The similarity in magnitude implies no connection.

## What Petrie concluded

- The pyramid is the tomb of Khufu. Petrie took the conventional attribution as established and offered no mystical purpose.
- He admired the precision of the masonry. He described the joints of the casing stones as being as fine as "opticians' work", and recorded the chamber dimensions to fractions of an inch.
- For working the granite he proposed **bronze saws and tubular drills charged with hard cutting points**, citing the saw and drill marks on the coffer and other granite pieces. This is a hypothesis about tools; modern experimental archaeology favours copper tools with loose sand abrasive.

An earlier version attributed to Petrie conclusions he did not state, about uniform wall thickness to ~1%, granite flatness "rivaling modern machining" and methods "beyond simple manual abrasion". Those attributions have been removed.

## Modern surveys

- **Glen Dash (2015 survey):** re-measured the base of the pyramid, its side lengths, squareness and orientation. This survey is the source of the modern orientation figures above. It did not re-measure the interior.
- **ScanPyramids (muon radiography):** in 2017 it detected a large void ("Big Void") above the Grand Gallery, at least ~30 m long (Morishima et al., *Nature* 552, 2017). In 2023 it confirmed a ~9 m corridor behind the north-face chevrons, which was imaged by endoscope. Neither result re-measured Petrie's chamber dimensions. The function of the Big Void is unknown.
- **No modern laser survey of the King's Chamber and coffer has been published.** Such a survey would be non-invasive and would sharpen every comparison in this compendium, especially `04.3` and `07.2`.
