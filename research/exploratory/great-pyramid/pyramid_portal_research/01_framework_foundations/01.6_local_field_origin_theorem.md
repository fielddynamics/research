## Abstract

GFD's field equation is built from topological integers and is therefore scale-invariant: every closed structure establishes its own field closure. This note makes the consequence precise for a bounded structure.

The note states one postulate: the closure capacity carried across a level surface is proportional to its area. Under it, the GFD state coordinate $s$ and coupling capacity $f$ become explicit functions of position inside the body. The structure's **local Field Origin** ($s = 0$, $f = 17$, $\mu = \tfrac12$) then lies on the surface that divides its closure area equally.

For any body whose level sections are similar figures shrinking to an apex, this surface lies at the universal fractional height $z^*/h = 1 - 1/\sqrt2 = 0.29289$. The result is independent of size and base shape, as scale invariance requires.

For the Great Pyramid, $z^* = 1691.8 \pm 2.1$ in (82.0 royal cubits). Petrie measured the King's Chamber floor at 1691.4–1693.7 ± 0.6 in (82.04 cubits). The structure's local Field Origin and its granite chamber coincide to within the measurement uncertainty.

## 1. Background: the Field Origin as exchange balance

In GFD the two tetrahedral sectors of the field, TetA and TetB, have capacities whose ratio is the odds ratio

$$\Omega \;=\; \frac{\mu}{1-\mu}.$$

The exchange symmetry of the tetrad's two parity-related bounded regions forces $\Omega = x$, the dimensionless field ratio, which gives the constitutive law (constitutive-law letter, Route 3):

$$\mu(x) = \frac{x}{x+1}.$$

The state coordinate of the coupling polynomial is the Möbius image of $x$:

$$s(x) = \frac{x-1}{x+1}, \qquad f(K,s) = 1 + sK + K^2, \qquad f(4,s) = 17 + 4s.$$

These two maps combine into a linear identity, used repeatedly below:

$$\boxed{\; s \;=\; 2\mu - 1 \;}$$

since $2\cdot\frac{x}{x+1} - 1 = \frac{x-1}{x+1}$. The **Field Origin** is the exchange-balance point:

$$\Omega = 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mu = \tfrac12 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; s = 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; f = 17.$$

In the galactic application, $\mu$ is read from the acceleration field, $x = g/a_0$, and the Field Origin lies where $g = a_0$.

## 2. Bounded closures

**Scale invariance.** Every factor of the GFD field equation is a topological integer: $4\pi = K\pi$, the ratio $17/13 = (K^2+1)/(KD+1)$, and the depth $\alpha^{21}$. The equation therefore takes the same form at every scale, and every closed structure establishes its own field closure (docs, *Poisson Equation and Scale Invariance*).

**Definition 1 (bounded closure).** A bounded closure is a compact body $B$ with a closure axis $\hat z$. Its level surfaces $\Sigma(z)$ are the sections of $B$ perpendicular to $\hat z$, for $0 \le z \le h$, and $A(z) = |\Sigma(z)|$ is the area of each.

**Definition 2 (local closure fraction).** The local closure fraction at level $z$ is the fraction of the body's maximal closure capacity carried across $\Sigma(z)$:

$$\mu_B(z) \;=\; \frac{C(z)}{C_{\max}},$$

where $C(z)$ is the closure capacity carried across $\Sigma(z)$.

**Postulate P1 (areal closure).** The closure capacity carried across a level surface is proportional to its area:

$$C(z) \;\propto\; A(z).$$

*Motivation.* In GFD, closure is read through faces, not volumes. The field closes through $K$ coupling faces, and the normalisation $4\pi = K\pi$ counts solid angle per face. In the Gauss form of the field equation, the content of the closure is the flux through a closing surface, and flux through a surface scales with its area. P1 extends this face-and-flux reading of closure from the infinitesimal tetrad to a finite body. It is the single assumption of this note.

## 3. The local state and capacity

With $C_{\max} = C(0)$ at the base:

$$\mu_B(z) \;=\; \frac{A(z)}{A(0)}.$$

By the identity $s = 2\mu - 1$:

$$\boxed{\; s_B(z) \;=\; \frac{2A(z)}{A(0)} - 1, \qquad f_B(z) \;=\; 17 + 4\,s_B(z) \;=\; 13 + 8\,\frac{A(z)}{A(0)} \;}$$

**Proposition 1 (full-range realisation).** A body whose sections shrink from the base to a point at the apex carries the complete GFD state range:

| Level | $A(z)/A(0)$ | $s_B$ | $f_B$ | GFD state |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| base, $z = 0$ | 1 | +1 | 21 | TetA |
| local Field Origin, $z = z^*$ | ½ | 0 | 17 | Field Origin |
| apex, $z = h$ | 0 | −1 | 13 | TetB |

The capacity runs linearly in area from the TetA value 21 to the TetB value 13. Its range, 8 = 2^D, is the same range as the coupling polynomial.

## 4. The theorem

**Theorem 1 (local Field Origin of an apex body).** Let $B$ be a bounded closure whose level sections are similar figures, homothetic about the closure axis, shrinking linearly to an apex at height $h$. Under P1, the local Field Origin of $B$ lies at

$$\boxed{\; \frac{z^*}{h} \;=\; 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt2} \;=\; 0.292\,893\ldots \;}$$

independent of the body's size, base shape and absolute field environment.

*Proof.* For homothetic sections shrinking linearly to the apex, the linear scale of $\Sigma(z)$ is proportional to $1 - z/h$, so

$$A(z) = A(0)\left(1 - \frac{z}{h}\right)^2.$$

The local Field Origin is defined by $s_B = 0$, that is, $A(z^*)/A(0) = \tfrac12$. Solving $(1 - z^*/h)^2 = \tfrac12$ gives $z^*/h = 1 - 2^{-1/2}$. Nothing in this depends on $h$, on $A(0)$ or on the shape of the section. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (equivalent vertical reading).** The area of the vertical cross-section lying above level $z$ is also proportional to $(1 - z/h)^2$. The plane $z^*$ therefore divides the vertical section into two equal areas: the triangle above and the trapezium below. The horizontal-area and vertical-area readings select the same plane.

**Corollary 2 (scale invariance).** $z^*/h$ is a pure number. A one-metre model and a 146-metre monument with the same proportions have their local Field Origins at the same fractional height. This is the scale invariance of GFD expressed in a bounded body.

**Remark (the volumetric alternative).** Under a volumetric postulate, $C \propto$ volume above $z$, the balance plane would instead lie at $z/h = 1 - 2^{-1/3} = 0.2063$. In the Great Pyramid that is 1191.6 in (30.3 m) above the base, where there is no chamber or architectural feature. The measured architecture therefore discriminates between the two postulates, in favour of P1 (§5).

## 5. Comparison with the Great Pyramid

**Height.** Petrie's original height is $h = 5776.0 \pm 7.0$ in (§144), which is 280 royal cubits of 20.632 in.

**Prediction.** By Theorem 1:

$$z^* \;=\; 5776.0 \times 0.292\,893 \;=\; 1691.8 \pm 2.1 \text{ in} \;=\; 82.0 \text{ cubits}.$$

**Measurement.** Petrie gives the King's Chamber floor level as 1691.4 to 1693.7 ± 0.6 in (§148), with mean 1692.6 in (82.04 cubits). He gives the level of half-area horizontal section as 1691.7 ± 1.8 in, and concludes that "the design of the level of the King's Chamber was the halving of the vertical area of the Pyramid."

The granite chamber, the only all-granite room in the structure, has its floor on the structure's local Field Origin. The architectural record also shows the builders deliberately setting a principal chamber at a precise height within the superstructure. In the neighbouring pyramids of Khafre and Menkaure, the burial chambers lie at or below ground level.

## 6. Nested structure inside the chamber

Within the chamber, the same balance recurs at the scale of the resonator:

- **The coffer.** Its interior capacity (72,000 ± 60 in³) equals its bulk of stone (≈ 70,500 in³) to within 2% (Petrie §155–156). This is a volumetric equal partition: an independent realisation of $\Omega = 1$ at the resonator scale, not a consequence of P1.
- **The floor plan.** The floor is two equal 10 × 10 cubit squares, a dual-chamber plan with $\Omega = 1$ between its halves (Petrie §155).
- **The K=5 capacity.** The space diagonal of 25 cubits plus the coffer gives 26 = $\Phi_4(5) = K^2 + 1|_{K=5}$, the Field Origin capacity of the shell into which the hinge $J_4$ lifts (`04.2`, `03.1`).

The structure thus realises the Field Origin at three nested scales: the whole body (Theorem 1), the chamber (its floor plan) and the resonator (the coffer). This is the hierarchy that scale invariance leads one to expect.

## 7. Status, scope and tests

**What is derived.** Given P1, Theorem 1 follows by elementary geometry. Its numerical agreement with the King's Chamber floor (§5) is a comparison against independent measurements: Petrie's height and his floor level.

**What is postulated.** Nothing further. P1 is derived from the covariant action in [`01.7_areal_closure_from_action.md`](01.7_areal_closure_from_action.md). The closure current is the Noether current of the scalar sector's shift symmetry, $\mathbf J = \mu\nabla\Phi/4\pi G$. The divergence theorem then gives $\mu_B = \Phi_\Sigma/\Phi_{\text{base}}$, which reduces to $A(z)/A(0)$ for an axially uniform driven field. The volumetric alternative has no counterpart in the action.

**Scope.** The theorem locates a local Field Origin. That the location is driven into a coherent state, the Field Origin *realised*, depends on the isolation, pumping and phase conditioning described in [`FIELD_ORIGIN_KINGS_CHAMBER.md`](../FIELD_ORIGIN_KINGS_CHAMBER.md) §4–5, and is tested by H2–H7 of [`PROGRAMME.md`](../PROGRAMME.md).

**Tests.**

1. **Survey.** A laser survey of the King's Chamber floor and of the casing-reconstructed height, to sub-inch precision (WP1). Theorem 1 predicts the floor at $0.292\,89\,h$; a departure of more than 3 in would disfavour the identification.
2. **Discrimination of P1.** P1 places the balance plane at $0.2929\,h$; the volumetric postulate places it at $0.2063\,h$. A first-principles derivation of the closure measure from the GFD action should select between them. The architecture currently selects P1.
3. **Model universality.** Corollary 2 predicts that scaled physical models of the pyramid carry their balance plane at the same fractional height. This can be tested in Phase 0 by modelling, with the electromagnetic and acoustic models of PROGRAMME §5, how the field distribution partitions with height in scaled geometry.
