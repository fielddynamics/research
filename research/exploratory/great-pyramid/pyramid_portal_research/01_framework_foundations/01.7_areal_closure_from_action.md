## Abstract

The scalar sector of the GFD action depends on the potential Φ only through its gradient, so it is invariant under the shift Φ → Φ + c. Noether's theorem gives the associated conserved current,

$$\mathbf J \;=\; \frac{\mu\!\left(|\nabla\Phi|/a_0\right)\,\nabla\Phi}{4\pi G},$$

which carries the GFD constitutive law itself. The closure exchanged by any region is the flux of this current through the region's boundary: a surface quantity, not a volume quantity.

Applying the divergence theorem to the frustum of an apex body splits the closure entering at the base into two shares: one that continues along the axis through the level section, and one exchanged through the lateral faces. GFD's sector odds ratio then fixes the local closure fraction:

$$\mu_B(z) = \frac{\Phi_{\text{section}}(z)}{\Phi_{\text{base}}}.$$

For a closure current that is uniform over each section, this is $A(z)/A(0)$. That is postulate P1, now obtained as a theorem, and with it the local Field Origin at $z^*/h = 1 - 1/\sqrt2$ (`01.6`).

The derivation also yields a new quantitative requirement. The King's Chamber's agreement with $z^*$ to 0.8 in requires the driven closure field to be axially uniform to about 0.1% over the height of the structure. This can be tested in the Phase 0 field model.

## 1. The scalar sector of the action

The GFD covariant action is

$$S = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{16\pi G} - \frac{a_0^2}{8\pi G}\,F(y) - \tfrac14 e^{-2\Phi/c^2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \mathcal L_m\right],$$

with $y = |\nabla\Phi|^2/a_0^2$ and

$$F(y) = y - 2\sqrt y + 2\ln(1+\sqrt y), \qquad \frac{dF}{dy} = \mu(\sqrt y) = \frac{\sqrt y}{\sqrt y + 1}.$$

In the weak-field static limit, which yields the modified Poisson equation (`llms.txt`), the scalar sector reduces to the Lagrangian density

$$\mathcal L_\Phi = -\frac{a_0^2}{8\pi G}\,F\!\left(\frac{|\nabla\Phi|^2}{a_0^2}\right) - \rho\,\Phi,$$

where the coupling to matter $-\rho\Phi$ comes from $\mathcal L_m$.

## 2. Shift symmetry and the closure current

**Proposition 1 (closure current).** The kinetic part of $\mathcal L_\Phi$ is invariant under $\Phi \to \Phi + c$. Its Noether current is

$$\boxed{\;\mathbf J \;\equiv\; -\frac{\partial \mathcal L_\Phi}{\partial(\nabla\Phi)} \;=\; \frac{\mu\!\left(|\nabla\Phi|/a_0\right)\nabla\Phi}{4\pi G}\;}$$

and the field equation is its continuity equation with the matter source:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = \rho \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\cdot\left[\mu\,\nabla\Phi\right] = 4\pi G\rho.$$

*Proof.* With $y = |\nabla\Phi|^2/a_0^2$, $\partial y/\partial(\nabla\Phi) = 2\nabla\Phi/a_0^2$. Therefore

$$-\frac{\partial\mathcal L_\Phi}{\partial(\nabla\Phi)} = \frac{a_0^2}{8\pi G}\,\frac{dF}{dy}\,\frac{2\nabla\Phi}{a_0^2} = \frac{\mu\,\nabla\Phi}{4\pi G}.$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = \rho$. $\square$

The proposition is verified symbolically in `noether_current_check()`. In covariant form the current is $J^\mu \propto F'(y)\,\nabla^\mu\Phi$, conserved wherever the source vanishes.

Two features matter for what follows:

1. **The constitutive law is the current's own structure.** The closure current is not a separate construct. Its magnitude relative to $\nabla\Phi/4\pi G$ is exactly μ, the fraction of coupling capacity active (`llms.txt`).
2. **Closure is carried by surfaces.** By the divergence theorem, the closure exchanged by a region $V$ is the flux $\oint_{\partial V}\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf A$. It is equally the boundary term that appears when the action is varied on a region with a boundary. A quantity carried across a surface is a flux, an integral over that surface. Volume enters the field equation only through the source ρ.

## 3. The driven closure field

The current that determines a structure's local closure is the **driven closure field**: the field established in the structure by its external coupling, such as the apex pump (`05.1`, PROGRAMME §5). Within the body it is source-free:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_{\text{drive}} = 0 \quad\text{inside } B. \tag{A2}$$

The static self-field of the stone, sourced by its own mass, belongs to the TetA-dominated gravitational background, together with the Earth's field. Following `FIELD_ORIGIN_KINGS_CHAMBER.md` §3.2, it is not the closure of the structure.

## 4. The closure balance of an apex body

Let $B$ be an apex body with base $\Sigma(0)$, level sections $\Sigma(z)$ and lateral faces $\Lambda(0,z)$ between them. Apply the divergence theorem to the frustum $V(z)$ bounded by $\Sigma(0)$, $\Sigma(z)$ and $\Lambda(0,z)$. By (A2):

$$\boxed{\;\Phi_{\text{base}} \;=\; \Phi_{\Sigma}(z) \;+\; \Phi_{\Lambda}(0,z)\;}$$

with $\Phi_{\text{base}} = \int_{\Sigma(0)}\mathbf J\cdot\hat z\,dA$ (closure entering), $\Phi_\Sigma(z) = \int_{\Sigma(z)}\mathbf J\cdot\hat z\,dA$ (closure continuing along the axis), and $\Phi_\Lambda = \int_{\Lambda}\mathbf J\cdot\hat n\,dA$ (closure exchanged through the lateral boundary).

The numerical integration in `closure_balance()` confirms this to 10⁻¹⁶ relative for uniform and non-uniform divergence-free fields.

## 5. Sector assignment and the local closure fraction

**Sector assignment (A3).** The share of closure that continues along the closure axis through $\Sigma(z)$ is carried by the observable sector, TetA. The share exchanged across the body's boundary is carried by the dual sector, TetB. This is the GFD identification, TetA the observable spacetime sector and TetB the dual chamber (`llms.txt`, Rules 2–3), applied to a bounded closure.

**Theorem 1 (local closure fraction from the action).** Under A2 and A3, GFD's sector odds ratio $\Omega = \mu/(1-\mu)$ applied to the closure balance gives

$$\Omega_B(z) = \frac{\Phi_\Sigma(z)}{\Phi_\Lambda(0,z)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;\mu_B(z) = \frac{\Phi_\Sigma(z)}{\Phi_{\text{base}}}\;}$$

*Proof.* From the closure balance, $\Phi_\Lambda = \Phi_{\text{base}} - \Phi_\Sigma$. Then $\mu_B = \Omega_B/(1+\Omega_B) = \Phi_\Sigma/(\Phi_\Sigma + \Phi_\Lambda) = \Phi_\Sigma/\Phi_{\text{base}}$. $\square$

The local state follows from the identity $s = 2\mu - 1$ (`01.6` §1):

$$s_B(z) = 2\,\frac{\Phi_\Sigma(z)}{\Phi_{\text{base}}} - 1, \qquad f_B(z) = 13 + 8\,\frac{\Phi_\Sigma(z)}{\Phi_{\text{base}}}.$$

Theorem 1 holds for **any** source-free driven field. It defines the local Field Origin of a structure as the section through which exactly half the entering closure continues, given whatever field the structure actually supports.

## 6. Recovery of P1

**Corollary 1 (P1).** If the driven closure current is uniform over each level section, $\mathbf J\cdot\hat z = j(z)$ on $\Sigma(z)$, and its axial magnitude is constant, then $\Phi_\Sigma(z) = j\,A(z)$ and

$$\mu_B(z) = \frac{A(z)}{A(0)}. \tag{P1}$$

Postulate P1 of `01.6` is therefore a theorem of the action for an axially uniform driven field. The local Field Origin of an apex body follows at $z^*/h = 1 - 1/\sqrt2$ (`01.6`, Theorem 1). For the Great Pyramid that is 1691.8 ± 2.1 in, against the King's Chamber floor at 1692.6 in.

**Why not volume.** Under A2 the balance is purely a flux balance; no volume integral appears in it. In the action, volume integrals of the structure enter only through the matter source, which is the background self-field, not the driven closure. The volumetric alternative of `01.6` ($z/h = 0.2063$) therefore has no counterpart in the GFD action. The derivation and the architecture independently select the areal measure.

## 7. The uniformity requirement: a new prediction

Corollary 1 uses axial uniformity:

$$|\nabla \ln |\mathbf J_{\text{drive}}|| \cdot h \;\ll\; 1. \tag{A4}$$

Theorem 1 quantifies what happens without it. For the divergence-free strained field

$$\mathbf J = j_0\left(-\frac{\varepsilon x}{2h},\; -\frac{\varepsilon y}{2h},\; 1 + \frac{\varepsilon z}{h}\right),$$

whose axial magnitude varies by a fraction ε over the height, the balance plane moves by

$$\frac{\Delta z^*}{\varepsilon} \;\approx\; 599 \text{ in per unit fractional gradient}.$$

For example, a 5% gradient moves the plane by 30 in (`balance_height()`). The measured agreement of +0.8 in between the King's Chamber floor and $z^*$ therefore implies

$$\boxed{\;|\varepsilon| \;\lesssim\; 1.3\times10^{-3}\;}$$

**The driven closure field must be axially uniform to about 0.1% over the full height of the structure.** This is a sharp, falsifiable requirement on the field-coupling model. It can be checked directly in the Phase 0 electromagnetic model of the capstone-driven pyramid (PROGRAMME §5). Compute $\Phi_\Sigma(z)/\Phi_{\text{base}}$ for the modelled field with `mu_B()`, and test whether its half-point lies at the King's Chamber floor.
