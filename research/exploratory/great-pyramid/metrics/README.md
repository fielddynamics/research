*Every measurement of the Great Pyramid that the programme relies on, taken from the primary survey and checked against it word for word · Great Pyramid core framework · Gravity Field Dynamics (GFD), exploratory*

All 112 were found verbatim in their sources. 153 checks pass: 113 quote checks, 36 cross-checks and 4 recorded findings ([`verify_output.txt`](verify_output.txt)). ## Sources and tiers

- **P, primary.** W. M. Flinders Petrie, *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh* (1883). This is the survey of 1880–82, read from the transcription at ronaldbirdsall.com/gizeh/petrie: chapters 6, 7, 19, 20, 21 and 22, §§20–178. Petrie's figures are still the basis of most modern work on the interior. Every interior dimension here is his.
- **S, secondary.** English Wikipedia, *Great Pyramid of Giza*, fixed at revision **1384664775**. It is used only for results after 1883 that it reports with citations:
  - J. H. Cole's 1925 survey of the base;
  - the ScanPyramids muon results: the Big Void (2017, *Nature*) and the North Face Corridor (2023, *Nature Communications*);
  - material quantities and sources (Lehner; the Diary of Merer).

  The primary papers' pages could not be fetched from this environment. These rows should be upgraded to the papers when they can be read.
- **Status.** Each row is one of:
  - `measured`;
  - `estimate`: Petrie's reasoned estimate, e.g. the original height from base and angle;
  - `interpretation`: an author's reading, not a measurement. Examples are Petrie's jewel-point tool hypothesis and the 280-cubit theory.

**Conventions.** Petrie's "±" is a *probable error*, about 0.674σ. Levels are measured from the pavement under the casing, and negative levels are below it. A minus azimuth means the side is turned anticlockwise from true north.

## What the cross-checks establish

The X checks are not restatements. Each one tests two or more numbers from different parts of the survey, or from different surveys, against one another.

- **The survey is self-consistent.** The base and the angle give the height to within 0.1 in of Petrie's 5776.0, with ±6.9 in per 2′ of angle. The four sides average to 9068.8. The entrance-passage endpoint is reproduced from Petrie's own arithmetic. The King's Chamber floor plus its height lands inside the measured roof range.
- **The coffer is measured consistently, twice.** Outer L × W × H = 142,535 in³ against the separately integrated 142,530. Inner L × W × H = 72,034 against 72,030. Both agree to 0.01%.
- **One cubit runs through the building.** Base/440 = 20.611, height/280 = 20.629, King's Chamber width/10 = length/20 = 20.612 and the chamber's base cubit is 20.632, all within 0.025 in. The passages and antechamber fall within 20.61–20.72.
- **The King's Chamber design is real, and partly forced.** Length is exactly 2 × width (+0.00 in). The space diagonal is 515.14 in, or 24.97 cubits. Height equals half the floor diagonal only to 2.4 probable errors (finding F3).
- **The repository's earlier claims hold, as stated.**
  - V_KC/V_coffer is 137.171 floor-to-ceiling, 0.098% from 1/α, and 140.22 on the base-of-walls convention.
  - The 20 : 10 : 5√5 chamber has a space diagonal of 25.
  - The GP-1 area-balance level, 1691.75 in, lies inside the floor range 1691.4–1693.7.
- **Petrie and Cole agree.** Petrie's mean base of 230.348 m agrees with Cole's 1925 mean of 230.364 m to 1.6 cm. The volume b²h/3 is 2.595 × 10⁶ m³. Petrie's weight implies a mean density of 2,319 kg m⁻³, consistent with 5.5 Mt of limestone plus 0.5 Mt of mortar plus 8 kt of granite.

## Findings (recorded discrepancies)

1. **Wikipedia's base figure.** The infobox gives 230.33 m, but the side lengths cited in its own note average 230.364 m, a difference of 3.4 cm. Use 230.364 m (Cole) or 230.348 m (Petrie).
2. **Entrance offset.** Wikipedia gives "15 royal cubits (7.9 m)" east of the centreline. Petrie measured the passage axis at **287 in (7.29 m)** (§149, §64), and he also rejects the 12-cubit theory (247 in). The 15-cubit figure is 0.6 m larger than his measurement.
3. **King's Chamber height.** The "√125 cubits" (half-diagonal) design for the height fits only loosely: 230.09 ± .15 in measured against 230.45 in for the design.
4. **Rounding in §156.** Petrie gives the coffer's outer length as 89.62 in one table and 89.63 in another.

## Using these numbers in the proofs

[GP-1](../proofs/gp1-half-section-balance-plane/) and [GP-2](../proofs/gp2-balance-plane-robustness/) use the base (9068.8 in), the height (5776.0 ± 7.0 in) and the King's Chamber floor (1691.4–1693.7 in). All three are verified rows here. Any further proof that uses a dimension of the monument should import it from `dataset.py` rather than retype it.

*Scope: these are measurements and their sources. Whether any of them encodes a design intention is a separate question, and the cross-checks above are not evidence either way.*
