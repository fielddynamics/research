## What this shows, and why it matters

GFD's coupling polynomial gives Newton's constant its integers: 21 as the exponent, and 17/13 as the ratio. The *I Ching*'s hexagrams carry two binary operations: the threefold read R(a, b) = (b, a ⊕ b), and the trigram swap. Proof 26 uses the Sierpiński generator L, and the [null-rotation proof](../../../proofs/g-null-rotation/) shows L is a null rotation.

This note shows that these are one object, seen through the prime 2:

- The three values 21, 17 and 13 are the determinants det(K·I − Cₙ) of the integer rotations of order 3, 4 and 6, the only rotations a lattice allows.
- Reduced mod 2, TetA's and TetB's rotations both become the *I Ching* read R. That's because 2 stays prime in the Eisenstein integers, whose residue field is F₄. The Field Origin's rotation becomes the trigram swap, which is conjugate to the Sierpiński generator L. That's because 2 ramifies in the Gaussian integers, as 2 = −i(1 + i)².
- R and the swap generate GL(2,2), the group whose orbits give the Fano decomposition of the hexagrams.

This gives a precise reason why a binary register can carry G's 21 but not its ratio. Mod 2, TetA's rotation and TetB's become the same map, so the root sign is lost. It survives one binary digit up, mod 4.

## 1. The coupling polynomial is a characteristic polynomial

Let Cₙ be the integer rotation of order n, the companion matrix of the cyclotomic polynomial Φₙ:

$$C_3 = \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad C_4 = \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad C_6 = \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Then

$$f(K, s) = 1 + sK + K^2 = \det(K\,I - C_n) = \Phi_n(K),\qquad s = -\operatorname{tr} C_n,$$

with n = 3, 4, 6 for s = +1, 0, −1 (TetA, the Field Origin, TetB). At K = 4 this gives 21, 17 and 13.

The state label s is minus the trace of the rotation. This extends the [read-and-rotation](../../g_from_topology/read-and-rotation/) result, where each value is |K − λ|² for the winding eigenvalue λ of its symmetry class.

## 2. The same numbers are norms

Each value is the norm of K minus a root of unity, in the ring generated by that root:

$$21 = N(4 - \omega),\qquad 13 = N(4 + \omega)\quad\text{in } \mathbb Z[\omega],\qquad 17 = N(4 - i)\quad\text{in } \mathbb Z[i].$$

Since (4 − ω)(4 + ω) = 17 + ω, the capacity is

$$273 = N(17 + \omega) = \Phi_6(17) = \Phi_3(16).$$

That is the TetB norm evaluated at the Field Origin's own value, 17. TetB's rotation C₆ has the sign-reversed eigenvalues of TetA's, −ω and −ω̄. So the root is a sign.

## 3. Reduction at two

The two rings behave differently at the prime 2:

| Ring | Rotation | Φₙ mod 2 | The prime 2 | Cₙ mod 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ℤ[ω] | C₃ (TetA), C₆ (TetB) | t² + t + 1, irreducible | stays prime; residue field F₄ | **R**, the *I Ching* read (a, b) → (b, a ⊕ b) |
| ℤ[i] | C₄ (Field Origin) | (t + 1)², a square | ramifies: 2 = −i(1 + i)² | **S**, the trigram swap (a, b) → (b, a) |

This has four consequences:

- **K = 4 is the size of the residue field.** ℤ[ω]/(2) is F₄, the field whose ω is the label cycle of [Proof 26](../../../proofs/recursive-field-closure-response/).
- **TetA and TetB become the same map.** Since −1 = +1 mod 2, C₃ and C₆ reduce to the same R. The binary register is root-blind. The two differ mod 4, so the sign lives in the second binary digit.
- **The Field Origin's rotation becomes unipotent.** S satisfies (S − I)² = 0 mod 2, and S is conjugate in GL(2,2) to the Sierpiński generator L = [[1,0],[1,1]] of Proof 26's Theorem 4. The [null-rotation proof](../../../proofs/g-null-rotation/) shows the Sierpiński blocks of G's graded operator are null rotations. So the null rotation is the Field Origin's 90° turn reduced at its ramified prime.
- **Together they generate the hexagram group.** R and S generate GL(2,2) ≅ S₃. Its orbits on the 64 hexagrams are 1 + 7·3 + 7·6, the Fano decomposition of the [Frobenius reciprocal tower](FROBENIUS_RECIPROCAL_TOWER.md). R alone gives Proof 26's 21 directions, and S alone fixes the 8 hexagrams with equal trigrams.

## 4. What this explains

**Why the hexagrams carry 21 but not 17/13.** The *I Ching* register is binary, so it sees each rotation only mod 2. There, TetA's and TetB's rotations are both R, which carries the 21 (as [The Hexagrams and the Closure Read](HEXAGRAMS_AND_THE_CLOSURE_READ.md) shows). But it cannot separate 21 from 13, because the sign that distinguishes the roots has been lost. The ratio 17/13 is a statement in characteristic zero. It needs the sign, which survives mod 4 but not mod 2.

**Why the Sierpiński generator cannot attenuate.** Proof 26 shows the one-way Sierpiński sweep is unipotent, with determinant one and no loss. Here that is no accident: it is the Field Origin's rotation seen at the prime where it becomes unipotent. Attenuation needs the alternating sweeps, the full rotation lifted back to characteristic zero.

**Where π and G sit** [E]. The [Book of Changes and π](BOOK_OF_CHANGES_AND_PI.md) traces π in the BBP formula to the pole 1 + i, the ramified prime above 2 in ℤ[i]. G's exponent 21 comes from the inert prime 2 in ℤ[ω], through F₄. So the two constants sit at the two ways the prime 2 can behave in the two rings of the lattice's rotations. This is an observation, not a derivation of either.

## What this does not say

- It does not derive the physical gain |t|² = α, or why the native action reads a determinant. Both remain premises of Proof 26.
- It does not make the binary register physical. It explains what a binary register can and cannot see.
- The mod-4 statement only shows that C₃ and C₆ differ in the second binary digit. Whether the *I Ching*'s π walk, which adds mod 8 with carries, uses that digit is open. Its carries are exactly what XOR lacks, which makes it a natural next test.
