## Summary

- GFD's Möbius map $s = (x-1)/(x+1)$ is exactly the reflection coefficient of a transmission line, with $x$ the normalised load. The constitutive law $\mu = (1+s)/2$ is the voltage divider, and the transition $x = 1$ is the point of maximum power transfer.
- The paper's N = 2 law is the per-section transfer of an infinite ladder network.
- The same construction applied to the Sierpiński gasket and tetrahedron gives new laws that can be computed exactly. Their deep-field correction and their Newtonian-side tail follow closed forms.
- **Every network of this kind satisfies $\mu_{\text{net}}(x) \le x/(1+x)$.** The simple law is the most Newtonian law any such network can produce, and fractal structure only slows the approach to Newton.
- Against the radial acceleration relation and the Solar System, the fractal laws fit worse than N = 1, and worse the more fractal they are. The literal fractal-antenna reading of the field fails. What survives is the bound, which characterises $\mu(x) = x/(1+x)$ as extremal.

All statements are checked by the script: 17 checks, exact or high precision.

## 1. The transmission-line dictionary

Treat $x$ as a load impedance normalised to the line, $z = Z_L/Z_0$. Then:

| GFD | Transmission line |
|---|---|
| $s = (x-1)/(x+1)$ | Reflection coefficient Γ (the Smith chart map) |
| $s = -1,\ 0,\ +1$ (TetB, Field Origin, TetA) | Short circuit, matched load, open circuit |
| $\mu = x/(1+x) = (1+s)/2$ | Load voltage over source EMF, with a matched source |
| $1 - \mu = (1-s)/2$ | The current share |
| $4\mu(1-\mu) = 1 - s^2$ | Power delivered to the load, maximal at $x = 1$ |
| $x \to 1/x$, so $s \to -s$; $\mu(x) + \mu(1/x) = 1$ | The quarter-wave transformer (impedance inversion) |
| $f(4, s) = 17 + 4s$ | The coupling polynomial is linear in Γ |
| $\max(x, 1/x)$ | VSWR |

The a₀ transition is therefore the matched condition: the point at which the field delivers the most power across the Field Origin.

One correction to [`read_sweep_synthesis.md`](../g_from_topology/read_sweep_synthesis.md), which calls $1 - \mu$ the reflection coefficient. In this dictionary the reflection coefficient is $s$ itself, and $1 - \mu$ is the current share.

## 2. One construction, four networks

Take a network of unit series edges with a shunt impedance $x$ from every node to ground. Drive one corner through a unit source resistance. The voltage remaining after the source resistance is

$$\mu(x) = \frac{Z}{1+Z},$$

where $Z$ is the driving-point impedance at the corner.

| Network | $Z$ | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Single node | $Z = x$ | $\mu = x/(1+x)$, the paper's N = 1 |
| Semi-infinite chain | $Z = x \parallel (1 + Z)$ | $\mu = (\sqrt{1+4x}-1)/(\sqrt{1+4x}+1)$, the paper's Eq. 23 (N = 2) |
| Sierpiński gasket | Exact self-similar decimation, 3 corners | New |
| Sierpiński tetrahedron | Exact self-similar decimation, 4 corners | New |

**The chain is N = 2.** The infinite L-ladder with series element 1 and shunt element $x$ has input impedance $z = (1 + \sqrt{1+4x})/2$. Its per-section transfer $t = (z-1)/z$ satisfies $x(1-t)^2 = t$, the paper's N = 2 screening equation. At $x = 1$, $z = \varphi$ (the classic infinite resistor ladder) and $t = 1/\varphi^2$. N = 1 is one divider section; N = 2 is the self-similar limit of a one-dimensional chain of sections.

**The gaskets by decimation.** A level-$(n+1)$ Sierpiński simplex is $k$ copies of the level-$n$ piece glued at their midpoints. Track the $k \times k$ boundary (Dirichlet-to-Neumann) matrix of a piece, add the midpoint shunts, and eliminate the midpoints by a Schur complement. This gives the next level exactly; the corner impedance of the infinite network is the limit. The decimation agrees with brute-force meshes of 88,575 nodes (gasket) and 131,074 nodes (tetrahedron) to machine precision.

## 3. Exact results

**Values at the transition.**

| Network | $\mu(1)$ |
|---|---|
| Single node | 0.5000 |
| Chain | 0.3820 ($= 1/\varphi^2$) |
| Sierpiński gasket | 0.2981 |
| Sierpiński tetrahedron | 0.2402 |

**Deep field.** $\mu \approx x - c\,x^2$, with $c = 1 + (\text{number of edges at the corner})$: 1, 2, 3 and 4. The paper's N-family $x(1-\mu)^N = \mu$ has $c = N$. On this side the gasket behaves as N = 3 and the Sierpiński tetrahedron as N = 4 = K.

**Newtonian side.** $1 - \mu \propto x^{-(1 - d_s/2)}$, where $d_s$ is the spectral dimension of the network:

| Network | $d_s$ | Predicted exponent $1 - d_s/2$ | Measured (60-digit decimation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single node | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| Chain | 1 | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| Gasket | $2\ln 3/\ln 5 = 1.3652$ | 0.31739 | 0.31740 |
| Tetrahedron | $2\ln 4/\ln 6 = 1.5474$ | 0.22629 | 0.22630 |

The paper's N-family has tail $x^{-1/N}$, which corresponds to $d_s = 2(1 - 1/N)$. The networks match the N-family to first order on each side, but they are not the same curves.

**An upper bound.** The corner's own shunt $x$ sits in parallel with the rest of the network, so $Z \le x$. Therefore

$$\mu_{\text{net}}(x) \le \frac{x}{1+x} \quad \text{for every network of this construction.}$$

Adding edges or nodes only lowers $Z$ (Rayleigh monotonicity), and each network here contains the previous one. So the ordering single node ≥ chain ≥ gasket ≥ tetrahedron holds at every $x$.

## 4. Against observation

**The radial acceleration relation.** Each law was fitted to the empirical relation of McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert (2016), $g_{\text{obs}} = g_{\text{bar}}/(1 - e^{-\sqrt{g_{\text{bar}}/g_\dagger}})$ with $g_\dagger = 1.20 \times 10^{-10}$ m/s², over $10^{-12}$ to $10^{-8.5}$ m/s², with a₀ left free. This compares curve shapes against the fitting function, not against the galaxy data themselves.

**The Solar System.** The fractional excess $1/\mu - 1$ at Saturn's orbit, $g = 6.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m/s², with a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s².

| Law | Best-fit a₀ (m/s²) | RMS vs RAR | Excess at Saturn |
|---|---|---|---|
| N = 1, single node | 1.17 × 10⁻¹⁰ | 0.011 dex | 2 × 10⁻⁶ |
| N = 2, chain | 8.0 × 10⁻¹¹ | 0.058 dex | 1.4 × 10⁻³ |
| Sierpiński gasket | 4.5 × 10⁻¹¹ | 0.117 dex | 2.5 × 10⁻² |
| Sierpiński tetrahedron | 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ | 0.182 dex | 1.2 × 10⁻¹ |

The binding Solar System constraint on MOND-type laws is the quadrupole induced by the Galaxy's external field, measured through Cassini ranging. It favours laws that reach the Newtonian regime exponentially fast. Hees et al. (2016) find the simple law, which is exactly $x/(1+x)$, excluded for low exponent and only marginal otherwise. Power-law tails slower than $1/x$ are worse still.

## 5. What this means

1. **Passive fractal structure cannot be how gravity recovers Newton.** It can only slow that recovery. The idea that the field is literally a Sierpiński antenna, taken as a passive network, is ruled out by the bound in §3 together with the constraints in §4.
2. **The bound characterises the constitutive law.** Among all networks of this construction, $\mu(x) = x/(1+x)$ is the most Newtonian law possible. This is a characterisation of GFD's law that does not depend on the coupling polynomial.
3. **The N = 2 prediction leans on screening.** The paper's N = 2 law leaves a 0.14% excess at Saturn. Its statement that both laws are consistent with Solar System tests therefore depends on the Vainshtein screening of the bimetric sector doing real work in the inner Solar System.
4. **The next model is a nonlinear shunt.** The circuit picture of screening is a shunt whose impedance depends on the local field strength. Such a shunt can steepen the tail beyond the passive bound. That is the version of the network idea worth building next.
