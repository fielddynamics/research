# Independent Review: Gram-matrix proof, vortex-hourglass research, CamB correction

*Reviewer: independent referee. Repository: /home/claude/research at ee0217b (clean tree before and after the review). Every script named below was rerun with `python3 -I`, and every output was compared byte for byte with the committed one. Throwaway scripts were kept in /tmp/rev/scripts and nothing in the repository was changed. The letter figures regenerated by `run_all_validations.py` were restored with `git checkout`.*

## Verdict

1. **The Gram-matrix proof is mathematically correct.** I checked every lemma, theorem and proposition by hand and reran its script (66/66). The revision fixes real errors in the original note. It is careful about scope, with one consequence: it shows that the "therefore root-resolving" step in Letter 2's Proposition does not follow. Its Proposition 2(a) is a valid counterexample. I confirmed that the script's example connection has nonzero torsion and is still identical on both roots. Letter 2 is under review, so this matters.
2. **Every vortex-hourglass script runs and reproduces its committed output exactly** (text, CSVs, PNG). The lemma and roll-ratio algebra are correct. Their physical identifications are readings, and the README is honest about that.
3. **The data analysis has three substantive problems.**
   - A bootstrap bug makes the quoted confidence intervals too narrow.
   - The "slip" and "saturates less than μ(x)" away from the ring are largely what binning on a noisy g_obs produces. A null simulation obeying μ(x) exactly reproduces them.
   - The headline tests of drive and brake against the ring test the analyst's own premise, not any GFD claim.
4. **On the framework's own definition** (drive and brake happen in the interior TetB at the drain), there is no framework reason to expect them to correlate with a galaxy's share inside the ring. No GFD paper claims they do. The "ring as quiet throat" wording mixes up the ring (the point where the interaction core peaks, out in the disk) with the throat (the Field Origin, at the drain).
5. **The CamB correction is right on its substance.** The old values match neighbouring table-1 columns to 4 significant figures, and the new values are exactly Gravis's. The regenerated Figure 2, however, shows a problem the letter should address: the Newtonian baryonic curve now lies above the observed velocities inside about 1 kpc.

**M1. Bootstrap bug in `referee_checks.py`; the README quotes the buggy intervals.**

  | Variant | Committed interval | Corrected interval |

**M2. The "slip away from the ring" is largely a binning artefact, and `referee_review.md` m4 is wrong.**
- **Where:**
  - `ROLL_RATIO.md` T2/T3 and "What it does not show";
  - `README.md` table row "Far from the ring the measured shares saturate less than μ(x)" and Next item 4;
  - `referee_review.md` m4: errors "do not change medians at this sample size".
- **What I found:** binning on x = g_obs/a0 while plotting μ_obs = g_bar/g_obs puts the same g_obs error on both axes. That biases the binned medians away from saturation on both sides (regression dilution). The null simulation `/tmp/rev/scripts/sim_slip.py` works as follows:
  - g_bar is SPARC's, at the same 149 galaxies and radii;
  - g_obs is generated from μ(x) exactly;
  - each point gets its own velocity error, plus 0.05–0.10 dex of per-galaxy scatter (distance and inclination);
  - the analyst's own `binned` and `cross_half` functions are then run on the simulated data.

  | | log x = −1.12 | −0.88 | ±0.12 | +0.62 | +0.88 | +1.12 | Crossing |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Null, 0.10 dex | +0.43 | +0.17 | ≈ −0.01 to −0.05 | −0.22 | −0.42 | −0.62 | x = 1.07 |
  | Measured (T3) | +0.61 | +0.30 | −0.03 | −0.16 | −0.25 | −0.62 | x = 1.03 |

  The sign pattern, the rough size and the "flat across the ring" behaviour are all reproduced with no departure from μ.
- **Consequence:** the analyst underclaims the agreement with μ and overclaims a physical "slip". Pointwise "slip" should not be read as interior activity without forward-modelling the errors.
- **Type:** error (statistical).

## Item 4: the white ring at x = 1

### What the GFD papers claim

**Letter 3, §"The partition in space" and Fig. 2:**
- The ring marks μ = ½, where both projections contribute equally.
- Inside it, the Christoffel (TetA) sector dominates ("general relativity is accurate and Keplerian decline holds"). Outside it, the torsion (TetB) sector dominates.
- It is the MOND transition scale a0 and the "geometric midpoint" of the partition, where Ω = 1.
- It is a "morphological boundary between a concentrated interior and an extended exterior". The "disk-and-bulge configuration" is "emergent", the "prediction for the field configuration of any bound mass", with the crossover "independent of galaxy mass or morphology".
- The logistic transition μ(1−μ) peaks there at ¼, where the continuous analogue of the s = 0 *interaction core* is "fully realised".
- Letter 3 also says "the interior drives the field configuration and the exterior continues that drive". This describes the partition itself, which is the same for every galaxy. It is not about M_dec versus M_phot.

**Letter 2:** x = 1 is the "parity midpoint", where TetA and TetB contribute ½ each and the torsion-free branch misses half the response. It is also where the partition shift is maximal (logistic law).

**Decoding-mass letter:** says nothing about the ring. It places the deficit in the "throat interior" and the "drain beneath" ("structures whose astrophysical counterparts reside at the centers of spiral galaxies").

No paper says the ring is a throat or Field Origin, a stellar bulge–disk boundary, or a place where drive or brake happens.

### Where the tested hypotheses came from

| Hypothesis | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **H1:** ring = photometric bulge–disk boundary | A literal reading of Letter 3's "disk-and-bulge morphology" | Has a textual hook in the paper, but Letter 3 is describing the *field-share* morphology. The failure (bulge–disk radius 0.48 dex inside the ring; reproduced) tests the wording, not the theory. The analyst's conclusion that Letter 3's phrase should say "field morphology, not stellar structure" is fair. |
| **H2/H2′/H3:** drive/brake correlates with the share inside the ring | Analyst. Introduced from the analyst's early "drive = TetB faster than TetA" reading (LEMMA (f), 1e8276a), combined with the roulette's TetB turning faster at x > 1 | Not in any paper. The analyst later conceded that this rate difference is the quiet baseline. |
| **"Drive inside the ring, drag outside"** | Analyst (same origin) | Not in any paper. Letter 3's "interior drives the field configuration" is about the universal partition, not drive/brake. |
| **"The ring as quiet throat"** | Analyst | Not in any paper. It conflicts with Letter 3 (core ≠ Field Origin) and with the framework (throat = drain). |
| **Ring = where the chambers turn at one rate** | Analyst's reading of the author's roulette (equal radii at x = 1) | Consistent with the roulette, but adds nothing beyond μ(1) = ½. |

### Did those tests test framework claims?

H1 tested a literal reading of a paper phrase. H2, H2′ and H3 tested the analyst's own premise. The measured-partition test (T1–T3) does test a paper claim, that the shares follow μ(x) and cross at x = 1. Because a0 was calibrated on SPARC and binning on a noisy variable produces the observed pattern (M2, M4), the test is weak, and its "departures" are mostly not evidence of anything.

### Is there a framework reason to expect drive/brake to follow the share inside the ring?

No.
- Under the stated definitions, drive and brake happen in the interior TetB at the drain. Their observable is a galaxy-level M_dec above or below M_phot.
- μ(x) is the TetA share at every radius and is already built into the decode. A quiet galaxy has M_dec = M_phot at every radius, whatever its share inside the ring, so the ring cannot itself produce a gap.
- The share of baryonic mass inside the ring measures how much of the disk is above a0, that is, its compactness or surface density. It does not measure interior activity.
- Any correlation found would most likely come from acceleration-dependent systematics. D1 shows exactly this: ρ falls from +0.47 to +0.16 with SPARC's thin-disk g_bar.
- If one wanted a framework-motivated galaxy property to test, it would be a measure tied to the drain or centre. The analyst's own "Next" items point there. The ring share is not such a measure.
