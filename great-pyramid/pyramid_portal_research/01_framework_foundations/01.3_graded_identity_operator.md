## The graded identity operator

Let $P_0, P_1, P_2$ be the canonical projectors on the three summands of $\mathcal C_K = \Lambda^K V \oplus V \oplus (V \otimes V)$:

- $P_0$: projector onto $\Lambda^K V$, dimension 1
- $P_1$: projector onto $V$, dimension $K$
- $P_2$: projector onto $V \otimes V$, dimension $K^2$

Define the **graded identity** parametrized by $s$:

$$\boxed{\;\mathbb I_s \;=\; P_0 \;+\; s\, P_1 \;+\; P_2,\qquad s \in \{+1,\, 0,\, -1\}\;}$$

The three values of $s$ correspond to the three natural operators on a $\mathbb Z/2$-representation:

| $s$ | Operator on $\mathcal C_K$ | Geometric reading |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| $+1$ | identity on $\mathcal C_K$ | **TetA** — full identity |
| $0$ | projector onto parity-invariant subspace $\Lambda^K V \oplus (V \otimes V)$ | **Field Origin** |
| $-1$ | parity operator (sign-flip on $V$) | **TetB** — parity-graded read |

Each value of $s$ produces a different read of the same closure-read representation.

## Theorem 1 — The parity-graded character

For $\mathbb I_s$ as defined above:

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_s) \;=\; 1 + sK + K^2\;}$$

**Proof.** Linearity of trace plus dimension count:

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_s) \;=\; \operatorname{Tr}(P_0) + s\,\operatorname{Tr}(P_1) + \operatorname{Tr}(P_2) \;=\; \dim(\Lambda^K V) + s\cdot\dim(V) + \dim(V\otimes V) \;=\; 1 + sK + K^2 \quad\square$$

**Evaluation at K=4:**

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{+1}) \;=\; 1 + 4 + 16 \;=\; 21$$
$$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{0}) \;=\; 1 + 0 + 16 \;=\; 17$$
$$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{-1}) \;=\; 1 - 4 + 16 \;=\; 13$$

These are exactly the three integers $\Phi_3(4), \Phi_4(4), \Phi_6(4)$.

**Identity (immediate):**

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{+1}) \;=\; \Phi_3(K), \qquad \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{0}) \;=\; \Phi_4(K), \qquad \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb I_{-1}) \;=\; \Phi_6(K)$$

The three traces are exactly the three degree-2 cyclotomic polynomial evaluations.

## Theorem 2 — The det-trace identity

For any operator $A$ on a finite-dimensional space and any $\alpha > 0$:

$$\boxed{\;\det(\alpha^A) \;=\; \alpha^{\operatorname{Tr}(A)}\;}$$

**Proof.** Using $\alpha^A = e^{A \ln \alpha}$ and the standard identity $\det(e^M) = e^{\operatorname{Tr}(M)}$:

$$\det(\alpha^A) \;=\; \det(e^{A \ln \alpha}) \;=\; e^{\operatorname{Tr}(A) \ln \alpha} \;=\; \alpha^{\operatorname{Tr}(A)} \quad\square$$

This is standard linear algebra.

**Applied to the graded identity at K=4:**

$$\det(\alpha^{\mathbb I_{+1}}) \;=\; \alpha^{21}$$
$$\det(\alpha^{\mathbb I_{0}}) \;=\; \alpha^{17}$$
$$\det(\alpha^{\mathbb I_{-1}}) \;=\; \alpha^{13}$$

These are the three Coulomb attenuation factors that combine to produce the gravity coupling.

## Why three sweeps and not two or four

The integer 3 comes from the three summands of $\mathcal C_K = \Lambda^K V \oplus V \oplus (V \otimes V)$ — three isotypic components under the $\mathbb Z/2$ parity action.

Three sweeps correspond to three signatures $s \in \{+1, 0, -1\}$, which are the three natural operators on a $\mathbb Z/2$ representation:

| Operator | Signature | Reading |
|---|:---:|---|
| identity | $+1$ | full read |
| invariant projector | $0$ | parity-even read |
| parity operator | $-1$ | parity-odd subtracted |

A two-sweep kernel would miss one of the three readings. A four-sweep kernel would have a redundant signature. The closure-read representation forces exactly three.

## The $\alpha^7$ per-sweep exponent

The per-sweep exponent is $K + D - 1 = 7$ at $K=4, D=3$. The framework reads this as:
- $K = 4$: frame dimension
- $D = 3$: spatial dimension
- $-1$: subtraction of one "trivial" mode (the orientation/scalar)

So $K + D - 1 = 7$ corresponds to the non-trivial spatial modes accessible to one Hadamard sweep on the K-dim frame across the D-dim base, minus one trivial. This counts as $7$ at the framework's natural dimensions.
