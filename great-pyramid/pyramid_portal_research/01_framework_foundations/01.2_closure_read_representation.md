## The three primary inputs

The framework has exactly three primary inputs. Everything else is forced.

### Input 1 — The tetrad equation

$$g_{\mu\nu} \;=\; \eta_{ab}\, e^a_\mu\, e^b_\nu$$

This is general relativity in tetrad form (Einstein 1928, MTW §10). The metric tensor is expressed as a bilinear contraction of frame fields $e^a_\mu$ with the Minkowski metric $\eta_{ab}$.

### Input 2 — The parity action on the internal frame

$$e^a_\mu \;\longmapsto\; -e^a_\mu$$

A discrete $\mathbb Z/2$ action on the internal frame space. The tetrad equation is *invariant* under this action because $g$ is bilinear in $e$. But the framework reads the action's *induced decomposition* of the tetrad's closure-read representation.

### Input 3 — The uniqueness condition

$$2(D+1) \;=\; 2^D$$

This equation has a **unique** positive-integer solution: $D = 3$, hence $K = D + 1 = 4$. This forces the framework's spatial dimension (3) and frame dimension (4).

**Proof of uniqueness.** Check small $D$:

| $D$ | $2(D+1)$ | $2^D$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 4 | 2 |
| 2 | 6 | 4 |
| **3** | **8** | **8** ✓ |
| 4 | 10 | 16 |
| 5 | 12 | 32 |

For $D \ge 4$, $2^D$ grows exponentially while $2(D+1)$ grows linearly; no further solutions. $\square$

## Constructing the closure-read representation

From the tetrad equation's bilinearity in $e$, define the **closure-read representation** of the internal frame space $V = \mathbb R^K$:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal C_K \;=\; \underbrace{\Lambda^K V}_{\text{orientation}} \;\oplus\; \underbrace{V}_{\text{frame}} \;\oplus\; \underbrace{V \otimes V}_{\text{ordered bilinear}}\;}$$

The three summands are the **isotypic components** of the $\mathbb Z/2$ parity action on $\mathcal C_K$:

| Summand | Dimension | Parity at even $K$ | Geometric reading |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| $\Lambda^K V$ | $1$ | invariant ($(-1)^K = +1$) | top form / pseudoscalar |
| $V$ | $K$ | flipped ($-1$ under parity) | frame vector |
| $V \otimes V$ | $K^2$ | invariant (bilinear) | ordered bilinear |

**No choices are made in this decomposition.** The three summands are the canonical isotypic components of the parity action.

## Total dimension

$$\dim \mathcal C_K \;=\; 1 + K + K^2$$

At $K = 4$:

$$\boxed{\;\dim \mathcal C_4 \;=\; 1 + 4 + 16 \;=\; 21 \;=\; \Phi_3(4)\;}$$

The total dimension of the closure-read representation at K=4 is the framework's 21-band integer.

## Why the bilinear summand is $V \otimes V$ and not $\text{Sym}^2 V$

The tetrad equation is bilinear in $e$ but the two factors $e^a_\mu$ and $e^b_\nu$ are independently labeled (different spacetime indices $\mu, \nu$). They are not symmetrized. The closure-read representation therefore uses the full tensor product $V \otimes V$ of dimension $K^2$, not the symmetric part $\text{Sym}^2 V$ of dimension $K(K+1)/2$.

This distinction is load-bearing: at $K = 4$, $\text{Sym}^2 V$ has dimension 10, while $V \otimes V$ has dimension 16. The framework requires the latter.

## The orientation summand $\Lambda^K V$

$\Lambda^K V$ is the top exterior power of $V$ — the 1-dimensional space of $K$-forms on $V$. It carries the orientation of the frame.

At $K = 4$: $\Lambda^4 \mathbb R^4 \cong \mathbb R$, dimension 1.

The orientation summand has parity action $(-1)^K$. At even $K$ (including $K = 4$), this is $+1$, so $\Lambda^K V$ is parity-invariant.

## The frame summand $V$

The bare frame $V = \mathbb R^K$ is the natural 1-tensor representation. Dimension $K$.

The parity action acts as $-1$ on $V$ — frame vectors flip under $e \to -e$. So $V$ is the parity-odd summand.

At $K = 4$: $V \cong \mathbb R^4$, dimension 4.

## The full parity decomposition

| Parity sector | Summands | Dimension at K=4 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Parity-invariant (+) | $\Lambda^K V \oplus (V \otimes V)$ | $1 + 16 = 17$ |
| Parity-odd (−) | $V$ | $4$ |

This is where the integers 13, 17, 21 come from. Section `01.3_graded_identity_operator.md` constructs the operator that reads each parity sector and produces all three integers via traces.

- Trace of identity on parity-graded ("$V$-subtracted"): $1 + K^2 - K = 13$

These three traces are exactly $\Phi_3(4), \Phi_4(4), \Phi_6(4)$.

## Why parity flips $V$ and not $V \otimes V$

The parity action $e \to -e$ on $V$ acts as $-1$ on each frame vector.

On $V \otimes V$, the parity action is $(e \otimes e') \to (-e) \otimes (-e') = (+1) e \otimes e'$. So $V \otimes V$ is parity-*invariant* (bilinearity squares the sign).

On $\Lambda^K V$, the parity action is $e^1 \wedge \dots \wedge e^K \to (-e^1) \wedge \dots \wedge (-e^K) = (-1)^K e^1 \wedge \dots \wedge e^K$. At even $K$, this is $+1$.

So only $V$ itself is parity-odd. This is what makes $\{13, 17, 21\}$ asymmetric: removing the parity-odd $V$ from the trace gives 13; including it with weight zero gives 17; including it fully gives 21.
