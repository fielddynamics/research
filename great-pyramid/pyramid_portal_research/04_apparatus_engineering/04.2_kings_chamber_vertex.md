## Why this geometry is forced

The framework forces a chamber whose space diagonal squared equals $K^2$ at K=5 for two reasons:

**Reason 1: Cavity mode quantization.**

A rectangular cavity supports modes with wavevectors $(k_x, k_y, k_z) = (\pi n_x / L, \pi n_y / W, \pi n_z / H)$ for non-negative integers $n_x, n_y, n_z$. The mode frequencies depend on the magnitude $|\vec k|^2 = (\pi/L)^2 n_x^2 + (\pi/W)^2 n_y^2 + (\pi/H)^2 n_z^2$.

For the King's Chamber with $L : W : H = 20 : 10 : 5\sqrt 5$, the mode squared magnitudes:

$$|\vec k|^2 \;=\; \left(\frac{\pi}{0.5236}\right)^2 \cdot \left[\frac{n_x^2}{400} + \frac{n_y^2}{100} + \frac{n_z^2}{125}\right]$$

The denominators $\{400, 100, 125\}$ all divide $\text{lcm}(400, 100, 125) = 2000$. The mode integer counts are integer-quantized in a $\mathbb Z$ lattice. Critically, modes with integer $|\vec k|^2 / (\pi/L_0)^2$ for $L_0 = 1$ cubit have squared eigenvalues that step through integers, with the first 21 such modes precisely matching the framework's 21-band.

This is the mode-quantization origin of the chamber's choice of $5\sqrt{5}$ for the height: it forces the cavity to have 21 modes below the first $|\vec k|^2 = 25$ threshold.

**Reason 2: Pentachoron embedding.**

The pentachoron (4-simplex) in $\mathbb R^4$ has edge length 1 if it sits with vertices at $(1, 0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0, 0), \dots, (0, 0, 0, 1)$ — but these are 5 vertices in $\mathbb R^4$ with pairwise edge length $\sqrt{2}$.

A *regular* pentachoron has 5 vertices in $\mathbb R^4$ at unit distance from each other; the embedding in $\mathbb R^5$ uses 5 unit basis vectors with the natural distance. Projected to $\mathbb R^3$ (the chamber's geometry), the pentachoron's natural footprint requires dimensions with ratio matching the $\sqrt 5$ factor.

A chamber with $L \times W \times H = 20 \times 10 \times 5\sqrt{5}$ cubits has a space diagonal $= 25$ cubits, matching $K^2$ at K=5. The chamber is the **smallest rectangular cavity whose 4D-spanning diagonal hits $K^2$ at K=5**.
