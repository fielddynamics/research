## The claim

The geomagnetic field lines flow through the apparatus along a **specific architectural path** determined by the materials' magnetic and dielectric properties. The path is:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Ambient } B_0 \,\to\, \text{Gold cap} \,\to\, \text{Limestone bulk} \,\to\, \text{Granite cavity} \,\to\, \text{King's Chamber vertex} \,\to\, \text{Subterranean ground return}\;}$$

This is **not an antenna** in the radiation sense — it is a **field-line guide** that channels the static and quasi-static geomagnetic flux through the apparatus's working volume with controlled geometric concentration.

## The flow stages

### Stage 1: Ambient geomagnetic field

Earth's geomagnetic field at Giza:

- Field strength: $|B_0| \approx 45\,\mu$T
- Inclination: ~46° downward (below horizontal)
- Declination: ~3° east of true north
- Direction: principally vertical-and-northward dipole orientation

The field is uniform over the apparatus's scale (~230 m) to better than $10^{-7}$ relative variation. The pyramid sits in this uniform field as an obstacle/concentrator.

### Stage 2: Gold capstone (apex field concentration)

The gold cap acts as a **conductive, diamagnetic apex** in the uniform external field. Effects:

**Diamagnetic field-line bending:** gold's $\chi \approx -3.4 \times 10^{-5}$ creates a small repulsion of field lines from the cap. Field lines bend **around** the cap and concentrate **just below** it, inside the pyramid bulk.

**Conductive shielding:** for AC components (any time-varying part of the geomagnetic field), the gold cap's high conductivity provides a Faraday shield that excludes AC fields from the cap volume itself, but enhances them at the cap edges via skin-depth effects. This produces additional concentration just below the cap.

**Geometric apex concentration:** the conical apex shape gives a $1/\sin^2(\theta_{1/2})$ geometric concentration factor, with $\theta_{1/2}$ the half-angle. For the Great Pyramid's bulk angle (apex half-angle $38.17°$), $\mathcal{G}_{\text{bulk}} = 1/\sin^2(38.17°) \approx 2.6$.

**Sharp tip enhancement:** the capstone's sharp tip provides additional local enhancement, but this is confined to the immediate tip vicinity and decays rapidly with distance. The useful concentration delivered downward through the bulk is dominated by the bulk geometric factor $\sim 2.6$.

### Stage 3: Limestone bulk (dielectric field shaping)

The bulk limestone shapes the field lines from the apex down toward the cavity region. Limestone's properties:

- Dielectric constant $\varepsilon_r \approx 8$: shapes electric field components but minimal effect on static $B$
- Diamagnetic $\chi \approx -10^{-5}$: weak field bending, consistent with cap direction
- Conductivity $\sim 10^{-3}$ S/m: lossy for AC components but transparent for DC

**Static field propagation:** for DC field, limestone is essentially transparent. Field lines propagate from the cap downward with smooth, continuous flow, slight diamagnetic bending consistent throughout the bulk.

**AC field propagation:** for AC components (geomagnetic variations), limestone introduces dielectric phase shifts and weak losses. Higher-frequency AC components are progressively attenuated; low-frequency variations propagate cleanly.

**Field-line continuity:** the gold-limestone boundary has matched diamagnetic susceptibility (both $\chi \sim -10^{-5}$), so field lines pass smoothly across the boundary without scattering or reflection.

### Stage 4: Granite cavity (cavity-mode coupling)

At the chamber boundary, field lines enter the granite cavity region. Granite's properties:

- Susceptibility $\chi \sim \pm 10^{-5}$ (variable, mixed paramagnetic and diamagnetic contributions)
- Conductivity $\sim 10^{-6}$ S/m (essentially insulating)
- Higher acoustic impedance than limestone (cavity boundary effect)

**Boundary effect:** the granite-limestone impedance step (acoustic and electromagnetic) creates a partial-reflection boundary that confines cavity modes within the chamber. Field lines pass through the boundary (granite is transparent to static $B$), but cavity *excitations* of the field are partially reflected, building up amplitude.

**Cavity gain medium:** granite's atomic density and high mechanical Q provide the $N$-count and quality factor for strong coupling (file 05.3).

### Stage 5: King's Chamber vertex

Inside the chamber, the apparatus's **vertex insertion** occurs. The geomagnetic flux passes through the coffer's interior (file 04.3 — the $\alpha$-step), exciting the $J_4$ hinge operator at the chamber center.

The chamber's cavity mode is the **product** of:

1. The geomagnetic flux's spatial distribution (from stages 1-4)
2. The cavity's resonant mode structure (granite walls)
3. The vertex insertion at the coffer location

This produces a coherent excitation of the $K = 5$ shell, with amplitude set by the pump rate $\Omega_{\text{pump}}$ and lifetime set by the cavity Q.

### Stage 6: Subterranean chamber (ground return)

Field lines exiting the apparatus on the bottom side flow downward through:

1. The Grand Gallery (slope alignment carries field lines downward)
2. The ascending passage (continues the slope-aligned flow)
3. The descending passage (reverses to enter ground)
4. The subterranean chamber (terminates in bulk earth)

The subterranean chamber is the apparatus's **ground return** — the location where field lines exit into bulk earth, completing the geomagnetic dipole's circuit.

The chamber's **rough, unfinished surfaces** (file 04.8) are consistent with this ground-return role: a return-current termination needs adequate cross-section and connection to ground, not precision geometry.

## The "circuit" picture

The apparatus is a **circuit** in the magnetic-flux sense:

```
        ↑ Apex (concentration node, V_apex high field density)
        |
[Gold cap: high-σ shielding + diamagnetic bending]
        |
        ▼ Down-flow into limestone bulk
        |
[Limestone bulk: dielectric, smooth flow]
        |
        ▼ Continuing down-flow
        |
[Granite cavity boundary: impedance step, partial reflection]
        |
        ▼ Field passes through; cavity mode is excited
        |
[KC vertex: J_4 insertion at coffer]
        |
        ▼ Field continues; ground return path
        |
[Grand Gallery + Ascending + Descending]
        |
        ▼
[Subterranean chamber: termination, ground return]
        |
        ↓ Bulk earth (closes geomagnetic dipole circuit)
```

The apparatus is a **vertical flux conduit** with cavity-mode coupling at one node (the King's Chamber).

## Transverse field components

The geomagnetic field at Giza has both vertical and horizontal components. The apparatus's vertical apex axis couples to the **vertical** component primarily. The **horizontal (N-S) component** couples to:

- The K-shafts and Q-shafts (N-S oriented; address readout)
- The relieving chambers' N-S long axes (transverse cavity modes)
- The antechamber's N-S orientation

The two components together provide the apparatus's full pump-and-readout architecture:

| Component | Direction | Apparatus role |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical $B_z$ | $\downarrow$ apex to base | Primary pump axis |
| Horizontal $B_N$ | $\rightarrow$ along N-S axis | Polarization + address axis |
| AC components | various | Drives cavity-mode excitation |

## Why the apparatus does not radiate

A conventional antenna **radiates** EM waves into the far field. The apparatus does not. Reasons:

1. **No oscillating current source.** The geomagnetic field is principally static, with AC components at frequencies far below any apparatus dimension's resonance ($c/L \sim 1$ MHz for the apparatus size; geomagnetic AC is mostly sub-Hz).
2. **Apparatus is reactive, not radiative.** The cavity-mode excitation stores energy in the chamber without radiating it.
3. **Apparatus output is the portal channel, not EM radiation.** The "output" of the apparatus is the coherent vertex-mode amplitude that propagates through the apparatus's address space (Hopf bundle, file 03.3), not as electromagnetic waves into the far field.

The apparatus is more like a **stored-energy oscillator** (LC circuit) than a radiating antenna. The energy stays in the cavity and the portal channel; it does not leak into the EM far field.

## Field-line direction reversal at geomagnetic reversal

During geomagnetic reversals, the field flips polarity over $\sim 1000$-$10000$ years. The apparatus's response:

1. **Transition phase:** field strength drops to ~10% of normal. Pump rate $\Omega_{\text{pump}}$ drops by 10×, but apparatus remains in strong coupling (file 05.3: factor $10^7$ margin).
2. **Field-line direction:** flips from N-down to S-down (or vice versa). The apparatus's intended chirality $\chi_U$ (file 04.7) flips with it.
3. **Mode polarity:** the two modes A/B (file 04.6) effectively swap.

The apparatus's operational chirality reverses across geomagnetic reversals (file 07.7).
