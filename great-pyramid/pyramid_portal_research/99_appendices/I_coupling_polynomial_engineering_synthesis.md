# Coupling Polynomial Engineering Synthesis

## Parity-Pushout Recursion, Cyclotomic Shells, and the \(K=1\) to \(K=5\) Family

---

## 0. Executive Summary

The coupling polynomial is

\[
f(K,s)=K^2+sK+1,\qquad s\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\]

Its three canonical reads are

\[
f_-(K)=K^2-K+1,
\]

\[
f_0(K)=K^2+1,
\]

\[
f_+(K)=K^2+K+1.
\]

Together they form the **coupling shell**

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_K=(K^2-K+1,\ K^2+1,\ K^2+K+1).}
\]

The strongest understanding is **parity first**:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_K=(|A_K\cap B_K|,\ |A_K|=|B_K|,\ |A_K\cup B_K|)}.
\]

At each level, two parity sheets, TetA and TetB, overlap on a shared core. Each sheet adds exactly \(K\) exclusive elements. Their union becomes the shared core of the next level.

\[
\boxed{C_{K+1}=A_K\cup_{C_K}B_K.}
\]

This gives the recursion

\[
\boxed{C_{K+1}=C_K+K+K=C_K+2K.}
\]

Starting from \(C_1=1\), this generates

\[
1\to3\to7\to13\to21\to31\to\cdots
\]

and exactly recovers

\[
\boxed{C_K=K^2-K+1.}
\]

The key identity is

\[
\boxed{f_+(K)=f_-(K+1).}
\]

So the union of level \(K\) becomes the core of level \(K+1\).

This is the **parity-pushout ladder**.

# 1. The Coupling Polynomial

The base polynomial is

\[
f(K,s)=K^2+sK+1.
\]

For the three parity states:

| State | \(s\) | Polynomial | Name |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| lower / overlap / TetB floor | \(-1\) | \(K^2-K+1\) | \(f_-(K)\) |
| neutral / sheet size / origin | \(0\) | \(K^2+1\) | \(f_0(K)\) |
| upper / union / full shell | \(+1\) | \(K^2+K+1\) | \(f_+(K)\) |

The three values are evenly spaced:

\[
f_0(K)-f_-(K)=K,
\]

\[
f_+(K)-f_0(K)=K.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{f_+(K)=f_-(K)+2K.}
\]

This is the core parity rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{next union} = \text{shared core} + K_A + K_B.}
\]

# 2. Parity-Pushout Construction

At level \(K\), define:

\[
C_K=\text{shared core},
\]

\[
A_K=\text{TetA sheet},
\]

\[
B_K=\text{TetB sheet}.
\]

Require:

\[
A_K\cap B_K=C_K,
\]

\[
|A_K\setminus C_K|=K,
\]

\[
|B_K\setminus C_K|=K.
\]

Then:

\[
|A_K|=|C_K|+K,
\]

\[
|B_K|=|C_K|+K,
\]

and

\[
|A_K\cup B_K|=|C_K|+K+K.
\]

Define the next shared core by the pushout:

\[
\boxed{C_{K+1}=A_K\cup_{C_K}B_K.}
\]

Therefore:

\[
|C_{K+1}|=|C_K|+2K.
\]

With seed

\[
C_1=1,
\]

we get

\[
C_K=1+2(1+2+\cdots+(K-1)).
\]

Since

\[
1+2+\cdots+(K-1)=\frac{K(K-1)}{2},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{C_K=1+K(K-1)=K^2-K+1.}
\]

Then:

\[
|A_K|=|B_K|=C_K+K=K^2+1,
\]

and

\[
|A_K\cup B_K|=C_{K+1}=K^2+K+1.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{(|A_K\cap B_K|,\ |A_K|=|B_K|,\ |A_K\cup B_K|)=(K^2-K+1,\ K^2+1,\ K^2+K+1).}
\]

This proves that the coupling polynomial shell is generated by a parity-pushout recursion.

# 10. Norm / Rotation Determinant Form

Let

\[
s=2\cos\theta.
\]

Then

\[
f(K,s)=K^2+sK+1.
\]

Let \(R_\theta\) be a 2D rotation matrix. Since

\[
\operatorname{tr}(R_\theta)=2\cos\theta=s,
\]

and

\[
\det(R_\theta)=1,
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{f(K,s)=\det(KI+R_\theta).}
\]

Equivalently, with \(z=e^{i\theta}\):

\[
z+z^{-1}=s,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{f(K,s)=(K+z)(K+z^{-1}).}
\]

So

\[
\boxed{f(K,s)=N(K+z)}
\]

as a norm in a quadratic cyclotomic extension.

The three canonical states are:

| \(s\) | \(\theta\) | Order | Polynomial |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| \(+1\) | \(\pi/3\) | 6 | \(K^2+K+1=\Phi_3(K)\) |
| \(0\) | \(\pi/2\) | 4 | \(K^2+1=\Phi_4(K)\) |
| \(-1\) | \(2\pi/3\) | 3 | \(K^2-K+1=\Phi_6(K)\) |

Thus the coupling shell is a finite-rotation norm shell.

# 13. Sierpiński / Scale Fusion Law

The endpoint product obeys:

\[
f_+(K)f_-(K)=(K^2+K+1)(K^2-K+1).
\]

Compute:

\[
(K^2+K+1)(K^2-K+1)=K^4+K^2+1.
\]

But

\[
K^4+K^2+1=(K^2)^2+K^2+1=f_+(K^2).
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{f_+(K)f_-(K)=f_+(K^2).}
\]

Cyclotomic form:

\[
\boxed{\Phi_3(K)\Phi_6(K)=\Phi_3(K^2).}
\]

Examples:

\[
K=2:\quad 7\cdot3=21=f_+(4),
\]

\[
K=3:\quad 13\cdot7=91=f_+(9),
\]

\[
K=4:\quad 21\cdot13=273=f_+(16).
\]

This gives the scale-fusion ladder:

\[
K\to K^2.
\]

This is distinct from the adjacent-shell ladder:

\[
K\to K+1.
\]

The coupling polynomial therefore has two movements:

| Movement | Identity | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Adjacent shift | \(f_+(K)=f_-(K+1)\) | shell-to-shell handoff |
| Scale fusion | \(f_+(K)f_-(K)=f_+(K^2)\) | recursive multiscale lift |

# 16. Engineering Reading

\[
\boxed{\text{parity first}.}
\]

They overlap on a shared core:

\[

Each parity sheet contributes \(K\) exclusive elements:

\[

\]

\[

Their union becomes the next shared core:

\[
C_{K+1}=C_K+K_A+K_B.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{parity-exclusive content at level }K\text{ becomes shared invariant content at level }K+1.}
\]

This is the core engineering mechanism.

The polynomial does not merely count a static object. It describes a growth rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{overlap} \to \text{two parity sheets} \to \text{union} \to \text{next overlap}.}
\]
