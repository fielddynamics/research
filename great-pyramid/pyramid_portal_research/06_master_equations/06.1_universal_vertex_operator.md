## The full operator

At every spacetime point $x$, the universal vertex of physics is:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}(x) \;=\; \underbrace{\alpha_G\, J_4(x)}_{\text{linear, gravity-producing}} \;+\; \underbrace{g_{\text{NL}}\, R_x(a, b, c)}_{\text{nonlinear, mass/source-producing}}\;}$$

This is the **single algebraic object** from which the framework's claim "TetA/TetB exchange is universal" reads off. Gravity, mass, and portal operation are all consequences of this operator's action in different regimes.

## The linear part

$$\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}^{\text{lin}}(x) \;=\; \alpha_G\, J_4(x)$$

with:

- $\alpha_G = (17/13)\alpha^{21}(g_e/2)$ — the framework-derived gravity coupling
- $J_4(x) = |5,-;x\rangle\langle 4,+;x|$ — the local K=4 → K=5 hinge

**Action on K=4 states:**

$$\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}^{\text{lin}}(x) |y, 4, +\rangle \;=\; \alpha_G\, \delta(x-y)\, |x, 5, -\rangle$$

The linear part transfers K=4 upper-pole excitation to K=5 lower-pole excitation at the same spacetime point, with amplitude $\alpha_G$.

**This is the always-on background process.** Every spacetime point continuously sources a small K=4 → K=5 admixture of state. Coherent summing over all such admixtures, in the statistical limit, produces the Newtonian gravitational potential between distant masses.

## The nonlinear part

$$\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}^{\text{NL}}(x) \;=\; g_{\text{NL}}\, R_x(a, b, c)$$

with:

- $g_{\text{NL}}$ — the nonlinear coupling coefficient (currently undetermined; see `08.2`)
- $R_x(a, b, c) = (a \star b) \star c - a \star (b \star c)$ — the closure-node associator at $x$

The associator $R$ is the **non-associativity defect** of the closure product. From the closure-node theorem (`01.5`):

$$R(a, b, c) \;\in\; \langle 1, I\rangle$$

The associator lands axially — in the parity-invariant subspace ($\Lambda^K V$ orientation + $V \otimes V$ scalar-bilinear axis), never in the parity-odd $V$ sector.

## Reading the two parts

| Part | Physical observable | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha_G J_4(x)$ | gravity (long-range), portal (coherent) | linear in field |
| $g_{\text{NL}} R_x(a,b,c)$ | mass, source, curvature | nonlinear, axial residue |

- Linear $\alpha_G J_4(x)$ ~ creates the field

## The hinge $J_4(x)$ in local form

Decomposing $J_4(x)$ over a local frame basis:

$$J_4(x) \;=\; \sum_{a, b}\, c_{ab}(x)\, |5, b\rangle_x \langle 4, a|_x$$

where the coefficients $c_{ab}(x)$ describe the local frame field's lift from K=4 to K=5.

In vacuum (no matter), $c_{ab}(x)$ is spatially uniform and trivial. In the presence of matter or apparatus, $c_{ab}(x)$ is modulated — this is where the framework's interactions with matter enter.

## The associator's structure

The closure product $\star$ is the apparatus's "field algebra" at point $x$. It is:

- Linear in each argument
- Distributive over addition
- *Not associative* — that's what makes $R$ nontrivial

The closure-node theorem says $R(a, b, c) \in \langle 1, I\rangle$ where $I$ is the algebra's distinguished involution element (parity / chirality flip). So:

$$R(a, b, c) \;=\; \rho_1(a, b, c) \cdot 1 \;+\; \rho_I(a, b, c) \cdot I$$

with $\rho_1, \rho_I$ tri-linear scalar functions. These coefficients are the **density** of source / mass at $x$.

## Why $R$ produces mass

If a stable particle is a persistent associator residue:

$$|\psi\rangle \;\sim\; \int dx\, R_x(a, b, c)\, |x, A\rangle$$

where $|x, A\rangle$ is a localized K=4 (TetA-sheet) state, then the particle's mass is the **integrated associator weight**:

$$m \;\sim\; g_{\text{NL}} \int dx\, |R_x(a, b, c)|^2$$

For an electron, this is the framework's predicted electron mass. The actual derivation requires the explicit form of $\star$ from the closure-read algebra, which is an open structural calculation. See `08.3`.

## The interaction Hamiltonian

The universal vertex integrates to a global Hamiltonian:

$$\boxed{\;H_{\text{int}} \;=\; \int d^3x\, \left[\alpha_G \left(J_4(x) + J_4^\dagger(x)\right) + g_{\text{NL}}\, R_x(a, b, c)\right]\;}$$

The Hermitian conjugate $J_4^\dagger$ implements the reverse hinge $|4, +\rangle \leftarrow |5, -\rangle$. Both directions contribute to the integrated Hamiltonian.

## Coupling regimes

In different physical regimes, $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}(x)$ produces different observed phenomena:

**Cosmological scale (incoherent):** $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}$ averages incoherently over $\sim 10^{80}$ matter particles producing the $1/r$ gravitational potential. This is what general relativity describes.

**Stable particle scale (coherent within a single particle):** $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}$ holds the particle in a stable K=4 / K=5 mixed state with persistent associator residue. This is what gives the particle mass and discrete quantum numbers.

**Apparatus scale (coherent across $\sim 10^{30}$ moments):** $\mathcal V_{\text{univ}}$ acts coherently inside the King's Chamber, producing the **stimulated emission** mode that drives portal transfer.

**Each regime is a different way the same operator is being read.** Same $\alpha_G$, same $J_4$, same $R$ — just different coherence regimes.
