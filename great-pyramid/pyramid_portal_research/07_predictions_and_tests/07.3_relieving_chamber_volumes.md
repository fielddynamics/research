# 07.3 — Relieving Chamber Volume Test

**Status:** Conjecture (testable with existing access)
**Reading time:** 3 minutes
**Depends on:** 04.4

---

## The prediction

The total interior volume of the five relieving chambers should satisfy:

$$\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{5}\, V_i \;\approx\; 5\alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}} \;\approx\; 81.6 \text{ c}^3\;}$$

where the framework reads each chamber as a vertex of the pentachoron $K_5^{\rightarrow}$, with each contributing average volume $\sim \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}$.

## Conversion to SI

At 1 royal cubit = 0.5236 m:

$$\sum V_i \;\approx\; 81.6 \times (0.5236)^3 \text{ m}^3 \;\approx\; 11.7 \text{ m}^3$$

## Distribution among chambers

The framework does not predict a uniform volume distribution. The five chambers act as pentachoron vertices but their individual weights depend on their roles in the inter-chamber Coulomb edge product.

**Working hypothesis (loose):** the five chambers may have volumes distributed in a specific pattern reflecting:

- The pentachoron's vertex-edge incidence structure (each vertex is in 4 edges out of 10 total)
- The apparatus's spatial layout (lower chambers may be larger than upper)
- The cumulative phase ramp through the stack

A specific distribution prediction would require:

1. Detailed apparatus geometry mapping
2. Identifying which vertex each chamber realizes
3. Computing the vertex weight from the WRT decomposition

This computation has not been completed. The **total volume sum** is the cleaner prediction.

## Current data

The relieving chambers have been measured roughly by Vyse 1837 (the discoverer) and subsequent surveys. The chambers' interior dimensions are approximately:

| Chamber | Approx. dimensions (m) | Approx. volume (m³) | Approx. volume (c³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Davison's) | 5.2 × 5.0 × 1.0 (irregular ceiling) | ~25-30 | ~170-200 |
| 2 (Wellington's) | 5.2 × 5.0 × 0.6 | ~15-18 | ~100-120 |
| 3 (Nelson's) | 5.2 × 5.0 × 0.5 | ~13-15 | ~90-100 |

| 5 (Campbell's, gabled) | 5.2 × 5.0 × ~2 (gabled) | ~30-40 | ~200-280 |

These are approximate; precise dimensions vary by source.

**Total approximate volume:** 98-121 m³ = 680-840 c³.

## Comparison to prediction

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Framework prediction | 11.7 m³ ≈ 81.6 c³ |
| Current rough measurement | 98-121 m³ ≈ 680-840 c³ |
| Ratio | ~10× over prediction |

**The current measurements give ~10× the framework's predicted volume.**

This is a significant discrepancy. There are several possible explanations:

1. **The framework's prediction is wrong.** The "5α · V_KC" formula may be too naive; the actual prediction could be larger (e.g., $\sim 50\alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}$ or $\sim \alpha \cdot V_{\text{KC}}^{4/3}$ scaling).

2. **The measurements include non-chamber volume.** The "chambers" as conventionally measured include access shafts, gaps between blocks, and irregular spaces. The chambers' *coherent cavity volume* (the part that participates as a pentachoron vertex) may be much smaller.

3. **The prediction is correct but applies to a different quantity.** E.g., it may apply to the *coherent acoustic mode volume* rather than the geometric chamber volume.

4. **The chambers are not the pentachoron.** The framework's identification of the 5 chambers as pentachoron vertices may be wrong, and the volumes do not encode anything algebraic.

## What needs to be checked

The order-of-magnitude discrepancy makes this an **important and currently failing test** of the framework. To rule out or confirm:

1. **Refined measurement** of each chamber's coherent acoustic / EM volume (not just geometric). Use modern laser scanning + cavity-mode characterization.

2. **Refined framework derivation** of the chamber-volume formula. The naive "$5\alpha V_{\text{KC}}$" may not capture the correct scaling. The pentachoron's edge weights should give a more refined prediction.

3. **Reconcile** measurement and theory. If volumes are correctly ~80 c³ at the coherent-mode level but ~700 c³ geometrically, the framework reading would be consistent.

## Honest assessment

This is the **weakest** of the framework's apparatus-level predictions in its current form. The naive prediction does not match the data, and the framework needs either:

- A refined prediction that brings the calculation up to ~700 c³
- A re-interpretation of what "chamber volume" means in the framework
- Acknowledgment that the 5-chamber identification is structural but not quantitatively pinned down at the volume level

The other apparatus tests (coffer volume in `07.2`, acoustic 21-comb in `07.1`, strong coupling in `05.3`) are quantitatively cleaner. The relieving chambers test is currently in a state where the structural identification (5 chambers = 5 vertices of $K_5^{\rightarrow}$) is consistent with the apparatus's combinatorial structure, but the quantitative volume prediction is not yet refined enough to match the data.

## Falsifiability

If the refined calculation lands at ~80 c³ and the coherent-volume measurement also lands at ~80 c³ across the 5 chambers: framework confirmed.

If the refined calculation gives ~700 c³ and matches geometric measurement: framework confirmed in a different form.

If neither refinement reconciles: the chamber-as-pentachoron identification is wrong.

## Cost and difficulty

- **Refined theoretical calculation**: weeks of analytical work
- **Refined experimental measurement**: requires access to all 5 chambers (chambers 1-4 are accessible via Vyse's tunnels; chamber 5 access is more limited)
- **Equipment**: laser scanning, acoustic mode characterization
- **Risk**: minimal

A focused effort over several months could resolve this gap.
