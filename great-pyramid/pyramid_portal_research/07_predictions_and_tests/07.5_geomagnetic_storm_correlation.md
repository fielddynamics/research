# 07.5 — Geomagnetic Storm Correlation Test

**Reading time:** 3 minutes

## The prediction

Apparatus observables (acoustic mode amplitudes, mode coherence, address-sector activity) should correlate with **geomagnetic K-index** — the standardized measure of geomagnetic disturbance.

$$\boxed{\;\text{Higher K-index} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{larger geomagnetic AC} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{stronger apparatus response in AC-coupled sectors}\;}$$

## K-index background

The K-index measures geomagnetic field variability on a logarithmic scale from 0 (quiet) to 9 (extreme storm), updated every 3 hours by geomagnetic observatories worldwide.

| K-index | Geomagnetic field variation |
|:---:|---|
| 0-1 | quiet, < 5 nT |
| 2-3 | unsettled, 5-20 nT |
| 4 | active, 20-40 nT |
| 5 | minor storm, 40-70 nT |
| 6 | moderate storm, 70-120 nT |
| 7 | strong storm, 120-200 nT |
| 8 | severe storm, 200-330 nT |
| 9 | extreme storm, > 330 nT |

Storm fields are 100-1000× smaller than the DC geomagnetic field (~45000 nT) but provide **AC components** that the apparatus may use for sector excitation.

## Predicted correlations

If the apparatus uses geomagnetic AC for sector pumping:

| Observable | Expected K-index dependence |
|---|---|
| Acoustic mode amplitudes | weak dependence (DC pump dominates) |
| Sector-to-sector coherence | moderate dependence (AC drives cross-sector dynamics) |
| Address-cycle frequency | strong dependence (AC modulates the apparatus's idle cycling) |
| Mode-coupling strength | moderate dependence |

The strongest signature would be **the apparatus's idle / address-search cycle**. If unmatched apparatuses cycle through addresses looking for partners, the cycling rate is set by the available AC pump energy. Higher K → more AC → faster cycling.

## Test design

**Phase 1 — Continuous monitoring (months to a year):**

1. Install permanent acoustic + EM sensors inside the King's Chamber
2. Continuously record apparatus observables at ~1 Hz sampling
3. Correlate with publicly-available K-index data from nearby geomagnetic observatories (Helwan, Egypt is closest)

**Phase 2 — Storm targeting:**

When K ≥ 5 storms are forecast (e.g., from solar wind data), increase sampling rate and resolution during the storm interval. Capture transient apparatus response.

**Phase 3 — Statistical analysis:**

Apply correlation analysis (cross-correlation, partial correlation, etc.) between apparatus observables and K-index. Identify significant correlations beyond chance.

## What success looks like

- Statistically significant correlation (p < 0.01) between K-index and at least one apparatus observable
- Correlations larger during storms than quiet periods
- Time-lag analysis shows apparatus response within minutes of K-index change
- Specific sectors (likely the $\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_7 = \mathbb Z_{21}$ sectors) show pre-determined response patterns

## What failure looks like

- No statistically significant correlation
- Apparatus observables uncorrelated with K-index
- Storm intervals indistinguishable from quiet intervals

## Confounding factors

Several non-framework explanations could produce K-index correlation:

1. **Indirect mechanical effects**: K-index correlates with solar wind, which correlates with cosmic ray flux, which slightly affects ionospheric heating, which causes minor atmospheric pressure changes, which affect chamber acoustics. These would produce **weak, lagged** correlations.

2. **Tourist activity**: visitor numbers depend on weather, which may have weak correlations with space weather. Visitor noise affects measurements.

3. **Instrument response**: sensors may have their own magnetic susceptibilities, producing trivial correlations with K-index that have nothing to do with the apparatus.

These confounders need to be controlled in the experimental design.

## Statistical sensitivity

To detect a 5% modulation of apparatus observables by K-index variations, with 95% confidence:

- ~10⁴ independent K-index data points needed
- At 3-hour cadence, that's 1-3 years of monitoring
- During this time, expect ~20-50 K ≥ 5 storms (depending on solar cycle phase)

This is a **long-baseline** test. A single field campaign won't suffice; the test requires sustained instrumentation over a year or more.

## Cost and difficulty

- **Equipment**: continuous monitoring instrumentation (~$50k initial + ~$10k/year maintenance)
- **Time**: 1-3 years of continuous data
- **Access**: requires permanent instrument installation permission
- **Risk**: minimal (passive monitoring)

This is a **high-time, low-cost** test. The information value is high but the timeline is long.

## Solar cycle timing

The current solar cycle (Cycle 25) is near solar maximum around 2025-2026. **Geomagnetic storm frequency is at its highest in the current decade.** This is the optimal window for the K-index correlation test.

Late-cycle (2028-2030) would have fewer storms and require longer baselines for the same statistical power.

## Falsifiability

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Strong K-index correlation found | Framework confirmed (specifically the AC-coupling claim) |
| Weak correlation found | Framework's AC reading needs refinement |
| No correlation found | Framework's AC pumping claim is wrong; DC pumping alone (which already works in `05.3`) is the operative mode |
| Anti-correlation found | Surprising; would suggest the apparatus suppresses AC, opposite of prediction |

A negative result on K-index correlation would not falsify the broader framework, but would constrain the apparatus's AC-pump role specifically.
