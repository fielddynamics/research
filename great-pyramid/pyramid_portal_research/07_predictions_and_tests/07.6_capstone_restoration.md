# 07.6 — Capstone Restoration Experiment

**Status:** Conjecture
**Reading time:** 3 minutes
**Depends on:** 05.1, 05.2

---

## The prediction

Restoring a properly-designed capstone (Pyramidion) at the apex should **measurably enhance** the apparatus's coherent operation by reinstating the apex field-concentration factor.

$$\boxed{\;\text{Restore sharp gold cap} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{apex concentration } \mathcal G_{\text{apex}} \times 10\text{ at tip} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{KC mode amplitudes increased}\;}$$

## Current state

The Great Pyramid's original capstone has been missing since at least Roman times (possibly earlier). Without the cap, the apex is a slightly rounded, weathered limestone surface lacking the sharp conductive tip the framework predicts as the apparatus's pump concentrator.

**Effect of missing capstone:** the apparatus operates without the sharp-tip lightning-rod field concentration. Bulk concentration (~2.6× from pyramid geometry) is preserved, but the sharp-tip enhancement (~30× local at the tip) is absent.

## Predicted enhancement from restoration

| Capstone configuration | Bulk concentration | Tip concentration | Combined effect on chamber |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original (gold, sharp) | 2.6× | ~30× local | full pump |
| Current (missing) | 2.6× (geometric only) | ~1× | reduced pump |
| Restored replica (gold) | 2.6× | ~30× | full pump |
| Restored (wrong material, e.g., steel) | varies | varies / saturates | mismatched pump |
| Restored (right material, blunt) | 2.6× | ~5× | partial pump |

**The apex tip enhancement is local — it affects field density at the tip itself, not directly at the chamber.** What propagates to the chamber is the bulk concentration ($\mathcal G_{\text{bulk}} \approx 2.6$), already present.

So the capstone restoration's primary effect on chamber-level observables is more subtle: it influences the **coupling of geomagnetic AC components** to the apparatus, since AC has a less uniform spatial distribution than DC and benefits from the sharp tip's local enhancement.

**Predicted observable:** chamber-level acoustic mode amplitudes should increase by ~30% to 3× under restoration, depending on AC component contribution. Mode positions should not change.

## Material constraints

The replica must be:

- **Gold** (or equivalent — silver works similarly but tarnishes; copper oxidizes). Must be conductive, diamagnetic, inert.
- **Sharp at tip** (point ratio > 10:1, ideally 30:1 to match original lightning-rod effect)
- **Properly sized**: ~3 m base, ~3 m height (matching the original Pyramidion proportions, estimated from base-to-apex geometry)
- **Aligned** with the apex centerline to ~1 cm precision

Approximate mass: ~50 tons of gold at the original Pyramidion's estimated size. Cost: ~$3 billion at 2026 gold prices ($60/g).

This is **astronomically expensive** for a single experiment. A scaled-down or alternative version is needed.

## Scaled-down alternative

A **0.3 m gold cap** (1/10 scale) placed at the apex would test the local tip-enhancement without the cost. Expected effect on chamber observables: ~10% (smaller than full restoration but measurable).

Cost: ~$2 million for a few-kg gold pyramidion replica.

Alternative materials for a scaled test (cheaper):

- **Copper** (~$5k for a few kg) — works initially but oxidizes (apparatus observable should slowly degrade over months)
- **Silver-plated copper** (~$10k) — better than copper, less expensive than gold
- **Pure aluminum** (~$1k) — works but is not diamagnetic, may have different effect

A **multi-material test** (sequentially install Al, Cu, Ag, Au caps and measure chamber response) would discriminate the framework's material specification predictions.

## Test protocol

1. **Baseline**: characterize the apparatus's current state (acoustic spectrum, mode amplitudes) over 1 year
2. **Phase 1**: install scaled-down replica cap (copper, silver, gold sequentially) for ~1 month each
3. **Measure**: changes in chamber observables for each material
4. **Phase 2** (if Phase 1 confirms effect): consider full-scale restoration with appropriate authority approval

## Confounding factors

- **Lightning rod effect**: any sharp conductive cap is a lightning rod and would increase strike frequency / risk. This is a real safety concern requiring grounding measures.

- **Tourism impact**: a visible cap would alter tourist experience; political / cultural sensitivities apply.

- **Reversibility**: installation and removal of any cap should not damage the apex's existing surface.

## What success looks like

- Detectable increase (>5%) in chamber acoustic mode amplitudes upon cap installation
- Effect appears within minutes of installation (DC field response is immediate)
- Effect scales with cap conductivity / geometry as predicted
- Effect reverses upon cap removal

## What failure looks like

- No detectable change upon cap installation (within ~5%)
- Effect appears but for the wrong materials (e.g., iron works equally well as gold)
- Effect is anti-correlated with prediction

## Cost and difficulty

| Option | Cost | Time | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-scale gold cap | $3B | years | extreme (political, financial) |
| Scaled gold cap | $2M | 1 year | moderate (technical, regulatory) |
| Multi-material scaled cap | $50k | 1-2 years | moderate |
| Numerical modeling instead | $0 (computation only) | 6 months | low |

A **multi-material scaled test** at $50k is the most practical first step. Numerical modeling can refine predictions before committing to physical tests.
