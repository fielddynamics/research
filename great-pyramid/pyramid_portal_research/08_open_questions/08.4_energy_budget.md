# 08.4 — Portal Energy Budget

**Status:** Open
**Reading time:** 4 minutes
**Depends on:** 03.1, 05.3, 06.2, 06.4

---

## The question

How much energy is required to operate the portal mode of the apparatus, and where does this energy come from?

The cavity-QED calculation in [05.3](../05_cavity_QED_operation/05.3_strong_coupling_calculation.md) shows the chamber operates in the strong-coupling regime when pumped by Earth's geomagnetic field. But:

- Strong coupling describes the **regime of operation**, not the **energy cost of a portal event**
- The geomagnetic pump provides **continuous low-power coupling**, not the burst needed for a portal transition
- A K=4 → K=5 hinge transition involves moving an entire wave-read structure between shells, with associated energy

## Energy scale estimates

### Estimate 1: Per-quantum energy

If the hinge frequency is $\omega_\text{hinge} \sim 2$ MHz (the Larmor estimate from [08.1](08.1_transition_frequency.md)):

$$
E_\text{quantum} = \hbar \omega_\text{hinge} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-27} \text{ J}
$$

This is the energy of a single photon at that frequency. Trivially small.

### Estimate 2: Per-portal-event energy

A portal event involves $\sim N$ coherent quanta, where $N$ is the number of cooperating dipoles in the apparatus. From [05.3](../05_cavity_QED_operation/05.3_strong_coupling_calculation.md), $N \approx 1.6 \times 10^{30}$ in the King's Chamber walls.

Total energy:

$$
E_\text{event} \approx N \hbar \omega_\text{hinge} \approx 2 \times 10^3 \text{ J} \approx 0.5 \text{ Wh}
$$

About half a watt-hour — comparable to a flashlight running for a minute. Easily within the pump's energy budget.

### Estimate 3: Per-mass-equivalent energy

If a portal event transports something with rest mass $m$, the rest energy is:

$$
E_\text{rest} = m c^2
$$

For an electron: $E_\text{rest} \approx 0.5$ MeV $\approx 10^{-13}$ J. Trivial.
For a proton: $\approx 1$ GeV $\approx 1.6 \times 10^{-10}$ J. Trivial.
For a kilogram: $\approx 9 \times 10^{16}$ J $\approx 21$ kilotons TNT. **Not** trivial.

This is the fundamental tension: the cavity-QED-level energy budget supports particle-level transport but **cannot account for macroscopic mass transport** without additional energy sourcing.

## Where the energy comes from

Possible sources:

### Source 1: The geomagnetic field

The geomagnetic dipole stores energy:

$$
U_\text{geomag} \sim \frac{1}{2 \mu_0} \int B^2 \, dV \approx 10^{18} \text{ J}
$$

(integrated over the magnetosphere)

The apparatus draws on this. But the available *power* (rate at which the apparatus can extract from the field) is limited by the apparatus's coupling and the field's recharge timescale.

### Source 2: The Earth-ionosphere capacitor

The Schumann cavity stores $\sim 10^9$ J in low-frequency modes; the global atmospheric electric circuit dissipates $\sim 1$ TW continuously.

If the apparatus taps the atmospheric electric circuit, it has access to TW-level continuous power. **More than enough** for any plausible portal event.

### Source 3: The vacuum (zero-point energy)

If the portal mode reorganizes the local vacuum state, energy can in principle be drawn from (or returned to) the zero-point field. This is the most speculative source.

The Casimir effect demonstrates that geometric boundary conditions modify the zero-point energy of the electromagnetic field. The apparatus's cavity geometry could in principle do something analogous on a larger scale.

**Unknown:** the framework does not yet specify whether the universal vertex operates on the vacuum or only on excitations above it.

### Source 4: Operator-supplied (active pumping)

If the apparatus has an external pump (priest, ritual, machinery, technology), the energy comes from outside the natural environment. This was presumably *not* the design intent of the framework's apparatus (whose pump is the geomagnetic field), but it's a logical option.

## The macroscopic mass problem

The $kg \cdot c^2$ energy required for human-scale teleportation is **enormous**. Two scenarios:

### Scenario A: Mass is not transported, address is

In this picture, the portal does not move mass through space. It modifies the **address** of a state: the K=4 state at location $x$ becomes the K=4 state at location $y$ without physically moving the constituent matter.

Energy cost: only the binding-energy cost of breaking the local mass's coherence with its environment, **not** the full $mc^2$. This is comparable to the binding energy of a solid, $\sim 10$ eV per atom, total $\sim 10^9$ J for a kilogram.

Still large but $10^8$ times smaller than $mc^2$.

### Scenario B: Mass is transported through K=5

In this picture, mass is "lifted" to the K=5 shell, transported through the address space, and "dropped" at the new K=4 location. The lifting energy is $mc^2$.

Energy cost: $mc^2$ per kilogram. Recoverable on the descent, but the apparatus must transiently host the energy. This requires very large capacitive (or analogous) reserves.

The framework currently does not distinguish between these scenarios.

## What goes "through" the portal

A separate question: what type of object can pass through the portal at all?

The K=5 shell has 5-fold symmetry (pentachoron, A_5 icosahedral). Objects must be **commensurable** with this symmetry to pass through coherently. Some plausible cases:

- Information (binary strings, qubits) — passes trivially
- Single particles (electron, photon) — pass via their natural representations
- Atomic states — pass if their internal structure is compatible
- Molecules — pass if their symmetry decomposition into A_5 reps is non-trivial
- Macroscopic objects — require all internal modes to align with A_5; **generically impossible**

This suggests the portal naturally handles **simple, symmetric** objects but not macroscopic chaotic ones. Consistent with the framework's emphasis on coherence preservation.

## What progress looks like

The question is closed when the framework specifies:

1. The energy cost per portal event as a function of what's transported
2. The power source (geomagnetic, atmospheric, vacuum, external)
3. The limits on transportable objects (mass, symmetry, coherence)
4. The reversibility relations (Carnot-like efficiency bounds)

This is essentially the **thermodynamics** of the portal. Without it, the portal is an algebraic object without operational constraints.

## A near-term anchor

Even before resolving the macroscopic question, a near-term study could compute the energy cost for the simplest case: **single-photon teleportation through the apparatus**. This is well-defined in the cavity-QED formalism and would set a baseline scale.

If single-photon transport costs $\sim 10^{-19}$ J (one photon's worth) and macroscopic transport scales linearly with the number of bits transmitted, then the apparatus could handle classical information transmission cheaply but not physical mass transport.

This is a much weaker (and less mythological) interpretation of the portal — closer to a quantum communication channel than a Star-Trek transporter.
