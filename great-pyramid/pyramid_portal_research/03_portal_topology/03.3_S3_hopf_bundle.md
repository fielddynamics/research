## The K=5 shell boundary

The K=5 pentachoron lives in $\mathbb R^5$, with its boundary being a tessellation of the 3-sphere $S^3 \subset \mathbb R^5$ by 5 tetrahedral cells (the boundary of the regular 4-simplex).

**Topological fact:** The boundary of the pentachoron is $S^3$.

Verification by Euler characteristic: pentachoron has 5 vertices, 10 edges, 10 triangular faces, 5 tetrahedral 3-cells. Euler characteristic of the boundary:

$$\chi \;=\; 5 - 10 + 10 - 5 \;=\; 0$$

consistent with $\chi(S^3) = 0$. ✓

## The Hopf fibration

$S^3$ carries a canonical fiber bundle structure: the **Hopf fibration**

$$\boxed{\;S^1 \;\hookrightarrow\; S^3 \;\xrightarrow{\pi_H}\; S^2\;}$$

with $S^1$ as the fiber, $S^2$ as the base. The total space $S^3$ is identified with the group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ of unit quaternions.

**Explicit map:** For $q \in S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$:

$$\pi_H(q) \;=\; q\sigma_3 q^{-1} \;\in\; S^2 \subset \mathbb R^3$$

where $\sigma_3 = \text{diag}(1, -1)$ is the third Pauli matrix and the result is viewed as a point on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^3$ (Bloch sphere).

lifted via the Hopf bundle.

## The quaternionic lift

The natural unified object is a single quaternion $q_U \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$:

$$\boxed{\;q_U \;=\; (\text{shaft-frame lift}) \cdot e^{i\Theta_U / 2} \;\in\; \mathrm{SU}(2)\;}$$

The Hopf projection gives the direction:

$$\pi_H(q_U) \;=\; q_U \sigma_3 q_U^{-1} \;\in\; S^2$$

The fiber phase:

$$e^{i\Theta_U} \;\in\; U(1)$$

The chirality flip is the antipodal map on the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ double cover.

## Mirror address lock in Hopf form

The mirror-match condition $G_U = P^T G_V P$, $\chi_U = -\chi_V$, $\Theta_U - \Theta_V = 2\pi N$ becomes:

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal M_H(U, V) \;=\; 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \pi_H(q_U) = \pi_H(q_V),\quad q_V = \mathcal C(q_U),\quad e^{i(\Theta_U - \Theta_V)} = 1\;}$$

- Share **fiber phase** (phase closure)

## The five sub-tetrahedra as $A_5$ icosahedral structure

The boundary $S^3$ of the pentachoron tessellates into 5 tetrahedral cells. Under Hopf projection $S^3 \to S^2$, these 5 cells push down to 5 spherical regions on the base $S^2$.

The orientation-preserving symmetry of the 5-cell tessellation is $A_5$. The lift to $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the **binary icosahedral group** $2A_5 \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ of order 120.

The 5 sub-tetrahedra are therefore an $A_5$-orbit on $S^2$, with the geometric realization being icosahedral. The choice of "which sub-tetrahedron is active" is the choice of an $A_5$ element acting on $S^2$ — a discrete address from a 60-element discrete group.
