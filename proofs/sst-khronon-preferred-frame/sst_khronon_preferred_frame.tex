\author{Stephen Nelson}

\begin{abstract}
The time-leg proof~\cite{NelsonTL} showed that the SST, varied as
the framework varies it, has the field equations of Blanchet and
Marsat's preferred-foliation theory~\cite{BM2011} with
$f = a_0^2[F(y) - y]$, and left its preferred-frame sector open: the
PPN parameters $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ and the khronon's dynamics and
stability. We settle that sector. (i) The SST has no expansion or
shear terms, so in Bonetti and Barausse's khronometric
parametrisation~\cite{BB2015} $\beta = \lambda = 0$; there their
$\alpha_2$ has a pole and the khronon's sound speed vanishes, and
Flanagan has shown that their expansion does not apply at that
point~\cite{Flanagan2023}. (ii) In Flanagan's slow-motion limit,
which does apply, GFD's $f$ is his $f$ exactly, and the khronon's
mass density is the TetB share's phantom density: the preferred frame
carries the TetB share as a conserved fluid. (iii) His conditions for
stable stationary solutions, $f' \le af'' \le 0$, say that neither
$\mu$ nor the TetB share decreases with the field. For $x/(x+1)$ the
TetB share in units of $a_0$ is $\mu$ itself, so the two conditions
are one, and they hold at every acceleration. Flanagan argued that no
$f$ meeting Blanchet and Marsat's condition for general relativity
($f \to$ constant) can satisfy them everywhere; GFD's $f$ does not
tend to a constant, and that is why it can. (iv) Stability at every
acceleration forces a per-mass TetB tail; in the family
$x/(1+x^n)^{1/n}$ only $n = 1$ is stable everywhere with a bounded
tail. (v) The khronon's inertia and density are proportional to the
TetB share, so in a drainless field, where the net-field rule sets
that share to zero, the preferred-frame sector is empty:
$\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0$ inside a star system. Steady rotating discs
are exact; non-stationary systems carry an order-unity khronon
correction, a prediction.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{SST, khronon, preferred foliation, PPN, $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, stability, net-field rule, MOND, proof}

\section*{Setting}

The SST~\cite{NelsonL3supp} is
$S = \int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,[R/16\pi G - (a_0^2/8\pi G)F(y) - \tfrac14
e^{-2\Phi/c^2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \mathcal L_{\rm m}]$ with
$F(y) = y - 2\sqrt y + 2\ln(1+\sqrt y)$, $y = c^4a_\mu a^\mu/a_0^2$
and $a^\mu$ the acceleration of the tetrad's time leg $u = e_0$,
normal to GFD's time foliation~\cite{NelsonTL}. Varied as the
framework varies it, it is Blanchet and Marsat's theory
$(1/16\pi G)\sqrt{-g}[R - 2f(a)]$~\cite{BM2011} with
\begin{equation}
f(a) = a_0^2[F(y) - y],\qquad f'(a) = 2a(\mu - 1),
\label{eq:f}
\end{equation}
$\mu = x/(x+1)$, $x = a/a_0$ ($c = 1$)~\cite{NelsonTL}. The foliation
is a field, the khronon; its perturbation $\pi$ is the shift of the
preferred time, $T = t + \pi/c^2$~\cite{Flanagan2023}. Write the TetB
share of the field as $(1-\mu)g$.

Each identity below is checked by the accompanying script (24
checks, sympy), and the structural ones are machine-checked in Lean~4
(13 theorems, importing the net-field rule's
formalisation)~\cite{NelsonScript}.

\textbf{Prior work.} The khronometric parametrisation, its PPN
parameters and the strong-coupling analysis are Bonetti and
Barausse's~\cite{BB2015}; the slow-motion limit, the khronon fluid and
the stability conditions are Flanagan's~\cite{Flanagan2023}. What
this note adds is their application to GFD's $f$: the dictionary, the
reading of the conditions in terms of $\mu$ and the TetB share, the
proof that $f = a_0^2(F - y)$ meets them everywhere and why, the
forced tail, the uniqueness of $n = 1$, the khronon frequency, and
the khronon sector under the net-field rule~\cite{NelsonNFR}.

\section*{Theorem 1 (the published PPN formulas do not apply)}

\emph{In Bonetti and Barausse's convention,
$(1/16\pi G)\sqrt{-g}[R - \tfrac13(\beta+3\lambda)\theta^2 -
\beta\sigma^2 + f_{\rm BB}]$, the SST has $f_{\rm BB} = -2f$, whose
deep series $2a^2 - 4a^3/3a_0$ is theirs, and $\beta = \lambda = 0$.
At high acceleration $f_{\rm BB}/a^2 \to 0$: GFD reduces to general
relativity there, not to khronometric theory. At this point their
$\alpha_1 = -4\alpha$ is finite, but
$\lambda\,\alpha_2 \to -\alpha^2/(\alpha - 2) \ne 0$ as
$\lambda \to 0$, so $\alpha_2$ has a pole, and the khronon's sound
speed $c_s^2 \propto \beta + \lambda$ vanishes.}

\begin{proof}
The series, the limit and their Eqs.~14--16 at $\beta = 0$ are
checked exactly (script A4--A6; Lean \texttt{bb\_point}).
\end{proof}

Bonetti and Barausse read the pole as strong coupling. Flanagan showed
that the limit $c \to \infty$ does not commute with
$\beta, \lambda \to 0$: their expansion holds when $\beta c^2$ and
$\lambda c^2$ are large, and at $\beta = \lambda = 0$ his own
expansion, in which the limit is well defined, is the one to
use~\cite{Flanagan2023}. So the answer to ``what are GFD's $\alpha_1$
and $\alpha_2$'' is not read off their formulas; the rest of this
note uses Flanagan's limit.

\section*{Theorem 2 (the khronon is the TetB share)}

\emph{In Flanagan's slow-motion limit~\cite{Flanagan2023}, GFD's $f$
is his $f$ with no rescaling (deep series $-a^2 + 2a^3/3a_0$,
$\Lambda_0 = 0$), and his $\bar\chi = f'/2a = \mu - 1$. Hence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item his static equation $\nabla\cdot[(1 + \bar\chi)\nabla\Phi] =
4\pi G\rho_m$ is GFD's field equation $\nabla\cdot[\mu\nabla\Phi] =
4\pi G\rho_m$;
\item his khronon density at $\pi = 0$,
$\rho_T = -\nabla\cdot[\bar\chi\nabla\Phi]/4\pi G$, is the time-leg
proof's phantom density
$\rho_{\rm ph} = \nabla\cdot[(1 - \mu)\nabla\Phi]/4\pi G$;
\item the khronon obeys $\dot\rho_T + \nabla\cdot(\rho_T v_T) = 0$
with $v_T = -\nabla\pi$: the TetB share is a conserved fluid, carried
irrotationally by the preferred frame.
\end{enumerate}}

\begin{proof}
(1) and (2) are checked exactly (script A1--A3). (3) is Flanagan's
Eqs.~21--24 with (2) substituted.
\end{proof}

In plain terms: the preferred frame is not an extra ingredient
alongside GFD's two shares. It is what carries the TetB share.

\section*{Theorem 3 (stable at every acceleration)}

\emph{Flanagan's conditions for stable stationary solutions, a
non-negative kinetic matrix and (in spherical, cylindrical and planar
symmetry) a non-negative khronon density, are
$f' \le af'' \le 0$~\cite{Flanagan2023}. For any law with
$f' = 2a(\mu - 1)$:
\begin{equation}
-\tfrac12 f'' = \frac{d}{dx}\big[x(1 - \mu)\big],\qquad
\frac{af'' - f'}{2a} = x\mu'(x),
\label{eq:dict}
\end{equation}
so the conditions say: (i) $\mu$ does not decrease with the field, and
(ii) the TetB share $(1 - \mu)g = a_0x(1-\mu)$ does not decrease with
the field. For $\mu = x/(x+1)$, $x(1 - \mu) = \mu$ exactly, so the
two conditions are the single condition $\mu' = 1/(1+x)^2 > 0$, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f' &= -2a_0\mu,\qquad f'' = -\frac{2}{(1+x)^2},\\
af'' - f' &= \frac{2a_0x^2}{(1+x)^2}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:closed}
\end{equation}
give $f' < af'' < 0$ at every $a > 0$.}

\begin{proof}
(\ref{eq:dict}) is the chain rule for $f'(a) = 2a(m(a/a_0) - 1)$ with
any differentiable $m$, and (\ref{eq:closed}) is direct (script
B1--B4; Lean \texttt{stability\_dictionary}, \texttt{share\_eq\_law},
\texttt{law\_deriv}, \texttt{share\_deriv}, \texttt{gfd\_stable}).
\end{proof}

Flanagan notes that the chain cannot hold for all $a$ when $f$ tends
to a constant at high acceleration, Blanchet and Marsat's condition
for recovering general relativity, so a generic $f$ has unstable
stationary solutions near $a \sim a_0$~\cite{Flanagan2023}. GFD's $f$
does not tend to a constant (time-leg proof, Theorem~6), and that is
the property that lets it satisfy the chain everywhere.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

\section*{Theorem 4 (stability forces the tail)}

\emph{(1) If $f'$ is non-increasing on $[a_1, \infty)$ and
$f'(a_1) < 0$, then $f \to -\infty$; in particular $f$ has no finite
limit. So a law stable at every acceleration, with the MOND sign
$f' < 0$ somewhere, cannot tend to a constant, and its TetB share
$-f'/2$ stays at least $-f'(a_1)/2 > 0$: every mass carries a TetB
tail. For GFD the tail is $a_0\mu \to a_0$.
(2) In the family $\mu_n = x/(1+x^n)^{1/n}$ the TetB share rises
monotonically for $n \le 1$ and turns over for $n > 1$. For the
standard law $n = 2$ it peaks at $x = 0.786$ and falls to zero, so
condition (ii) fails for $x > 0.79$. For $n < 1$ it grows without
bound, as $x^{1-n}/n$. Only $n = 1$ is stable at every acceleration
with a bounded tail.}

\begin{proof}
(1) $f + ka$, with $k = -f'(a_1)$, has derivative
$f' - f'(a_1) \le 0$, so $f(a) \le f(a_1) - k(a - a_1)$ (Lean
\texttt{stability\_forces\_tail}, \texttt{no\_constant\_limit},
\texttt{share\_limit}). (2) Exact for $n = 1, 2$ and a scan over
$10^{-4} \le x \le 10^{10}$ for $n = 0.5, 0.8, 1.5, 3$ (script B5--B7;
Lean \texttt{standard\_law\_turns\_over}, the share at $x = 10$
below its value at $x = 1$).
\end{proof}

The net-field rule found that the per-mass tail is forced for every
exchange-symmetric law with flat rotation curves~\cite{NelsonNFR}.
Theorem~4 is a second, independent reason: the khronon's stability.
Together with the involution of the flux exponent~\cite{NelsonNFR},
it is the second property that singles out $n = 1$ in the family.

\section*{Theorem 5 (the khronon around a point mass)}

\emph{The khronon's kinetic matrix (Flanagan's Eq.~32) is
$h_\perp = (1 - \mu)/4\pi G$ across the field and
$h_\parallel = (1 - \mu)^2/4\pi G$ along it. Around a point mass,
$\rho_{T0} = (a_0\mu/4\pi G r)\cdot 2/(2-\mu) > 0$, where
$2/(2 - \mu) = 2 - s$ with $s$ the net-field rule's flux exponent.
A short-wavelength khronon mode across the field oscillates at
\begin{equation}
\omega^2 = \frac{\rho_{T0}}{h_\perp} = \frac{2}{2 - \mu}\,\Omega^2
= -\frac{dg}{dr},
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
$\Omega$ the circular orbital frequency: $\omega = \Omega$ in the deep
field and $\sqrt2\,\Omega$ in the strong field, the radial tidal
frequency of the field it sits in. It does not depend on wavenumber,
so the mode does not propagate.}

\begin{proof}
From $d\ln x/d\ln r = -2/(2 - \mu)$ and $x\mu'/\mu = 1 - \mu$, with
$(1-\mu)g = a_0\mu$ (script C1--C5; Lean \texttt{density\_exponent},
\texttt{oscillation\_frequency}, the latter also giving
$\Omega^2 < \omega^2 < 2\Omega^2$).
\end{proof}

This replaces, for the khronon, the scalar-sector speed
$c\sqrt{2 - \mu}$ that the archive found with $\Phi$ treated as a
covariant scalar~\cite{NelsonTL}: with $\Phi$ the lapse, the scalar
degree of freedom is the khronon, and it oscillates locally rather
than propagating.

\section*{Theorem 6 (the preferred frame in a drainless field)}

\emph{The khronon's inertia ($h_\perp$, $h_\parallel$), its density
$\rho_T$ and its momentum $\rho_Tv_T$ are all proportional to the TetB
share. In a drainless field the net-field rule sets the TetB share to
zero~\cite{NelsonNFR}, so Flanagan's khronon action vanishes there,
his modified Poisson equation becomes Poisson's, and his
gravitomagnetic equation becomes general relativity's,
$\nabla^2\zeta_i = 16\pi G\rho_mv_m^i$. Inside a star system the
preferred-frame sector is empty: $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0$.}

\begin{proof}
$(1-\mu)$ and $(1-\mu)^2$ tend to zero with the TetB share (Lean
\texttt{inertia\_vanishes}); the rest is Flanagan's Eqs.~25, 35 and~42
with $\bar\chi = 0$ (script D1).
\end{proof}

\textbf{Numbers.} Without the rule, with $a$ taken from the Sun's own
time leg, Bonetti and Barausse's $\alpha_1 = -8(1 - \mu)$ would be
$-1.6\times10^{-7}$ at 1~AU, within $|\alpha_1| < 10^{-4}$, but
$\alpha_2$ is undefined (Theorem~1). In Flanagan's limit the Sun would
carry a khronon density $a_0/2\pi Gr$, $2.0\times10^{-13}$~kg\,m$^{-3}$
at Saturn's orbit, about $2\times10^4$ times the ephemeris bound on
unseen mass there~\cite{Pitjev2013}; moving with the Sun it would
drive $\pi$ at order unity, giving preferred-frame accelerations
$\sim a_0$. That is the per-mass tail already excluded by
perihelia~\cite{NelsonNFR}, seen in the khronon sector, and the rule
removes both together. With the rule, the coarse-graining kernel bound
$c(\nu - 1)(r/\ell)^3$ of~\cite{NelsonNFR}, $\ell = r_J$, limits a
planet at 1~AU to a coupling of at most $4\times10^{-17}$ of the
galaxy's TetB share (script D2--D4).

\section*{Proposition 7 (stationary and non-stationary systems)}

\emph{(1) Flanagan's linearised khronon equation has source
$\dot\rho_{T0}$. For a stationary system, including an axisymmetric
disc in steady rotation, $\pi = 0$ solves it, so the static field
equation, and every rotation-curve and decoding-mass result, is
exact. (2) For a non-stationary system (a bar or spiral pattern, an
interaction, accretion) $\pi \sim L^2/T$~\cite{Flanagan2023}, so the
khronon changes the TetB share's field at order unity: the TetB share
is carried as a fluid and lags a moving pattern.}

(2) is a prediction of the SST as written, not an adjustable
correction. Its size in a given system needs the full khronon
equation; this note does not compute it.

\section*{Scope}

Proved here: the dictionary to both published analyses; that the PPN
formulas of~\cite{BB2015} do not apply at GFD's point; the khronon as
the TetB share; stability at every acceleration and its equivalence to
$\mu' > 0$; the forced tail and the uniqueness of $n = 1$; the
khronon frequency; the empty preferred-frame sector in a drainless
field.

Not proved here:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Stability in general geometry.} Flanagan's density
condition is established for spherical, cylindrical and planar
symmetry; the general case is open, and the TetB share's density can
be negative in some configurations~\cite{Flanagan2023}.
\item \emph{The non-stationary correction}, Proposition~7(2), in any
particular system.
\item \emph{The net-field mechanism}, as in~\cite{NelsonTL,NelsonNFR}:
Theorem~6 assumes the rule; the leakage figure rests on its kernel
bound with $\ell = r_J$.
\item \emph{Higher orders and gravitational waves.} Tensor modes are
carried by $R$ and travel at $c$; the khronon's coupling to radiating
binaries beyond the slow-motion limit is not computed.
\item \emph{The fourth term} (electromagnetic coupling), as
in~\cite{NelsonTL}.
\end{enumerate}
