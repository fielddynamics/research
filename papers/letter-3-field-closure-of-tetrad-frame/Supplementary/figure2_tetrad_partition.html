The Tetrad Partition

Two surfaces of revolution over the radial domain of a gravitationally bound system. Height = partition fraction: what share of the total field response each root carries at each radius.

TetA (s = +1) - Blue, Y = mu - retained by GR

TetB (s = -1) - Red, Y = 1-mu - removed by torsion-free condition

MOND Transition (x = 1, mu = 1/2)

The tetrad equation admits two algebraically distinct roots, TetA and TetB, related by spatial parity. Both satisfy the same equation with the same metric; neither can be discarded. The constitutive law mu + (1 - mu) = 1 makes the two surfaces perfectly anti-symmetric: exact mirrors, summing to a constant at every radius. The physical content is their relationship to each other, not their coordinate orientation. Rotate freely.
