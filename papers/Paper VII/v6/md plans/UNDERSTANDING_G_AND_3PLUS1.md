## 1. The Single Polynomial

### 1.1 The G Equation

$$G = \frac{17}{13} \times \alpha^{21} \times \frac{\hbar c}{m_e^2}$$

Three integers appear: 21 (the exponent), 17 (the numerator), 13 (the denominator). The standard decomposition writes them as:

- 21 = 1 + (3+1) + (3+1)^2 = 1 + 4 + 16       (t = +1: full polynomial)
- 17 = 1 +   0   + (3+1)^2 = 1 + 0 + 16       (t =  0: middle term vanishes)
- 13 = 1 - (3+1) + (3+1)^2 = 1 - 4 + 16       (t = -1: middle term reverses)

This looks like three separate counting exercises, each adding its own "+1." But there is only one origin. The three integers must come from one place.

### 1.2 One Polynomial, Three Time States

Define k = (3+1) = 4. Define the structural polynomial:

$$f(k) = 1 + ck + k^2$$

The middle coefficient c is the time coordinate. It takes three values: +1, 0, -1.

| Time State | c | Expression | Value | Role in G |
|------------|---|-----------|-------|-----------|
| t = +1 | +1 | 1 + k + k^2 | 21 | Exponent (vortex in) |
| t = 0 | 0 | 1 + 0 + k^2 | 17 | Numerator (Field Origin) |
| t = -1 | -1 | 1 - k + k^2 | 13 | Denominator (vortex out) |

All three integers come from one polynomial. No separate "+1" origins needed.

### 1.3 The G Equation in Terms of k

$$G = \frac{1 + k^2}{1 - k + k^2} \times \alpha^{1 + k + k^2} \times \frac{\hbar c}{m_e^2}$$

- **17 - 13 = 4 = k** = the tetrad propagation that reversed between the two states. The difference is one tetrad.
