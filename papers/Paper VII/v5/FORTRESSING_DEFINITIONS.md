## Field Object

A unified field configuration that behaves as a single entity due to non-linear self-gravity. Within the **Vainshtein radius** $r_V = (r_g/M_\text{Pl}^2)^{1/3}$, non-linear field interactions dominate over linear propagation, binding what would otherwise be $10^{33}$ independent fields into one coherent structure. Mathematically, this is a **self-gravitating soliton**: a localized, stable field configuration where the field energy serves as its own gravitational source. Examples span all scales—from electron-scale gravity envelopes (Compton wavelength) to asteroids (kilometers) to galaxies (megaparsecs)—each unified by the same topological mechanism.

**Key point:** The Vainshtein mechanism explains why an asteroid with $10^{33}$ atoms doesn't require $10^{33}$ separate field closures. Non-linearity binds them into one.

**Established precedent:**
- Vainshtein mechanism (1972)
- Self-gravitating solitons (1970s-present in field theory)
- Non-linear field binding (standard in GR)

## Field Atom ($A_\Phi$)

The fundamental unit of gravitational structure: a bound gravity field with electromagnetic manifestations at its interaction boundary. This is Wheeler's **geon** (1955)—"mass without mass"—where localized field energy density creates its own gravitational source without requiring external matter. The **valence horizon** marks a topological phase transition: inside this radius, gravity dominates (bound compression states); at the boundary, electromagnetic torsion emerges from gravitational density gradients.

**Key point:** Wheeler established 70 years ago that fields can be self-sourcing. Field atoms are geons realized through dual tetrad topology.

**Established precedent:**
- Wheeler, J.A. (1955). "Geons". Physical Review 97(2): 511–536
- "Mass without mass" - field energy as gravitational source
- 70+ years of geon research in classical GR

## NET Field

The ambient gravitational field environment: the **massless spin-2 mode** in the bimetric sector. This is the background metric $f_{\mu\nu}$ that propagates freely through spacetime, distinct from localized matter configurations (the massive spin-2 mode). In the weak-field limit, the NET field reduces to the **Minkowski metric background**, recovering standard General Relativity. Field objects couple to this background at interaction boundaries through field closure.

**Key point:** Bimetric gravity has two metrics. NET field is the propagating background; field objects are localized excitations.

**Established precedent:**
- Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity (2011-2012)
- Massless vs massive spin-2 modes (standard in bimetric formalism)
- Minkowski limit recovery (required for consistency with GR)

## Closure Boundary ($\Sigma$)

The interface where field object and NET field must satisfy consistency conditions. This boundary enforces the **Israel junction conditions**—the requirement that matter and geometry match smoothly across the interface—and the **Bianchi identities** $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0$, which express energy-momentum conservation. Violation of these conditions produces mathematical inconsistency (divergent stress-energy) and physical nonsense (mass appearing from nowhere).

**Key point:** The closure boundary is not arbitrary. It's where fundamental geometric identities must hold.

**Established precedent:**
- Israel, W. (1966). "Singular hypersurfaces and thin shells in general relativity"
- Bianchi identities (fundamental to differential geometry, 1902)
- Junction conditions (standard in GR since 1960s)

## Field Closure (FC$_\Sigma$)

The process by which field configurations satisfy the **ADM Hamiltonian constraint** and achieve a **ghost-free state** in Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity. Field closure is the physical manifestation of **gauge invariance**: the requirement that field equations remain valid under coordinate transformations. Partial closure violates the Hamiltonian constraint, introducing ghost modes (negative-energy excitations) that destabilize the theory. Field closure ensures the system remains on the constraint surface where all gauge conditions are satisfied.

**Key point:** Field closure isn't optional—it's the requirement that the field theory be mathematically consistent.

**Established precedent:**
- ADM formalism (Arnowitt, Deser, Misner, 1962)
- Hamiltonian constraint (canonical GR since 1960s)
- Ghost-free condition (Hassan-Rosen 2011-2012)
- Gauge invariance (fundamental symmetry principle)

## Field Origin

The **interaction vertex** in the bimetric Hamiltonian where both metric fields couple through the interaction potential $V(\mathcal{S})$. At $r = 0$, the **symmetric vielbein condition** $e^a_\mu(g) = e^a_\mu(f)$ is enforced, requiring both tetrahedra to share the same field origin. This is the point of maximum non-linearity where the **Vainshtein mechanism** dominates, allowing bound states to form. The Field Origin provides the topological link that enables spatially extended configurations to achieve simultaneous field closure.

**Key point:** Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity requires both metrics to couple at a common point. The Field Origin is that point.

**Established precedent:**
- Hassan-Rosen interaction potential V(S) (2011-2012)
- Symmetric vielbein coupling (bimetric formalism)
- Interaction vertex (standard in field theory)
- Vainshtein mechanism for non-linear binding (1972)

## Full State Snapshot (FSS)

The requirement for **global hyperbolicity** and a **well-posed initial value problem** in field theory. As established by Choquet-Bruhat (1952), Einstein's equations admit unique solutions only when initial data is specified on a complete Cauchy surface—a spatial hypersurface that intersects every inextendible timelike curve exactly once. Partial reads (incomplete Cauchy data) fail to determine a unique solution: the evolution becomes ill-posed. Field closure requires FSS because the Hamiltonian constraint must hold everywhere simultaneously on the Cauchy surface.

**Key point:** "Partial reads are prohibited" is not new physics. It's a 1952 theorem about well-posed initial value problems.

**Established precedent:**
- Choquet-Bruhat, Y. (1952). "Théorème d'existence pour certains systèmes d'équations aux dérivées partielles non linéaires"
- Global hyperbolicity (standard in mathematical GR)
- Cauchy problem in GR (fundamental since 1950s)
- Well-posed initial value problem (mathematical requirement)

## Settlement

The dynamical process by which non-linear constraints resolve into a unique geometric configuration. This follows the **York-Lichnerowicz conformal method**: decompose the metric into conformal factor and conformal geometry, then solve the Hamiltonian constraint as an elliptic PDE for the conformal factor. The solution—if it exists and is unique—represents the settled state where all constraints are satisfied. Settlement is not instantaneous propagation; it's the mathematical process of finding the unique solution to a boundary value problem.

**Key point:** Standard numerical relativity uses this method to generate initial data. We're applying it to field closure.

**Established precedent:**
- York, J.W. (1971-1973). Conformal decomposition method
- Lichnerowicz equation (1944) - elliptic PDE for conformal factor
- Standard in numerical relativity for initial data
- Conformal method (textbook technique since 1970s)

## Registration ($B^*_\Sigma$)

The **on-shell configuration** on the constraint surface: the field state where all equations of motion and constraint equations are satisfied. In the Hamiltonian formulation, this is a point in **geometric phase space** $(g_{ij}, \pi^{ij})$ that lies on the constraint surface defined by $\mathcal{H} = 0$ (Hamiltonian constraint) and $\mathcal{H}_i = 0$ (momentum constraints). All physical observables—gravitational lensing, orbital acceleration, gravitational time dilation—are derived from this registered state through standard field theory calculations.

**Key point:** Registration is the field-theoretic state from which we compute measurements. It's the "solution" to the field equations.

**Established precedent:**
- On-shell vs off-shell (standard in field theory)
- Constraint surface (canonical GR since ADM 1962)
- Geometric phase space (Hamiltonian formulation of GR)
- Standard observable calculations from on-shell states
