### Opening (WHY)

Section~\ref{subsec:prefactor} established that at the Field Origin ($s=0$), the coupling polynomial evaluates to $f(k)|_{s=0} = k^2+1 = 17$, counting the coupling capacity, while in the spatial projection ($s=-1$), it evaluates to $f(k)|_{s=-1} = kd+1 = 13$, counting the observable capacity. This algebraic derivation yielded the ratio $(17/13)$ appearing in the gravitational constant $G = (17/13)\alpha^{21}\hbar c/m_e^2$. However, the Poisson equation contains two factors: the prefactor $(17/13)$ and the normalization $4\pi$. Standard field theory derives $4\pi$ from the surface area of a sphere, $A = 4\pi r^2$, by applying Gauss's law to a point source. But if the coupling structure is fundamentally topological rather than geometric, both factors should emerge from the same topological constraints. This subsection demonstrates that the field closure requirement $\det(e) \neq 0$ forces a recursive simplicial structure that produces the entire Poisson equation—normalization and prefactor—as topological consequences of the coupling polynomial.

### Core Derivation (WHAT)

**Topological foundation.** The field closure requirement $\det(e) \neq 0$ must hold at every point $p$ in spacetime. This constraint forces $k = d+1$ linearly independent basis vectors at each point (simplex theorem). For $d=3$ spatial dimensions, every point admits a tetrahedral structure with $k=4$ faces. Spacetime therefore admits a simplicial complex $\mathcal{K}$ where each cell is a 3-simplex (tetrahedron). This is the minimal structure required by field closure in three dimensions.

The simplicial complex $\mathcal{K}$ is not merely scale-invariant; it is \emph{recursively generative}. Define a sequence of scales $\lambda_n = \lambda_0 \cdot \alpha^n$ where $\lambda_0$ is the electron horizon scale ($\sim 10^{-13}$ m) and $n$ ranges from electron to cosmic scales. At each scale $\lambda_n$, spacetime admits a simplicial complex $\mathcal{K}_n$ with tetrahedral cells, but these complexes are not independent: $\mathcal{K}_n$ is generated by the vertices of $\mathcal{K}_{n-1}$. Each vertex at scale $(n-1)$ becomes a tetrahedral cell at scale $n$, creating a nested hierarchy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}_\infty \supset \mathcal{K}_{\text{galaxy}} \supset \mathcal{K}_{\text{star}} \supset \mathcal{K}_{\text{planet}} \supset \mathcal{K}_{\text{atom}} \supset \mathcal{K}_{\text{electron}}
\end{equation}

We live inside $\mathcal{K}_\infty$ (the universal Field Origin), which contains all smaller scales recursively.

**Field normalization from tetrahedral tiling.** At each scale $\lambda_n$, the field normalization is determined by the discrete topology (k=4 tetrahedral faces) and the continuous geometry ($\pi$ from spherical symmetry of the field):
\begin{equation}
\text{Field normalization} = k \times \pi = 4\pi
\end{equation}

This is \emph{not} the surface area $A = 4\pi r^2$ from integrating over a spherical shell, which is the standard geometric derivation. This is $k\pi$ where $k=4$ is the number of simplex faces required by field closure. The factor $4\pi$ is topological (tetrahedral tiling) rather than geometric (spherical integration). Because $k=4$ is a topological invariant (simplex theorem) and $\pi$ encodes spherical symmetry, this normalization is scale-independent: the same $4\pi$ appears at electron scales and galactic scales.

**Coupling ratio from polynomial evaluation.** The coupling polynomial $f(k) = 1 + sk + k^2$ governs the field dynamics at each scale. At the Field Origin ($s=0$), where both tetrahedra meet, the middle term vanishes:
\begin{equation}
f(k)|_{s=0} = 1 + 0 + k^2 = k^2 + 1 = 17
\end{equation}
This counts the coupling capacity: $k^2=16$ bimetric channels (face-to-face couplings between two tetrahedra) plus 1 existence condition ($\det(e) \neq 0$). In the spatial projection ($s=-1$), the polynomial evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
f(k)|_{s=-1} = 1 - k + k^2 = kd + 1 = 13
\end{equation}
using $k^2 - k = kd$ for $k=d+1$. This counts the observable capacity: $kd=12$ spatial channels (3 dimensions $\times$ 4 directions) plus 1 existence condition. The ratio $(k^2+1)/(kd+1) = 17/13$ is the coupling amplification factor.

**Complete Poisson equation.** Combining the field normalization $k\pi$ with the coupling ratio yields:
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Phi = k\pi \cdot \frac{k^2+1}{kd+1} \cdot G_{\text{bare}} \cdot \rho
= 4\pi \cdot \frac{17}{13} \cdot G_{\text{bare}} \cdot \rho
\label{eq:poisson-topological}
\end{equation}

Both the normalization $4\pi$ and the prefactor $17/13$ are topological consequences of the recursive simplicial structure. The $4\pi$ is $k\pi$ (tetrahedral tiling $\times$ spherical symmetry), not $4\pi r^2$ (spherical surface area). The $17/13$ is the polynomial $f(k)$ evaluated at two structural states ($s=0$ and $s=-1$), not a fitted parameter. Because field closure is scale-independent, this Poisson equation applies at all scales—from electrons to galaxies—with the same prefactor.

**Validation through bimetric action.** This topological derivation is independently confirmed by the Hassan-Rosen bimetric formalism. The coupling polynomial determines the beta coefficients $\beta_n = (1, 0, 16, 0, 1)$ (Equation~\ref{eq:beta-from-polynomial}), which determine the interaction potential $V(\mathcal{S}) = e_0 + 16\,e_2 + e_4$ (Equation~\ref{eq:V-determined}). In weak field, variation with respect to the metric yields a trace coupling coefficient:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{trace}} = \beta_0 c_0 + \beta_2 c_2 + \beta_4 c_4 = 1 \times 1 + 16 \times 2 + 1 \times 1 = 34
\end{equation}
where $c_0 = c_4 = 1$ (constant and determinant contributions) and $c_2 = 2$ (derivative factor from pairwise elementary symmetric polynomial). This counts coupling in both directions (TetA $\leftrightarrow$ TetB). For the effective coupling in the observable metric, we divide by 2:
\begin{equation}
G_{\text{eff}} = \frac{C_{\text{trace}}/2}{kd+1} \times G_{\text{bare}} = \frac{17}{13} \times G_{\text{bare}}
\end{equation}

The numerator 17 from trace coupling ($34/2$) is identically the numerator 17 from Field Origin evaluation ($k^2+1$). Both derivations read the same topological structure through different mathematical operations: polynomial evaluation and action variation. The chain is $f(k) \to \beta_n \to V(\mathcal{S}) \to C_{\text{trace}} \to G_{\text{eff}}$.

**Non-uniform objects and field topology.** The field topology is determined by closure, not by mass distribution. An elongated asteroid (physically asymmetric) still produces a field with $k=4$ tetrahedral structure because the field must satisfy $\det(e) \neq 0$ everywhere in its envelope. This closure requirement forces $k=4$ basis vectors at every point, regardless of the object's shape. The analogy is a bar magnet (elongated physical object) producing a dipole field (definite topological structure): the field topology is an emergent property of the governing equations, not a reflection of the source geometry. Similarly, the asteroid's field has $k=4$ topology because closure demands it, not because the asteroid is symmetric.

### Closing (SO WHAT)

The factor $4\pi$ appears in every textbook derivation of the Poisson equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$, always attributed to the surface area of a sphere. This geometric interpretation—integrate the field over a closed surface, apply Gauss's law, and $4\pi r^2$ cancels to yield $4\pi G\rho$—is correct for standard field theory. But it is not the only interpretation, and in dual tetrad gravity it is not the fundamental one.

Here, $4\pi = k\pi$ where $k=4$ is the number of tetrahedral faces required by the simplex theorem and field closure. The field normalization is the product of discrete topology ($k=4$ faces) and continuous geometry ($\pi$ from spherical symmetry), not the integral of a surface area. The prefactor $17/13$ is not phenomenological. It is the ratio of the polynomial $f(k)$ evaluated at the Field Origin ($s=0$, coupling capacity $k^2+1=17$) and the spatial projection ($s=-1$, observable capacity $kd+1=13$). Both factors are topological consequences of recursive simplicial tiling.

The recursive structure explains why the same Poisson equation applies at all scales. An asteroid with $10^{33}$ atoms produces a unified field envelope satisfying $\nabla^2\Phi_{\text{asteroid}} = 4\pi(17/13)G_{\text{bare}}\rho_{\text{asteroid}}$ because field closure forces $k=4$ topology at the asteroid scale. Each constituent atom also satisfies $\nabla^2\Phi_{\text{atom}} = 4\pi(17/13)G_{\text{bare}}\rho_{\text{atom}}$ because field closure forces $k=4$ at the atomic scale. The equation is the same at both scales—and at all scales—because the topological constraint is scale-independent. This is why gravity is universal: not because $G$ is constant (it is not; $G_{\text{eff}} = (17/13)G_{\text{bare}}$), but because the topological structure that determines $G_{\text{eff}}$ is the same everywhere.

The entire Poisson equation—normalization, prefactor, and coupling constant—emerges from evaluating the coupling polynomial $f(k) = 1 + k + k^2$ at the Field Origin within a recursively nested simplicial complex. This is not a fitted model. It is the consequence of requiring that gravitational fields maintain closure ($\det(e) \neq 0$) at every point and every scale. The polynomial proves itself topologically.
