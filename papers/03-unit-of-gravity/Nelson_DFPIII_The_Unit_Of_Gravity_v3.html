Both quantities trace to \(T_{\mu\nu}\). The equivalence principle is not a coincidence requiring explanation; it is an identity. What we call "gravitational mass" and "inertial mass" are two manifestations of the same underlying quantity: the stress-energy that participates in field closure.

Key sentence: The equivalence principle is not a coincidence; it follows from field closure because both masses measure coupling load.

3.2 Lossless Coupling

Derivation from FC. Field closure is a resolution to geometric consistency, not an exchange of substance. Key sentence: Gravitational coupling is lossless because field closure is resolution to consistency, not transfer of substance.

Since field closure resolves based on this total (P1), and the environment's geometry responds to this total, composition cannot affect the gravitational response.

(Technical note: binding energy does contribute to gravitational mass, which is why nuclear reactions release energy. But for objects of the same total mass-energy, the internal source of that mass-energy is irrelevant to coupling.)

Key sentence: Composition independence follows because field closure couples total stress-energy, not internal arrangement.

Key sentence: Motion is history-free because field closure resolves present configurations, not accumulated histories.

7. Gravity as Emergence

7.1 The Geometric Gradient

In general relativity, gravity manifests as spacetime curvature. Near a massive object, clocks run slower and rulers measure differently than far from the object. We characterize this using the local geometry scale \(\ell(x)\), defined in terms of the metric.

In the weak-field, slow-motion limit (appropriate for planetary orbits and galactic dynamics), the metric takes the form \(g_{00} \approx -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2)\), where \(\Phi\) is the Newtonian potential. The local geometry scale is:

\[\ell(x) = \sqrt{-g_{00}} \approx 1 + \frac{\Phi(x)}{c^2}\]

This encodes how local clocks run compared to clocks at infinity: where \(\ell < 1\), clocks run slower (gravitational time dilation).

When matter couples to its gravitational environment through field closure, the resolved geometry varies spatially. Near the matter, \(\ell(x)\) is smaller; far from the matter, \(\ell(x)\) approaches 1. This spatial variation creates a gradient:

\[\vec{g} = -c^2 \nabla\ell(x) \approx -\nabla\Phi(x)\]

This is the Newtonian gravitational acceleration in the appropriate limit. What we call "gravity" is the spatial gradient in local geometry that field closure creates.
